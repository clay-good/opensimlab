# Evidence brief: Intracranial hemorrhage deterioration

## Intended rehearsal

This advanced adult emergency-department case rehearses recognizing serial neurologic decline,
reassessing immediate airway and physiologic threats, activating an intracranial-hemorrhage pathway,
reviewing authored CT and warfarin findings, recording urgent agent-specific reversal intent,
recording a smooth pressure strategy, and escalating intraventricular extension with hydrocephalus
to neurocritical and neurosurgical capability.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2022 AHA/ASA guideline emphasizes trained serial neurologic assessment because the first 12
  hours carry the highest risk of decline. This screen does not perform or score an examination.
- Anticoagulant-associated spontaneous ICH requires immediate anticoagulant discontinuation and
  rapid reversal. For VKA-associated ICH with INR at least 2.0, 4-factor PCC is preferred to plasma,
  followed directly by IV vitamin K. This case records that bundle without selecting a dose.
- For mild-to-moderate ICH with presenting SBP from 150–220 mmHg, targeting 140 mmHg and maintaining
  130–150 mmHg may be reasonable; lowering below 130 mmHg is potentially harmful. This fixed
  202/112 mmHg presentation rehearses that boundary, not a universal rule.
- Intraventricular extension and clinical hydrocephalus warrant neuro-specialized care and may
  require ventricular drainage. Transfer should not wait for a screen response, though necessary
  airway and hemodynamic stabilization precede transport.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not perform an examination or score, airway assessment or procedure, measurement,
imaging or laboratory acquisition or interpretation, reversal product or dose selection, medication
delivery, infusion titration, serial imaging, ventricular drainage, surgery, expansion or
cerebral-perfusion modeling, complication recognition, goals-of-care communication, team
performance, disposition, prognosis, or outcome.
