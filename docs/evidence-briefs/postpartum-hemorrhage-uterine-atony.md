# Evidence brief: postpartum hemorrhage from uterine atony

## Rehearsal value

This lab practices early recognition of postpartum hemorrhage, a uterine-atony pattern without
single-cause closure, immediate multidisciplinary ownership, review of the supplied competing-cause
evidence, a bounded qualified-care intent, strict reassessment, and active-risk handoff. It is not a
blood-loss calculator, medication trainer, fetal-monitor lesson, procedure trainer, or delivery
simulator.

## Source floor

1. World Health Organization, International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics, and
   International Confederation of Midwives. *Consolidated guidelines for the prevention, diagnosis
   and treatment of postpartum haemorrhage*. 2025. The WHO release and guideline diagnostic section
   support objective measurement, action at 300 mL plus an abnormal vital sign, the established
   500 mL trigger, immediate MOTIVE-bundle care, and escalation when bleeding persists.
2. American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists. *Practice Bulletin No. 183: Postpartum
   Hemorrhage*. 2017, reaffirmed 2024. The abstract supports cumulative blood loss of at least
   1,000 mL or bleeding with hypovolemia within 24 hours as the US definition and identifies atony as
   the principal driver of the increase in postpartum hemorrhage.

Consulted on August 26, 2026. The WHO guidance is the current global source floor. Local medication,
massive-hemorrhage, blood-bank, escalation, and procedural policy remains controlling.

## Authored defaults

- The fictional patient is 31 years old, 39 weeks pregnant before an uncomplicated vaginal birth,
  and 8 minutes postpartum when the lab opens.
- Qualified staff supply a cumulative measured blood loss of 650 mL and rising, active vaginal
  bleeding, a boggy enlarged uterus, HR 118/min, BP 94/58 mmHg (MAP 70), RR 24/min, room-air SpO2
  98%, temperature 36.7°C, pallor, dizziness, and preserved conversation.
- The placenta is supplied as delivered and grossly complete. No genital-tract examination,
  retained-tissue exclusion, coagulation result, concealed bleeding exclusion, or definitive cause
  is supplied at the opening state.
- A strict 10-minute qualified-team report after bounded intent supplies HR 104/min, BP 102/64 mmHg
  (MAP 77), RR 20/min, SpO2 99% on supplied support, a firmer uterus, and visibly slower bleeding.
  Cumulative loss, hemoglobin trajectory, durable control, treatment effect, transfusion need,
  procedure need, disposition, recovery, and maternal or newborn outcome remain open.
- There is no randomization. All findings and timing are authored and deterministic.

## Hazard and scope boundaries

- Do not wait for 1,000 mL when abnormal physiology and objectively measured bleeding already demand
  action; do not teach one threshold as universal across regions.
- Do not diagnose atony from bleeding alone or let a boggy uterus close trauma, tissue, thrombin,
  rupture, inversion, or concealed-bleeding work.
- Do not expose oxytocin, tranexamic acid, another uterotonic, fluid, blood-component, dose, route,
  rate, contraindication, massage technique, examination, tamponade, surgery, or hysterectomy as a
  learner control.
- Do not claim that a firmer uterus or improving vital signs proves treatment effect, durable
  hemostasis, normal coagulation, safe discharge, preserved fertility, or maternal/newborn outcome.
- Preserve respectful communication, pain, privacy, newborn/feeding support, blood-bank readiness,
  escalation, and locally governed protocol ownership in the handoff.

## Verification contract

The expert path is six ordered actions with elapsed gates before reassessment and handoff. Tests must
cover no-action, out-of-order, same-tick, hostile text, generic hemorrhage/drug/fluid/blood shortcuts,
adjacent trauma-hemorrhage isolation, deterministic state, exact authored claims, route/SEO/report
coverage, and the absence of learner examination, calculation, diagnosis, medication, procedure,
treatment-effect, disposition, or outcome claims.
