# Evidence brief: Acute aortic syndrome before definitive imaging

## Intended rehearsal

This advanced adult emergency case rehearses resisting premature closure in abrupt chest and back
pain, repeating bilateral pressure, pulse, limb-perfusion, and neurologic assessment, recognizing
evolving multi-territory discordance, escalating a suspected acute aortic syndrome, and protecting
end-organ perfusion while urgent definitive imaging remains unavailable.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2022 ACC/AHA aortic-disease guideline describes abrupt severe chest or back pain maximal at
  onset, pulse or blood-pressure differential, focal neurologic deficit, and limb ischemia as
  important acute-aortic-syndrome features. Bilateral arm and lower-extremity pressures should be
  assessed; serial findings matter because branch-vessel malperfusion can change the presentation.
- CT is recommended as initial diagnostic imaging for suspected acute aortic syndrome because it is
  fast, accurate, widely available, and defines anatomic extent; TEE and MRI are reasonable
  alternatives in appropriate contexts.
- Initial treatment uses prompt monitored anti-impulse therapy, ordinarily intravenous rate control
  before added vasodilation, analgesia, and an arterial line in an ICU setting. The guideline target
  is HR 60–80/min and SBP below 120 mmHg or the lowest pressure that preserves end-organ perfusion.
- Suspected acute type A disease warrants urgent surgical evaluation, but this lesson ends before
  definitive imaging, classification, operative selection, or transfer decisions.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine a real patient, calculate a risk score, diagnose dissection or another
acute aortic syndrome, place an arterial line, choose or deliver a drug or dose, determine
contraindications, acquire or interpret CT, TEE, or MRI, select an operation, perform a procedure,
coordinate real transfer, or assess diagnostic, procedural, disposition, prognosis, or outcome
competence. The evolving findings and treatment response are fixed authored teaching panels.
