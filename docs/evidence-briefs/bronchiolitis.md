# Evidence brief: bronchiolitis

## Learning contract

This lab rehearses whole-infant severity review, supportive-care prioritization, feeding and
hydration reassessment, contextual treatment restraint, and handoff in one authored bronchiolitis
trajectory. It is not a diagnostic, oxygen, fluid, suction, medication, or disposition algorithm.

## Evidence anchors

- WHO's 2026 consolidated guideline treats bronchiolitis as a clinical pattern and integrates
  severity, risk factors, feeding, hypoxemia, and supportive management. Oxygen and escalation sit
  inside whole-child assessment rather than making one saturation a complete decision.
- NICE NG9 integrates apnea, persistent oxygenation, oral intake, dehydration, risk factors, and
  respiratory distress. It advises against routine imaging, blood tests, bronchodilators, steroids,
  antibiotics without suspected bacterial infection, hypertonic saline, chest physiotherapy, and
  routine suction, while preserving secretion-related and alternative-diagnosis contexts.
- The 2014 AAP guideline is historical North American context for clinical diagnosis, risk review,
  nonroutine imaging and medicines, and supported hydration. WHO 2026 and current NICE guidance
  take precedence where recommendations or currency differ.

## Authored fixture

The child, illness day, findings, intake, urine report, support, and one-hour response are fixed.
The healthy-child engine profile is source-composed scaffolding, not a validated bronchiolitis model.
The later improvement is partial and does not prove oral readiness, room-air stability, discharge
readiness, resolution, prognosis, or outcome.
