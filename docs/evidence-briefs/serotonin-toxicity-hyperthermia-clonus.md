# Serotonin toxicity with hyperthermia and clonus

## Teaching claim

This lab teaches recognition and escalation of a declared serotonergic drug interaction with mental-
state, autonomic, and neuromuscular findings. It does not turn the Hunter decision rules into a
standalone diagnosis, infer an unknown exposure, prescribe cooling or sedation, or make a serotonin
antagonist automatic.

## Authoritative basis

- Dunkley et al, *The Hunter Serotonin Toxicity Criteria*, QJM 2003;96(9):635-642, PMID 12925718.
  In a serotonergic-exposure cohort, clonus, agitation, diaphoresis, tremor, hyperreflexia,
  hypertonicity and temperature were the discriminating findings. The study's life-threatening cases
  were not in its initial learning set, so this fixture does not claim universal diagnostic accuracy.
- DailyMed, current US linezolid prescribing information, Warnings and Precautions 5.3 (updated
  2026-07-09). The label identifies serotonin-syndrome risk with serotonergic agents and supports
  clinically appropriate discontinuation of implicated agents plus supportive treatment.
- American Heart Association, *2025 Guidelines for CPR and ECC, Part 10*, Section 16.2. Medication-
  or drug-associated core temperature above 40°C is life-threatening hyperthermia; active cooling
  begins with standard resuscitation. Consulted 2026-08-26.

## Fixed fixture

A 37-year-old woman develops rapidly progressive symptoms 6 hours after a first supplied linezolid
dose while continuing stable sertraline. Qualified findings are agitation and confusion, diaphoresis,
ocular and inducible ankle clonus, lower-limb hyperreflexia, tremor, increased leg tone, hyperactive
bowel sounds and diarrhea. HR is 128/min, BP 146/84 mmHg (MAP 105), RR 26/min, room-air SpO2 97%,
and core temperature 40.1°C. A supplied ECG reports sinus tachycardia with QRS 88 ms. Supplied values
are pH 7.34, PCO2 35 mmHg, bicarbonate 19 mmol/L, lactate 3.8 mmol/L, sodium 140 mmol/L, potassium
3.9 mmol/L, glucose 116 mg/dL, creatinine 1.0 mg/dL, and CK 640 U/L.

After elapsed time, a strict qualified-team report supplies implicated-agent cessation, active cooling,
supportive care and qualified sedation, with T 38.7°C, HR 104/min, BP 132/76 mmHg (MAP 95), calmer
mentation, and persistent inducible clonus and hyperreflexia. These authored changes do not prove
individualized treatment effect, exposure completeness, durable temperature control, renal or muscle
safety, seizure exclusion, safe disposition, or outcome.

## Deliberate boundaries

- The supplied interaction and Hunter-pattern findings support this authored fixture; no one sign,
  rule, score, or medication list independently diagnoses a real patient or excludes infection,
  anticholinergic or sympathomimetic poisoning, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, malignant
  hyperthermia, withdrawal, endocrine, neurologic, or mixed causes.
- The learner records qualified source cessation, rapid cooling and support, sedation or seizure care
  if needed, serial temperature, renal and CK surveillance, airway preparedness, and specialist-led
  serotonin-antagonist rescue intent only. No cooling method, sedative, cyproheptadine product, dose,
  route, access, airway device, neuromuscular blocker, or delivery is exposed.
- Improvement in temperature or agitation does not prove neuromuscular recovery or durable safety.

## Review need

Clinical reviewers should verify the interaction and fixture values, the Hunter-criteria limitations,
the hyperthermia and airway boundaries, specialist rescue language, and distinction from the adjacent
anticholinergic, sympathomimetic, cholinergic, tricyclic and malignant-hyperthermia labs.
