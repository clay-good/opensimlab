# Evidence brief: right-ventricular infarction

## Intended rehearsal

This advanced Cardiology lesson isolates acute inferior STEMI with authored right-ventricular
involvement in a PCI-capable cardiac unit. The learner reconciles the whole-patient trajectory,
reviews fixed right-sided ECG and echo reports, keeps reperfusion and rhythm-conduction readiness
active, records individualized hemodynamic guardrails, and hands off unresolved work after elapsed
reassessment.

The outpatient Cardiology STEMI lab owns EMS and regional-system transfer from a non-PCI clinic.
The Emergency Medicine STEMI lab owns general stable anterior-STEMI reperfusion preparation. The
post-infarction shock lab begins after PCI with persistent multi-organ hypoperfusion. This lesson
owns the complication-specific, pre-reperfusion RV phenotype and its support hazards without
performing treatment.

## Evidence boundary

- Contemporary ACS guidance keeps STEMI reperfusion time-sensitive. RV-focused assessment must not
  delay the active reperfusion pathway.
- The AHA right-sided-heart-failure statement treats acute RV support as physiology and context
  dependent. Preload, systemic pressure, RV perfusion, rhythm, oxygenation, congestion, and serial
  response belong together; indiscriminate volume loading is not a universal solution.
- In this authored hypotensive, preload-sensitive case, nitrate and reflex diuresis are not selected.
  That case-specific restraint is not presented as a universal prohibition for every patient with
  RV infarction. No fixed fluid volume or pressure target is supplied.

## Authored vignette

A 66-year-old man in a PCI-capable cardiac unit has 55 minutes of ongoing chest pressure and an
already activated primary-PCI pathway. Fixed ECG reports show inferior STEMI and 1.5 mm ST elevation
in V4R. HR is 54/min in sinus rhythm, BP 86/60 mmHg, RR 18/min, SpO₂ 96% on room air, and he is alert
with warm perfusion and capillary refill 2 seconds. JVP is reported elevated and lungs clear. Fixed
echo reports moderate RV dilation and reduced RV systolic function, a small underfilled LV with
preserved LV systolic function, no pericardial effusion, and no reported acute severe valve lesion
or ventricular-septal defect. Pulmonary embolism, evolving mechanical disease, rhythm or conduction
disease, bleeding, medication effects, and other contributors remain open.

The fixed later report remains similar: ongoing pain, HR 52/min, BP 88/62 mmHg, normal room-air
oxygenation, alert warm perfusion, elevated JVP, and clear lungs. No learner-delivered treatment or
completed reperfusion explains that authored snapshot, and no durable resolution or outcome follows.

## Exclusions

The browser does not examine; acquire or interpret ECG, echo, monitoring, laboratory, catheter, or
other test data; diagnose a real patient; select or deliver fluid, nitrate, diuretic, vasopressor,
inotrope, antithrombotic, oxygen, or another treatment; perform PCI, pacing, or another procedure;
select a device; determine disposition or prognosis; or predict complications or outcome.
