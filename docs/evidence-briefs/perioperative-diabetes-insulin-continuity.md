## Authored patient and contrasts

A fictional 42-year-old woman, 68 kg and 165 cm, has established type 1 diabetes. Her pump
stopped delivering insulin 90 minutes before presentation, without long-acting backup. Elective
laparoscopic cholecystectomy is delayed beyond one missed meal. She is awake and thirsty.
Supplied glucose is 180 mg/dL and blood beta-hydroxybutyrate is 0.6 mmol/L. Venous pH 7.38,
bicarbonate 24 mmol/L, potassium 4.2 mmol/L, and creatinine 0.8 mg/dL are historical context,
not evolving variables or proof that later acidosis is absent.

Initial BP is 118/72 mmHg (MAP 87), HR 88/min, RR 16/min, SpO2 98%, temperature 36.7°C.
Without restored coverage, private glucose/ketones become 240/1.2 after 30 minutes and
280/2.0 after 60 minutes; HR/RR become 96/18 and 104/20. Other vital signs remain fixed.
These clocks and values are fictional contrasts, not estimated clinical kinetics or safe waits.

Qualified restored insulin delivery independently starts the authored response. With prompt
care, glucose/ketones become 162/0.4 at 30 minutes and 144/0.3 at 60 minutes. After the authored
untreated deterioration, corresponding values are 198/1.0 and 162/0.4, with HR/RR 92/18 then
88/16. Prompt treatment does not worsen the baseline vital signs. Earlier delay and observed
deterioration stay in the learning record; the clock does not determine an objective grade.

Restoring coverage means qualified verified alternative delivery, not merely restarting an
unreliable device. It has no new-laboratory, support, or planning prerequisite. Fasting planning
coordinates individual insulin, substrate, fluids, electrolytes, procedural changes, and later
intake; it does not itself lower glucose or ketones or require one universal glucose infusion.

Glucose-only checks are valid partial observations. They update only their own value and
timestamp. Full assessments update glucose, ketones, and bedside findings together. Neither
accepted treatment nor elapsed clocks generate fresh laboratory results. The eight-decision
example pauses before each action and uses the ordinary session. Omission of all insulin,
CGM-only surveillance, and automatic surgical clearance are refused but retained for reflection.

Handoff requires coordinated ongoing care and a fresh later full assessment, not an error-free
history or completion of an earlier teaching checkpoint. Instructor takeover at 120 minutes
without insulin or 240 minutes unfinished bounds practice without predicting harm. No operation,
acidosis response, insulin dose, device mechanics, discharge, or competence certification exists.
