# Pediatric foreign-body airway obstruction evidence brief

## Decision boundary

This lab rehearses one narrow pediatric choking trajectory: an abrupt eating event with an effective
cough, a strictly later severe responsive obstruction, and then a strictly later unresponsive
transition. The learner preserves effective coughing rather than applying maneuvers early, recognizes
when the cough becomes ineffective, activates qualified responsive-child care, then transitions to
qualified unresponsive-child CPR and airway-check ownership without performing psychomotor care.

The scenario is distinct from croup's infectious upper-airway pattern, generic pediatric respiratory
distress, pediatric bradycardia after effective ventilation, and adult post-extubation obstruction.
It does not teach procedural removal or reproduce one regional CPR sequence.

## Fixed teaching facts

- A previously well 6-year-old boy is 20 kg and 115 cm. While eating a whole grape at school, he
  suddenly coughs and signals that something is stuck. No object is seen to exit; object and location
  remain unconfirmed.
- Initially he is awake, frightened, and follows directions. His cough is forceful and loud, airflow
  is audible, and he can say “Something is stuck” in a normal voice between coughs. Temperature is
  36.7°C, HR 118/min, RR 24/min between coughs, BP 100/64 mmHg (MAP 76), room-air SpO₂ 98%, color is
  normal, pulses are warm and strong, and refill is 2 seconds.
- No fever or respiratory prodrome, bark, hoarseness, stridor before the event, drooling, dysphagia,
  urticaria, swelling, vomiting, wheeze, neurological change, trauma, or known nonfood ingestion is authored.
  These snapshots do not exclude retained airway material, aspiration, evolving obstruction, or
  another dangerous cause.
- At strict-later minute 2 he remains responsive and tracks his caregiver but cannot speak or make an
  audible cough. Silent ineffective cough attempts, minimal air movement, and perioral cyanosis are
  supplied. HR is 138/min, BP 98/60 mmHg (MAP 73), room-air SpO₂ is 91% and falling on a coherent
  signal, RR is not reliably countable, and a central pulse remains present. No object is expelled or
  visibly accessible.
- At strict-later minute 3 the obstruction is not reported relieved. He is unresponsive without
  purposeful movement, normal breathing, effective cough, speech, or audible airflow. Pulse status is
  deliberately not supplied. A supplied ECG display reports electrical activity at 132/min while BP
  and SpO₂ are unavailable. No visible object or successful removal is reported; qualified
  unresponsive-child CPR and airway-check care is active.
- Handoff preserves unresolved obstruction and active resuscitation ownership. No removal, recovery,
  ROSC, disposition, prognosis, or outcome is reported.

All values, negative findings, timing, and transitions are fictional face-valid fixtures. The sources
support the qualitative branches, not these exact values or a predicted trajectory.

## Source synthesis

The 2025 AHA/AAP Pediatric Basic Life Support guideline distinguishes mild obstruction with an
effective cough from severe obstruction with an ineffective cough and directs prompt transition to
the unresponsive pathway when responsiveness is lost. The companion child algorithm depicts these
branches compactly. The simulator supplies the findings and records pathway ownership rather than
learner cough assessment, maneuvers, object checks, ventilation, or CPR mechanics.

The 2025 European Resuscitation Council pediatric life-support guideline and Resuscitation Council UK
resource likewise emphasize encouraging an effective cough, avoiding blind finger sweeps, treating an
ineffective cough as severe obstruction, and starting pediatric CPR when the child becomes
unresponsive. AHA and European/UK materials differ in the detailed opening sequence for unresponsive
FBAO care. This lab therefore exposes only qualified pathway activation, not a universal compression-
versus-breath ordering rule.

## Explicit nonclaims

- The learner does not examine the child or assess cough effectiveness, airflow, breathing, pulse,
  oxygenation, perfusion, responsiveness, an object, or treatment response.
- No back blow, chest or abdominal thrust, blind or visible-object sweep, suction, ventilation,
  compression, CPR sequence, oxygen, airway device, laryngoscopy, bronchoscopy, drug, device,
  procedure, or treatment is selected or performed.
- The grape history does not prove the object, location, retained obstruction, aspiration, or cause.
- Fixed negative snapshots do not exclude another dangerous cause.
- The transition is not modeled physiology, a treatment effect, or an individual prediction.
- Pulse status after unresponsiveness, removal, recovery, ROSC, disposition, prognosis, and outcome
  remain unreported.
