# Evidence brief: concealed placental-abruption hemorrhage

## Rehearsal value

This lab practices recognizing maternal and fetal compromise despite little visible blood,
preserving competing antepartum causes, activating simultaneous hemorrhage/resuscitation/delivery
ownership, reviewing early coagulation risk, and handing off unresolved maternal-fetal risk. It is
not a blood-loss calculator, fetal-monitor interpretation course, diagnostic ultrasound lesson,
transfusion trainer, anesthesia planner, or delivery simulator.

## Source floor

1. Smith CD, Lee A. *Placental abruption*. BJA Education. 2024;24(9):305-308. PMID 39234154.
   This supports revealed versus concealed presentation, poor correlation between vaginal bleeding
   and severity, underestimation during concealed abruption, primarily clinical diagnosis, early
   coagulopathy risk, and rapid maternal-fetal intervention.
2. Royal College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists. *Antepartum Haemorrhage*. Green-top Guideline
   No. 63. 2011, current while the second edition is in development. This supports judging shock
   rather than visible volume, fetal compromise as an important severity signal, simultaneous
   maternal resuscitation and urgent delivery when either patient is compromised, and the boundary
   that ultrasound does not exclude abruption.

Consulted on August 26, 2026. Local obstetric hemorrhage, fetal-surveillance, blood-bank,
anesthesia, delivery, neonatal, and massive-transfusion policy remains controlling.

## Authored defaults

- A fictional 34-year-old at 36 weeks 4 days has 35 minutes of abrupt constant abdominal and back
  pain, only 80 mL externally collected dark blood, a supplied tense tender uterus, pallor,
  restlessness, HR 126/min, BP 92/56 mmHg (MAP 68), RR 26/min, room-air SpO2 98%, and T 36.8°C.
- Qualified staff supply fetal tachycardia 170/min, minimal variability and recurrent late
  decelerations; hemoglobin 9.8 g/dL, platelets 112 × 10^9/L, fibrinogen 1.5 g/L, INR 1.4 and lactate
  4.0 mmol/L. No learner interpretation creates these findings.
- A strict 10-minute qualified-team report after bounded intent supplies HR 118/min, BP 98/60 mmHg
  (MAP 73), RR 22/min, SpO2 99% on supplied support, persistent fetal compromise, 120 mL visible
  cumulative blood, operating-room readiness, and unresolved total loss, coagulation, and delivery.
- All findings and timing are authored and deterministic.

## Hazard and scope boundaries

- Do not equate visible blood with total loss or allow modest pressure improvement to close concealed
  hemorrhage, coagulopathy, fetal compromise, rupture, previa, vasa previa, labor, or non-obstetric causes.
- Do not expose examination, fetal-trace interpretation, ultrasound, blood-loss calculation, fluid,
  blood, drug, dose, route, access, anesthesia, incision, delivery, or procedure controls.
- Do not claim diagnosis, treatment effect, coagulation correction, fetal recovery, completed
  delivery, safe disposition, fertility, or maternal/newborn outcome.
- Preserve pain, privacy, consent and communication, support person, blood-bank, operating-room,
  anesthesia, neonatal, pathology, recurrence, and bereavement/support uncertainty.

## Verification contract

The expert path is six ordered actions with elapsed gates before reassessment and handoff. Tests cover
order, time, hostile text, generic hemorrhage/fluid/blood/delivery shortcuts, atony and trauma
isolation, deterministic state, exact authored claims, route/SEO/report coverage, and all non-claims.
