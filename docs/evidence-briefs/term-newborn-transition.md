# Term newborn transition

Status: source-checked authoring brief; clinical review remains unsigned.
Checked: August 26, 2026.
Scope: one bounded term-newborn transition rehearsal, not a newborn assessment, resuscitation course, feeding plan, or discharge pathway.

## Defensible teaching boundary

The 2025 AHA/AAP neonatal-resuscitation guideline says every birth should have a trained and equipped person who can facilitate newborn transition. Most newborns do not need resuscitation. When a term newborn is breathing or crying, has good tone, and has an adequate heart rate, the qualified team can continue evaluation during deferred cord clamping for at least 60 seconds in most instances, then protect skin-to-skin contact and temperature while observing respiratory transition. The guideline gives an ideal newborn temperature range of 36.5°C to 37.5°C.

The 2025 ILCOR consensus reinforces that stable breathing or crying, good tone, and adequate heart rate support deferred cord clamping and immediate skin-to-skin thermal care, but ongoing observation still matters because expected low oxygen saturation in the first minutes may persist or recur and apnea or breathing difficulty can emerge. The lesson therefore avoids interpreting color or a saturation number alone and avoids declaring the newborn durably well from one early snapshot.

The authored case uses a 39-week-4-day, 3.4 kg newborn with spontaneous breathing and crying, good tone, heart rate 142/min, and temperature 36.8°C. A fixed 1-hour qualified report supplies regular breathing without apnea or increased work, heart rate 136/min, axillary temperature 36.7°C, protected skin-to-skin positioning, and feeding cues. These values are plausible teaching fixtures inside the cited ranges; they are not universal targets or learner measurements.

The shared engine schema has adult-oriented minimums for hidden stroke-volume and blood-volume scaffold fields and caps its set ventilator rate at 40/min. This cognitive lab therefore bounds those non-learner scaffold inputs to the schema while the declared newborn observations remain authored in the scenario and engine snapshot. It must not be used as validated neonatal hemodynamic or respiratory physiology.

## Controls and exclusions

Six ordered controls confirm prepared support, connect the supplied whole-dyad record, recognize stable transition without closure, review qualified protective care, read the fixed report after elapsed simulated time, and hand off active risk. No control examines or scores the newborn; measures or interprets monitoring or tests; clamps the cord; positions, dries, warms, suctions, stimulates, separates, feeds, treats, or resuscitates; determines disposition; or predicts outcome.
