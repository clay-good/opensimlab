# Neonatal apnea

Status: source-checked authoring brief; clinical review remains unsigned.
Checked: August 26, 2026.
Scope: one bounded neonatal-apnea threshold and qualified-response rehearsal, not ventilation instruction or a complete neonatal-resuscitation pathway.

## Defensible teaching boundary

The 2025 AHA/AAP neonatal-resuscitation guideline says a newborn who does not breathe within the first 60 seconds after birth or has a heart rate below 100/min despite initial steps should receive assisted ventilation. Effective lung ventilation is the priority, and an increasing heart rate is the primary indicator that ventilation is effective. Corrective steps and an alternative airway may be required when heart rate does not rise with face-mask ventilation; that nonresponse is reserved for the next lab.

The authored case supplies apnea after qualified warmth, positioning, drying, stimulation, and airway assessment, with heart rate 92/min at 40 seconds after birth. Qualified staff begin assisted ventilation before 60 seconds. The fixed 90-second report supplies visible chest movement, heart rate 126/min, and spontaneous irregular respirations emerging while qualified ventilation and direct assessment continue. These are plausible fixtures used to show an early response, not universal targets, learner measurements, stable transition, or outcome.

The shared engine schema has adult-oriented minimums for hidden stroke-volume and blood-volume scaffold fields and caps its set ventilator rate at 40/min. Learner-visible apnea, heart rate, respiratory rate, oxygenation, and temperature are authored snapshots. The lab is not validated neonatal physiology.
