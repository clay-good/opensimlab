# Hemorrhagic shock evidence brief

## What this slice teaches

One fixed adult emergency-department vignette asks the learner to integrate blunt mechanism,
suspected unstable pelvic injury, physiology, and impaired perfusion despite no visible external
bleeding. Pelvic stabilization and definitive bleeding-control escalation proceed immediately and
alongside major-hemorrhage activation, a bounded 2-unit red-cell bridge, coagulation and temperature
review, and serial reassessment.

## Current source floor

The 2023 European trauma bleeding guideline recommends minimizing time to bleeding control and
integrating physiology, anatomy, mechanism, and response. It supports immediate bleeding-control
intervention in extremis, early pelvic stabilization when pelvic-ring disruption is suspected,
early and repeated hemostatic monitoring, serial lactate as a perfusion marker, prevention of
hypothermia, and immediate coagulation support when massive hemorrhage is expected. Its blood-product,
TXA, pressure-target, and coagulation recommendations depend on injury pattern and clinical context.
This fixed case explicitly excludes severe traumatic brain injury but does not turn those broader
recommendations into a universal protocol.

## Authored vignette and modeled behavior

- Fixed evidence combines a high-energy blunt mechanism and suspected unstable pelvis with
  tachycardia, hypotension, narrow pulse pressure, cool mottled skin, inattention, lactate 5.8 mmol/L,
  temperature 35.3°C, and ongoing concealed blood loss.
- Pelvic stabilization and definitive-control controls record intent only. They do not place a
  device, choose or perform a procedure, move a patient, or stop the modeled blood loss.
- Major-hemorrhage activation opens one fixed bridge of 2 adult packed-red-cell units. Each shared
  teaching unit contributes 300 mL and 60 g hemoglobin. This is not a recommended universal ratio.
- Coagulation, fibrinogen, temperature, and the canonical monitor response can be reviewed. The
  fixed panel does not simulate sampling, viscoelastic testing, assay performance, or goal-directed dosing.

## Deliberate exclusions

This is not a trauma protocol, transfusion guide, procedure trainer, or dosing calculator. It does
not accept real-patient details or model competing injuries, severe traumatic brain injury, airway
or breathing interventions, permissive hypotension targets, TXA, calcium, plasma, platelets,
fibrinogen or cryoprecipitate delivery, component ratios, compatibility, warming delivery, imaging,
packing, embolization, operation, REBOA, transport, repeat transfusion, adverse effects, or outcome.
