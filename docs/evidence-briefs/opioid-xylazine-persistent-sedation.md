# Opioid poisoning: breathing can improve before sedation does

## Teaching claim

This lab rehearses an opioid-compatible respiratory emergency after bystander naloxone and rescue
breathing when persistent sedation, bradycardia, hypotension, and hypothermia keep non-opioid
co-exposure open. It teaches immediate breathing support without using pupils, naloxone response,
routine screening, wounds, or street-drug context to diagnose xylazine or declare “naloxone
resistance.” It is distinct from Emergency Medicine's initial fentanyl rescue and recurrence lab,
Anesthesia's postoperative ventilatory-impairment lab, and Respiratory Medicine's chronic sleep lab.

## Current source floor

- CDC's 2024 clinical-management fact sheet says to give naloxone for suspected opioid overdose and
  provide supportive and symptomatic care, including respiratory and hypotension support. It notes
  there is no approved reversal agent for xylazine and recommends toxicology or addiction expertise.
- FDA's November 8, 2022 alert says possible xylazine can complicate illicit opioid overdose,
  routine toxicology screens do not detect it, associated effects may not reverse with naloxone,
  and veterinary reversal agents are not known to be safe or effective in people and should not be
  used. The case therefore preserves possible co-exposure rather than diagnosing it.

## Fixed fixture

A 44-year-old woman is found beside an unlabeled powder packet after two reported community
naloxone administrations and intermittent rescue breathing. Arrival HR is 50/min, BP 86/48 mmHg,
RR 6/min shallow, room-air SpO2 84%, end-tidal CO2 62 mmHg, and T 35.5°C. She localizes to pressure,
does not answer, and has supplied 2 mm pupils. Glucose is 103 mg/dL; ECG, blood-gas, chemistry, CK,
skin, coingestion, and competing-cause reports are fixed. Product, route, amount, timing, opioid,
adulterant, and other sedative identity remain unknown.

After elapsed time, a strict qualified-team report supplies continued ventilation and oxygenation
plus opioid-antagonist and supportive care; HR is 54/min, BP 90/52 mmHg, RR 14/min, SpO2 97% on
supplied support, end-tidal CO2 43 mmHg, and drowsiness persists. The report does not prove a
specific agent, naloxone resistance, xylazine exposure, treatment effect, recovery, durable safety,
disposition, or outcome.

## Deliberate boundaries

The learner records reconciliation, bounded recognition, qualified ownership, supplied-evidence
review, continued-support intent, strict reassessment, and active-risk handoff only. No history,
examination, monitoring or test interpretation, airway or oxygen care, drug or dose, specialized
screen, wound or withdrawal care, disposition, prognosis, or outcome is exposed.

## Review need

Clinical reviewers should verify the initial and later respiratory, cardiovascular, temperature,
neurologic, blood-gas, chemistry, ECG, skin, and CK fixture; the no-veterinary-antagonist boundary;
the wording around routine screening and persistent sedation; and distinction from opioid-only,
clonidine, benzodiazepine, alcohol, hypoglycemia, trauma, infection, aspiration, and pulmonary injury.
