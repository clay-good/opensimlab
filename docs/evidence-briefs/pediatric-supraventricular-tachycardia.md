# Pediatric supraventricular tachycardia with perfusion compromise evidence brief

## Decision boundary

This lab teaches pediatric whole-child recognition when a fixed probable supraventricular
tachycardia pattern coexists with poor peripheral perfusion despite a measurable blood pressure.
The learner reconciles the abrupt clock, rhythm, breathing, circulation, and whole-child state;
recognizes perfusion compromise; activates qualified pediatric rhythm-care and resuscitation
ownership without delay; then reviews support, open causes, heart-failure risk, and deterioration.
A strictly elapsed fixed response precedes another elapsed recurrence, cardiology, caregiver, and
active-risk handoff.

The lab does not repeat the adult emergency lesson's hypotensive, altered, ischemic presentation or
learner synchronized-cardioversion intent. It does not repeat the stable Cardiology lesson's
vagal-maneuver and adenosine intent sequence. Pediatric perfusion assessment despite preserved
pressure, immediate qualified ownership, and caregiver-centered follow-through define this phase.

## Fixed teaching facts

- A previously well 6-year-old boy is 20 kg and 115 cm. He has no reported prior SVT or known
  congenital heart disease and takes no daily medicine.
- While seated at school 45 minutes earlier, he abruptly reported a pounding heartbeat and
  dizziness.
- A fixed qualified ECG report describes a very regular narrow-complex rhythm at 210/min, QRS 70
  ms, nonvariable RR intervals, and no clearly visible P waves. It is an authored probable SVT
  pattern, not a confirmed mechanism diagnosis.
- Temperature is 37.0°C, HR and palpable central pulse are 210/min, RR is 28/min, BP is 96/60 mmHg
  (MAP 72), and clean room-air SpO₂ is 98%. He is awake, answers appropriately, and is anxious and
  dizzy, with pale cool distal extremities, capillary refill 4 seconds, and weak peripheral pulses
  compared with the central pulse. He breathes spontaneously and has neither syncope nor pulse loss.
- No fever, infectious prodrome, vomiting or diarrhea, poor intake, pain, exertion, stimulant or
  medicine exposure, focal neurological finding, or respiratory distress is authored. These
  snapshots do not exclude another rhythm, pre-excitation, heart disease, heart failure, a
  contributor, or another dangerous cause.
- In a fixed qualified minute-12 report after unspecified acute rhythm care, ECG rhythm is sinus at
  118/min with a visible P wave before each QRS and QRS 70 ms. BP is 102/66 mmHg (MAP 78), RR is
  22/min, room-air SpO₂ is 99%, and temperature is 37.0°C. He is alert and conversational, dizziness
  is easing, extremities are warm, refill is 2 seconds, and peripheral pulses are stronger. No
  recurrent tachycardia is observed in this short window.
- The later report does not identify a treatment modality or prove treatment effect, durable rhythm
  control, complete recovery, mechanism, cause, recurrence exclusion, disposition, or outcome.

All values, negative findings, timing, and serial changes are fictional face-valid fixtures. The
guidelines support the qualitative recognition and escalation boundaries, not these exact values or
a predicted response.

## Source synthesis

The 2025 AHA/AAP Pediatric Advanced Life Support guideline defines pediatric narrow-complex
tachyarrhythmia as QRS duration 0.09 second or less and describes cardiopulmonary compromise through
altered mental status, signs of shock, or hypotension. Its SVT section supports urgent qualified
rhythm care when compromise is present while acknowledging limited pediatric comparative evidence.
This lab therefore exposes ownership rather than a drug, dose, energy, sedation, or cardioversion
control.

The AHA/AAP Pediatric Tachyarrhythmia With a Pulse Algorithm describes probable pediatric SVT using
the combined rhythm context of absent or abnormal P waves, a nonvariable RR interval, age-contextual
rate, and abrupt rate change. Its rate values are stated as usual patterns, not diagnostic cutoffs.
The fixed report uses the full pattern and never asks the learner to diagnose SVT from 210/min alone.

The Resuscitation Council UK 2025 pediatric arrhythmia algorithm distinguishes compensated from
decompensated circulation and integrates consciousness, breathing, refill, pulses, and blood
pressure. This patient deliberately has shock-associated peripheral findings without hypotension or
altered consciousness. Because regional algorithms frame the exact rhythm-control choice
differently, the learner activates qualified care while the simulator withholds modality selection.

## Explicit nonclaims

- No child is examined or palpated, monitored, diagnosed, treated, counseled, or dispositioned by
  the learner.
- No monitor or ECG is acquired or interpreted, and no rhythm mechanism, pre-excitation, structural
  disease, heart failure, contributor, or cause is established or excluded.
- No vagal maneuver, access, oxygen, medicine, concentration, dose, route, flush, sedation, pad,
  synchronization, energy, cardioversion, refractory therapy, device operation, procedure, or other
  treatment is selected or delivered.
- No laboratory test, imaging, or echocardiography is acquired or interpreted.
- A measurable pressure does not establish adequate perfusion, and the supplied rate is not a
  universal diagnostic threshold.
- The fixed later response does not establish treatment modality or effect, complete or durable
  recovery, recurrence exclusion, disposition, prognosis, or outcome.
