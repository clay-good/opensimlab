# Evidence brief: spontaneous-breathing trial

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate adult ICU case rehearses a standardized readiness review, a bounded SBT without
an oxygen increase, recognition of convergent intolerance, restoration of support, recovery
reassessment, and a reversible-cause plan before another trial.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2024 AARC guideline says a rapid shallow breathing index is not required to determine SBT
  readiness, SBTs may be conducted with or without pressure support, standardized daily assessment
  is reasonable, and FiO₂ should not be increased during the trial.
- CHEST/ATS conditionally supports modest inspiratory pressure augmentation for an initial SBT and
  describes resuming ventilation when the trial provokes respiratory-failure signs.
- This authored trial fails through a convergent pattern of excessive work, rapid shallow breathing,
  hypoxemia, tachycardia, diaphoresis, and distress rather than one universal numeric cutoff.
- Passing an SBT assesses spontaneous breathing. Extubation still requires separate airway,
  secretion, neurologic, risk, goals-of-care, and post-extubation-support decisions.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine, program a ventilator, acquire measurements, sample blood, change
sedation, treat a reversible cause, extubate, select post-extubation support, predict outcome, or
assess respiratory-therapy, ventilator, airway, or liberation competence. All panels are authored.
