# Acute delirium with reversible causes

## Scope and distinctness

This lab rehearses recognition and coordinated assessment of acute fluctuating delirium in a stable
older ward patient. It is distinct from postoperative emergence, shock, sepsis, encephalitis,
stroke, nonconvulsive status, intoxication, and future nursing falls-risk labs. Neurology owns the
acute brain-state pattern and diagnostic boundary; qualified teams own examination, cause finding,
capacity, safety, medication, supportive care, and disposition.

## Fixed fixture

- An 82-year-old woman was independently living, managing medicines and finances, and conversing
  normally at 08:00. She has no diagnosed cognitive disorder.
- Over 10 hours, she alternates between withdrawal and short restless periods, has 2 visual
  misperceptions, gives disorganized answers, and cannot sustain attention beyond 2 months backward.
- A qualified practitioner supplies 4AT 8 and a qualified clinician reports delirium after
  integrating history and examination. The score is not a cause, capacity test, severity scale, or
  dementia label.
- At a strict 6-hour report, qualified review identifies bladder volume 690 mL before team-managed
  drainage, diphenhydramine 25 mg 8 hours before the first recorded change, poor intake,
  movement-related pain, fragmented sleep, and absent hearing aids.
- She recognizes her daughter and the hospital but attention still fluctuates. No single cause,
  treatment effect, resolution, baseline recovery, capacity conclusion, or outcome is reported.

## Evidence application

NICE CG103 recommends looking for recent cognitive, perceptual, physical, and social change,
including often-missed hypoactive delirium; qualified 4AT assessment in ordinary hospital settings;
expert final diagnosis; identification of a possible cause or combination of causes; and tailored
care addressing orientation, hydration, hypoxia, infection, mobility, pain, medicines, nutrition,
senses, and sleep. NICE QS63 reinforces early change recognition, reversible-contributor care,
de-escalation before antipsychotic medication, family information, and continuity.

## Boundaries and hazards

- Cognition, perception, physiology, score, contributor review, care, and later findings are authored
  snapshots rather than a validated delirium physiology model.
- Controls only reconcile, recognize, activate owners, review boundaries, review a later report,
  and hand off. They do not perform care.
- A 4AT result is neither the final diagnosis nor a capacity, severity, cause, or dementia test.
- Multiple contributors can coexist. Retention, medicine exposure, pain, sleep loss, sensory loss,
  and poor intake are not assigned individual causal weights.
- Real care requires direct assessment, verified baseline and serial change, individualized
  least-restrictive care, familiar support, and re-evaluation when delirium does not resolve.
