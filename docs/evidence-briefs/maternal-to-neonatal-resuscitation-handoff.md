# Maternal-to-neonatal resuscitation handoff evidence brief

Status: development draft, not clinically reviewed. Checked August 26, 2026.

## Fixture boundary

The authored case supplies an urgent term birth, dedicated qualified maternal and newborn teams, an apneic newborn with an initial qualified heart-rate report below 100/min, assisted ventilation begun within the first minute, a rising heart rate, a stable supplied maternal snapshot, and a fixed 5-minute qualified-team report. The learner coordinates and verifies transfer only. No maternal or newborn examination, monitoring interpretation, resuscitation, oxygen, ventilation, airway care, drug, dose, procedure, transport, family counseling, disposition, or outcome is controlled.

## Evidence carried into the lab

- The 2025 AHA/AAP neonatal-resuscitation guideline requires at least 1 person at every birth whose only responsibility is the newborn and who can provide initial steps and ventilation. Risk assessment, equipment checks, prebirth briefing, and role assignment support readiness.
- Effective lung ventilation, indicated by an increasing heart rate, is the priority for a newborn who needs resuscitation. Ventilation should begin within 60 seconds for apnea or gasping or a heart rate below 100/min after initial steps.
- A newborn requiring resuscitation beyond initial steps needs further observation and monitoring. The guideline includes respiratory effort and oxygenation, heart rate, temperature, and blood glucose; prolonged ventilation or advanced resuscitation warrants closely monitored postresuscitation care.
- Standardized handoff should be active and interruption-limited, give the receiver an opportunity to ask questions, transfer responsibility explicitly, include social context and anticipated problems, and use locally standardized records where available.
- Family communication can be assigned during team briefing. The newborn chain of care extends through recovery, follow-up, family education, and support.

## Deliberate nonclaims

The clinical story, times, maternal values, newborn heart-rate reports, and fixed 5-minute report are fictional. A rising heart rate after qualified ventilation does not prove durable respiratory stability, normal oxygenation, normal temperature or glucose, absence of neurologic injury, maternal recovery, placental cause, safe separation, disposition, prognosis, or outcome. This cognitive lab does not teach neonatal-resuscitation technique or substitute for current NRP training, local protocols, direct care, or multidisciplinary simulation.
