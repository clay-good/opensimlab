# Salicylate: the falling number can be worse

## Scope and distinctness

This lab rehearses an acute aspirin exposure with mixed respiratory alkalosis and anion-gap metabolic
acidosis, then a falling serum concentration paired with worsening acidemia and CNS state. It is
distinct from chronic toxicity, uncomplicated hyperventilation, isolated ketoacidosis, sepsis,
toxic-alcohol exposure, and a concentration-only dialysis calculator.

## Fixed fixture

- A 29-year-old woman presents 7 hours after a supplied immediate-release aspirin ingestion with
  vomiting, tinnitus, diaphoresis, thirst, tachypnea, and volume-depletion clues.
- Initial authored state is HR 108/min, BP 116/74 mmHg, RR 30/min, room-air SpO2 99%, and T 37.6°C.
  Supplied salicylate is 52 mg/dL; pH 7.45, PCO2 23 mmHg, bicarbonate 16 mmol/L, anion gap
  20 mmol/L, potassium 3.2 mmol/L, glucose 92 mg/dL, and creatinine 1.0 mg/dL.
- A strict fixed 9-hour report supplies salicylate 46 mg/dL, pH 7.32, PCO2 25 mmHg, bicarbonate
  13 mmol/L, potassium 3.0 mmol/L, new confusion, persistent RR 30/min, and no pulmonary edema.

## Evidence application

ACMT guidance requires serial concentrations interpreted with acuity, formulation, coingestion,
clinical state, units, and acid-base status. A falling serum concentration with deterioration is
ominous because lower blood pH increases the nonionized fraction available for tissue distribution.
It also warns that loss of the patient’s compensatory ventilation around intubation can rapidly worsen
toxicity. The lab therefore exposes an airway hazard, never an intubation recipe.

ACMT and EXTRIP place CNS, pulmonary, acid-base, renal, clinical-failure, and concentration evidence
together when preparing for extracorporeal treatment. The lab records early qualified preparedness,
not learner eligibility, threshold calculation, modality, prescription, access, or delivery.

## Boundaries

- Exposure, monitoring, serial laboratory evidence, qualified care, and deterioration are authored.
- No fluid composition, bicarbonate or potassium dose, infusion rate, pH target, airway setting,
  dialysis threshold, modality, or procedure control is exposed.
- The fixed transition does not prove tissue concentration, treatment failure, ongoing absorption,
  dialysis eligibility, disposition, or outcome.
