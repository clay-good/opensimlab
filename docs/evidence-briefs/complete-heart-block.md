# Evidence brief: complete heart block

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate urgent Cardiology rhythm lesson rehearses acquired complete-AV-block recognition,
parallel cause review and pacing-capable escalation, elapsed reassessment, and definitive pacing
evaluation handoff before an otherwise stable patient deteriorates.

## Evidence boundary

- Acquired third-degree AV block not caused by reversible or physiologic causes is a permanent-pacing
  indication regardless of current symptoms. This differs from sinus-node dysfunction, where
  symptom-rhythm correlation matters and no minimum rate or pause establishes an indication.
- Reversible and physiologic causes still require review. A negative authored initial panel does not
  prove that every cause has been excluded.
- Current cardiopulmonary compromise determines the acute rescue branch. Present stability does not
  resolve complete block or justify delaying specialist, monitored, pacing-capable care.
- The fixed diagnostic report establishes AV dissociation. The teaching waveform illustrates that
  report but is not a learner-performed ECG interpretation or a capture assessment.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine; acquire or independently interpret ECG, monitoring, laboratory, or
imaging data; diagnose cause; prescribe or deliver oxygen, atropine, another drug, or an infusion;
perform transcutaneous or transvenous pacing; select pacing rate, current, energy, or sedation;
assess electrical or mechanical capture; determine device eligibility or select, implant, or program
a device; determine disposition or prognosis; or predict recurrence, benefit, or outcome.
