# Evidence brief: delayed vasopressor delivery

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate adult ICU case rehearses distinguishing a running pump from drug delivery,
tracing a declared source-to-patient infusion path, recognizing dead-space and startup mechanics,
activating a local safe-start or changeover protocol, and reassessing both delivery evidence and
perfusion without turning the simulator into an infusion calculator.

## Evidence boundary

- Microinfusion delivery can be delayed or irregular because of pump startup, mechanical gaps,
  compliance, resistance, pressure gradients, line dead volume, carrier flow, valves, connectors,
  occlusion state, and changeover technique.
- Downstream volume and combined flow affect transit, but real systems are not captured by one
  universal arithmetic shortcut. Very low flow can prolong startup and occlusion-alarm delay.
- Purging or pressure-management techniques are device-, setup-, policy-, and competency-specific.
  The case records escalation to a local protocol and explicitly rejects an unsupervised flush or
  bolus into the patient.
- The fixed patient response corroborates delivery in this teaching state; it does not prove that
  every blood-pressure change is caused by an infusion system.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not inspect or manipulate a pump, syringe, line, stopcock, connector, valve,
catheter, or patient; measure pressure or flow; calculate a dose or transit time; prime, purge,
flush, bolus, program, prescribe, compound, or deliver a drug; troubleshoot a commercial device;
diagnose shock; determine disposition; or predict outcome. All setup, delivery, and response facts
are authored proxies.
