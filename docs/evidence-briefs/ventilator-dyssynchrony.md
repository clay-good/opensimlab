# Evidence brief: ventilator dyssynchrony

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate adult ICU case rehearses integrating patient effort with pressure, flow, and
volume graphics; checking reversible drivers; recognizing a fixed flow-starvation and premature-
cycling pattern with double triggering; preserving lung-protective limits while matching support;
and proving improvement on reassessment.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2024 AARC patient-ventilator assessment guideline centers the patient and combines physical
  findings, physiology, airway, ventilator settings and alarms, graphics, mechanics, synchrony,
  humidification, secretions, documentation, and response to adjustment.
- A prospective waveform study of 4.26 million breaths associated double-triggered and flow-limited
  breaths with frequent tidal volumes above 10 mL/kg predicted body weight. Deep sedation reduced
  but did not eliminate dyssynchrony, so waveform suppression is not proof that the cause was fixed.
- The fixed pattern here is deliberately simple. Real dyssynchrony includes trigger, flow, cycling,
  auto-trigger, ineffective-effort, reverse-trigger, obstructive, neurologic, metabolic, pain,
  anxiety, device, and disease-specific phenotypes that require expert bedside interpretation.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine the patient, measure pain or sedation, inspect or manipulate an airway
or ventilator, acquire or interpret real waveforms, diagnose a phenotype, select a mode, set flow,
trigger or cycling parameters, prescribe or deliver analgesia or sedation, paralyze, sample blood,
or assess respiratory-therapy, airway, prescribing, procedural, disposition, or outcome competence.
All findings and responses are fixed authored teaching panels.
