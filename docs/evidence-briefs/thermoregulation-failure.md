# Neonatal thermoregulation failure

## Evidence boundary

WHO defines the normal newborn temperature range as 36.5–37.5°C and describes 35.5°C within the
moderate-hypothermia band. Thermal protection is a continuous warm-chain process, not one device or
one measurement. Gestation, size, environment, transfer, wet exposure, illness, feeding, glucose,
and the whole clinical trajectory remain relevant.

The 2025 AHA/AAP neonatal-resuscitation guideline recommends rewarming newborns who are
unintentionally hypothermic below 36°C after stabilization. Evidence is insufficient to choose a
rapid (at least 0.5°C/hour) over slow (less than 0.5°C/hour) rewarming rate. A protocol and frequent
or continuous temperature monitoring are needed, especially when a supraphysiologic warmer set
point is used, because hyperthermia can cause harm. Therapeutic hypothermia is a separate,
strictly protocolized pathway for eligible hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy.

## Authored case estimate

The fixed case is a 35-week-4-day, 2.4 kg newborn at 3 hours after birth. Admission axillary
temperature was 36.6°C; a supplied transfer and warming-continuity gap precedes a verified axillary
temperature of 35.5°C with sleepiness and feeding difficulty. A fixed 45-minute qualified-team
report during locally protocolized rewarming supplies temperature 36.3°C, preserved breathing and
oxygenation, glucose 64 mg/dL, and improved alertness with feeding assessment continuing. The
interval and values are source-informed teaching estimates, not a prescribed rewarming rate,
treatment-effect claim, cause, durable recovery, or outcome prediction.
