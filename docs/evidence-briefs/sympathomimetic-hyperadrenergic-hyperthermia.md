# Sympathomimetic toxicity: calm the surge, protect the person

## Teaching claim

This lab rehearses a declared methamphetamine exposure with severe agitation, paranoia,
diaphoresis, mydriasis, tachycardia, hypertension, hyperthermia, acidosis, and early CK elevation.
It is distinct from serotonin toxicity because no serotonergic interaction, clonus, hyperreflexia,
gastrointestinal hyperactivity, or increased tone is supplied. It is also distinct from isolated
anticholinergic poisoning because sweating and bowel sounds are present and urinary retention is not.

## Current source floor

- The 2024 ASAM/AAAP stimulant-use-disorder guideline recommendations 45, 49, 55-69 support urgent
  review of agitation, tachycardia, hypertension and hyperthermia; evaluation of alternative causes,
  complications and cardiac or muscle injury; compassionate verbal and nonverbal de-escalation;
  GABAergic first-line intent; serial vital-sign monitoring; qualified short-acting adjunct intent for
  persistent hyperadrenergic or ischemic complications; and continued psychiatric and safety review.
- The 2025 AHA Guidelines for CPR and ECC, Part 10, Sections 16.2 and 21.14 identify temperature
  above 40°C and sympathomimetic hyperthermia as rapidly life-threatening, recommend sedation for
  severe agitation, rapid external cooling, and warn that prolonged restraint without sedation is
  potentially harmful. The lab exposes no restraint, cooling method, rate, product, dose, route,
  device, or delivery.

## Fixed fixture

A 28-year-old man arrives 70 minutes after a supplied swallowed methamphetamine exposure. Qualified
observation supplies fearful hypervigilance, severe motor agitation, paranoid statements,
diaphoresis, mydriasis, warm skin, active bowel sounds, HR 150/min, BP 196/112 mmHg (MAP 140),
RR 30/min, room-air SpO2 98%, and core T 40.4°C. Supplied ECG is sinus tachycardia with QRS 90 ms;
pH 7.31, PCO2 34 mmHg, bicarbonate 18 mmol/L, lactate 5.2 mmol/L, creatinine 1.2 mg/dL, CK 980 U/L,
sodium 139 mmol/L, potassium 3.6 mmol/L, and glucose 154 mg/dL. No chest pain, focal deficit,
clonus, hyperreflexia, increased tone, secretion excess, urinary retention, seizure, trauma, or arrest
is supplied.

After elapsed time, a strict qualified-team report supplies calm de-escalation, reported GABAergic
sedation and active cooling, HR 112/min, BP 152/88 mmHg (MAP 109), RR 22/min, T 38.8°C, and calmer
cooperation without supplied chest pain or focal deficit. Hypertension, recurrence, ischemia,
arrhythmia, temperature, renal, CK, seizure, coingestion, psychiatric and safety risks remain open.
