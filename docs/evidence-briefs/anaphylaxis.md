# Emergency anaphylaxis evidence brief

## What this slice teaches

One fixed adult emergency-department vignette asks the learner to join a plausible community food
exposure with abrupt airway, breathing, and circulation compromise. The learner reviews the systemic
pattern, records recumbent positioning and emergency help, gives one fixed adult intramuscular
epinephrine action, adds oxygen and isotonic crystalloid, and reassesses.

## Current source floor

The 2025 Resuscitation Council UK special-circumstances guideline supports prompt recognition from
airway, breathing, or circulation problems with or without skin changes; immediate 500-microgram
intramuscular adrenaline for an adult; repeat treatment if there is no improvement within 5 minutes;
and early crystalloid with response monitoring. The World Allergy Organization 2020 guidance likewise
places intramuscular epinephrine first, supports high-flow oxygen for respiratory distress and further
epinephrine treatment, and describes 20 mL/kg crystalloid for cardiovascular instability. This
vignette stops before repeat-dose or refractory pathways.

## Authored vignette and modeled behavior

- A fixed community food exposure activates the existing bounded anaphylaxis physiology: vascular
  tone loss, plasma-only capillary leak, lower-airway obstruction, and gas-exchange deterioration.
- Whole-patient review reveals authored lip and tongue swelling, wheeze, hypoxemia, hypotension, and
  impaired perfusion. Absent skin findings do not block the response.
- The accepted 500-microgram IM epinephrine action applies a bounded teaching effect. High-flow
  oxygen changes inspired oxygen, and a fixed 1,500 mL bolus uses the shared crystalloid model.
- Serial reassessment reads the canonical monitor and fixed whole-patient domains. Recovery is not
  diagnostic proof or an individual outcome prediction.

## Deliberate exclusions

This is not a diagnostic system, drug or fluid calculator, injection trainer, airway trainer, or
complete anaphylaxis algorithm. It does not model examination acquisition, formulation or device
selection, preparation, injection or IV access technique, delivery rate, repeat-dose timing,
refractory infusions, adjunct medicines, airway procedures, arrest, observation, biphasic reaction,
discharge, safety planning, specialist referral, local protocol, recurrence, or outcome.
