# Evidence brief: ventilator circuit disconnection

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate adult ICU case rehearses recognizing that commanded ventilator settings are not
delivered breaths, immediately bridging oxygenation and ventilation, inspecting the patient-to-
ventilator path, recording reconnection intent, and proving restored support across the patient,
airway, circuit, ventilator, and independent monitoring.

## Evidence boundary

- A patient-ventilator assessment is patient-centered and includes bedside physiology, the airway,
  ventilator settings and monitoring, and the circuit rather than trusting a setting or alarm alone.
- A disconnected circuit can interrupt ventilation and oxygenation. FDA safety material supports
  checking circuit continuity and having alternative ventilation available when ventilator therapy
  stops or becomes unreliable.
- Alarm behavior is device- and configuration-dependent. The case declares a fixed teaching alarm
  and response trajectory; it does not generalize its timing, wording, priority, or sensitivity to
  another ventilator.
- The authored panel separates commanded rate and tidal volume from zero exhaled tidal volume,
  absent airway pressure and capnography, and a credible falling saturation. Reconnection is not
  closure until delivered breaths and the whole-patient response are reassessed.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not hear or configure an alarm, examine a patient, measure oxygenation, inspect or
handle a circuit, squeeze a bag, deliver oxygen or ventilation, secure a physical connection,
program a ventilator, troubleshoot a proprietary device, diagnose the cause, perform a procedure,
predict oxygen reserve, determine disposition, or assess clinical or technical competence. All
findings, actions, alarm behavior, and responses are authored teaching proxies.
