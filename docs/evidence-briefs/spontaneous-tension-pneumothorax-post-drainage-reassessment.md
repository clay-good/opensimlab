# Spontaneous tension pneumothorax post-drainage reassessment

## Intended rehearsal

Reconcile a reported spontaneous tension-pneumothorax event and experienced-team emergency
drainage; review current safety without declaring resolution; keep the pleural drain, persistent-air-
leak questions, complications, and recurrent-deterioration triggers visible; connect cause and
recurrence-prevention questions to patient preferences and named pleural and thoracic owners; and
hand off the unresolved work.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2023 BTS pleural-disease guideline makes symptoms and physiological compromise central to
  acute pneumothorax decisions. It identifies a tension presentation as a reason recurrence
  prevention and thoracic-surgical review may matter, but it does not make one procedure automatic.
- The 2024 joint ERS/EACTS/ESTS guideline supports specialist, individualized spontaneous-
  pneumothorax care and records important evidence gaps for secondary spontaneous pneumothorax and
  persistent-air-leak interventions.
- The lab therefore rehearses review, safety-netting, shared planning, ownership, and handoff. It
  does not teach drain operation, decompression, pleurodesis, thoracoscopy, or surgery.

## Authored vignette

A 68-year-old man with emphysema has a fixed spontaneous right tension-pneumothorax pattern with
severe dyspnea, hypoxemia, hypotension, confusion, cool perfusion, and markedly reduced right chest
movement and air entry. An experienced team immediately treats the emergency and places a right
pleural drain. Six hours later he is alert and speaking fully with HR 96/min, RR 22/min, BP 108/64
mmHg, room-air SpO₂ 93%, warm perfusion, and improved but still reduced right air entry. A fixed
radiograph reports partial re-expansion. A fixed drain record reports an upright bottle below the
site, an intact visible connection, respiratory swing, and intermittent bubbling. Persistent air
leak, durable patency, full re-expansion, complications, causes, recurrence prevention, patient
priorities, and definitive pleural strategy remain unresolved.

## Exclusions

The screen does not examine; acquire or interpret monitoring, imaging, ultrasound, pleural-pressure,
or laboratory data; diagnose; inspect or manipulate a drain; choose suction or clamping; flush,
remove, replace, or insert a drain; select a device, site, technique, oxygen target, drug, dose,
aspiration, pleurodesis, thoracoscopy, surgery, or treatment; perform decompression or another
procedure; determine disposition or prognosis; or predict recurrence, resolution, or outcome.
