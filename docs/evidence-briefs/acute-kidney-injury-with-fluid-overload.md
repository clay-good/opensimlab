# Acute kidney injury with fluid overload evidence brief

## Scope

A fixed adult ICU case with severe oliguric AKI, +8.2 L cumulative balance, 9 kg weight gain,
worsening pulmonary edema, and poor reported response to an adequate loop-diuretic challenge. The
lesson covers recognition, reversible and urgent-complication review, limiting further accumulation,
individualized kidney-support planning, and immediate reassessment. It does not diagnose AKI,
prescribe diuretics, or provide kidney support.

## Evidence translated into the lesson

- KDIGO recommends emergent kidney-replacement therapy for life-threatening changes in fluid,
  electrolyte, or acid-base balance and recommends using the broader clinical context, modifiable
  conditions, and laboratory trends rather than single BUN or creatinine thresholds.
- The KDIGO controversies conference reframes initiation around whether metabolic and fluid demands
  exceed kidney capacity while acknowledging that exact measurement and optimal timing remain
  uncertain. Complications, global prognosis, potential recovery, and patient preferences belong in
  the decision; modality follows clinical status and available expertise.
- STARRT-AKI found no 90-day survival benefit from a universal accelerated initiation strategy in
  severe AKI and found more adverse events and more kidney-support dependence among survivors in the
  accelerated group. The lesson therefore does not make AKI stage alone an automatic start trigger.
- The fixed response achieves a negative immediate balance and improves oxygenation while preserving
  uncertainty about ongoing fluid removal, solute control, hemodynamic tolerance, kidney recovery,
  modality, duration, prognosis, and outcome.

## Boundaries and disputed practice

Volume assessment, diuretic strategy, urgency, access, modality, dose, anticoagulation, net removal,
medication clearance, nutrition, goals, resource availability, and timing depend on the full patient
trajectory and expert team. The browser does not perform or assess any of them. No copyrighted
algorithm, prescription, or dosing table is reproduced.
