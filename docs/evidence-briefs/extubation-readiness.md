# Evidence brief: extubation readiness

## Learning boundary

- **Target learner:** anesthesia learners integrating adult low-risk awake-extubation readiness.
- **Environment:** one stable, intubated adult after surgery and anesthetic delivery have ended.
- **Primary objective:** treat quantitative neuromuscular recovery as necessary but not sufficient,
  then integrate awake airway protection, spontaneous gas exchange, airway risk, and rescue planning.
- **Why simulation adds value:** progressive disclosure prevents a reassuring train-of-four ratio
  from standing in for the complete decision and makes skipped readiness domains inspectable.

## Evidence and applicability

1. Difficult Airway Society Extubation Guidelines Group, Popat M, Mitchell V, et al. *Difficult
   Airway Society Guidelines for the management of tracheal extubation*. Anaesthesia.
   2012;67:318-340. PMID 22321104; DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2044.2012.07075.x. Consulted 2026-08-24.
   The guideline frames extubation as elective and stepwise, emphasizing planning, preparation,
   risk stratification, full neuromuscular recovery, awake response, adequate spontaneous
   ventilation, continued oxygen delivery, rescue planning, and post-extubation care.
2. Apfelbaum JL, Hagberg CA, Connis RT, et al. *2022 American Society of Anesthesiologists Practice
   Guidelines for Management of the Difficult Airway*. Anesthesiology. 2022;136:31-81.
   PMID 34762729; DOI 10.1097/ALN.0000000000004002. Consulted 2026-08-24. The guideline supports a
   preformulated extubation strategy, deliberate awake-versus-not-awake decision, skilled help,
   supplemental oxygen, and a reintubation plan when difficulty is possible.
3. Thilen SR, Weigel WA, Todd MM, et al. *2023 American Society of Anesthesiologists Practice
   Guidelines for Monitoring and Antagonism of Neuromuscular Blockade*. Anesthesiology.
   2023;138:13-41. PMID 36520073; DOI 10.1097/ALN.0000000000004379. Consulted 2026-08-24. The
   guideline supports quantitative confirmation of a train-of-four ratio of at least 0.90 before extubation.

These sources support the domains and strategic sequence. They do not supply this fictional patient,
the exact breathing values, a universal checklist, or a prediction of extubation success.

## Modeled variables and exact fixture

- The case starts with a secured tracheal tube, delivered ventilation, and a static quantitative
  train-of-four ratio of 0.93. That value opens the broader assessment but cannot earn final credit alone.
- The awake-airway review reveals sustained eye opening, command following, strong cough, and cleared
  secretions. The gas-exchange review reveals spontaneous breathing at 14/min, average tidal volume
  420 mL, end-tidal carbon dioxide 39 mmHg, and oxygen saturation 98% on inspired oxygen 0.40.
- The airway-plan review declares uncomplicated mask ventilation and intubation, no new airway change,
  and availability of oxygen, monitoring, skilled help, and a reintubation plan.
- The expert path records readiness for a planned awake extubation. The engine deliberately keeps the
  tube and delivered ventilation in place and rejects skipped, repeated, unknown, and out-of-lesson actions.

## Exclusions and unsafe inferences

The browser does not measure consciousness, respiratory effort, airway reflexes, cough, secretions,
airway edema, bleeding, distortion, cuff leak, analgesia, acid-base status, or hemodynamic optimization.
It does not teach suction, positioning, cuff deflation, tube removal, oxygen-delivery technique, deep
extubation, at-risk strategies, airway-exchange catheters, reintubation, communication, monitoring,
complications, or outcome. The exact values are fixtures, not universal readiness thresholds.

## Review needs

Required review domains are adult airway management, emergence and recovery, neuromuscular monitoring,
respiratory physiology, simulation education, accessibility, and patient safety. The content remains
unsigned draft work until those reviews are recorded against the exact content version.
