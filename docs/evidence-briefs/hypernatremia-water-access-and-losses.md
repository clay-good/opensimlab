# Hypernatremia: water access and ongoing losses

Content version 0.1.0; renal/electrolyte preview. Sources checked August 27, 2026.
Independent clinical approval remains pending. Educational simulation, not patient care.

## Distinct care needs

Restoring circulation, replacing a water deficit, replacing continuing losses, and ensuring safe
water access solve different problems. This lesson separates them without providing a fluid
calculator or prescription. It is distinct from the endocrine scenario involving known AVP
deficiency and omitted prescribed desmopressin.

[Yun, Baek, and Kim's 2023 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10175862/)
supports volume-first care during hypotension, contextual interpretation of pretreatment urine,
and individualized replacement with serial reassessment. Low-volume concentrated urine supports
the supplied water-access and gastrointestinal-loss context. It does not exclude every renal
cause, establish current clearance, or turn a historical urine result into a response model.

[NICE CG174](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg174/chapter/key-priorities-for-implementation),
updated May 5, 2017, informs general fluid assessment and reassessment. Sections 1.1, 1.2, and 1.5
were checked in the [NICE guideline text](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK554180/?report=printable).
Its scope limits remain relevant; it is not a hypernatremia-specific dosing protocol.
[NHS Ayrshire and Arran's local guidance](https://aaamedicines.org.uk/guidelines/electrolyte-disturbances/management-of-hypernatraemia/),
reviewed January 2026 and updated May 2026, supports assessment of water intake, diarrhea,
circulation, and ongoing replacement. Its local rates and monitoring intervals are not adopted
as universal targets. US/GB controls share this fiction, not a validated regional treatment matrix.

Adult correction-rate evidence remains uncertain. The retrospective
[Chacon-Palma et al. 2025 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40152929/) reports differing mortality
and disposition associations, not a causal optimal rate. Its publication record and abstract
were checked alongside indexed article text. Neither that study nor the review validates the
numerical trajectory below. No dose, composition, volume, route, correction formula, or predicted
neurologic injury is supplied. Source material is paraphrased, not reproduced as clinical figures.

## Observation, transfer, and private reporting

Volume care has no administrative or laboratory prerequisite. After circulation improves,
water replacement and loss care are independently available without a new-test gate. Access
support is available at any time and includes individualized route, swallowing safety, assistance,
and alternatives when drinking is unsafe. It never forces oral water.

Sodium-only, fluid-balance-only, and full assessments retain separate timestamps. Partial checks
cannot refresh the full panel. Handoff requires delivered care and access support, context,
monitoring, qualified support, and a current full assessment after a water response. It can
transfer observed recurrence with the newly delivered loss-care response pending. Normal sodium,
all older teaching panels, symptom resolution, and an error-free history are not prerequisites.
