# Methanol toxicity: the gaps are clues, not answers

## Teaching claim

This lab rehearses a declared windshield-washer-fluid exposure with delayed visual symptoms,
tachypnea, confusion, high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis, and an increased osmolar gap. It teaches
that exposure, clock, visual injury, acid-base trajectory, and complementary gaps belong together;
neither gap is a standalone diagnostic or treatment calculator.

## Current source floor

- EXTRIP's 2015 systematic review and consensus statement treats new visual deficits and metabolic
  acidosis attributed to methanol as severe features that support urgent extracorporeal treatment.
  It states that the osmolar gap may be informative when a methanol concentration is unavailable.
  Evidence quality and clinical context remain explicit constraints. PMID 25493973; DOI
  `10.1097/CCM.0000000000000708`.
- The current DailyMed fomepizole label supports beginning treatment immediately when methanol or
  ethylene-glycol ingestion is suspected from history and/or anion-gap acidosis, an increased osmolar
  gap, visual disturbance, or a documented concentration. The learner surface exposes no dose,
  calculation, concentration cutoff, dialysis threshold, modality, or stopping rule. Set ID
  `911312e2-3a7c-4c97-88a8-b8d92cd12923`.

## Fixed fixture

A 45-year-old man arrives approximately 14 hours after a supplied windshield-washer-fluid
ingestion with nausea, abdominal discomfort, headache, blurred snowfield-like vision, tachypnea,
and confusion. HR is 118/min, BP 110/68 mmHg, RR 30/min, room-air SpO2 98%, and T 36.6°C. Supplied
reports include pH 7.19, PCO2 20 mmHg, bicarbonate 7 mmol/L, sodium 140 mmol/L, chloride 102 mmol/L,
glucose 108 mg/dL, BUN 14 mg/dL, creatinine 1.2 mg/dL, lactate 2.4 mmol/L, measured osmolality
322 mOsm/kg, ethanol below the reporting limit, authored anion gap 31 mmol/L, and authored osmolar
gap 31 mOsm/kg. The values are internally consistent teaching fixtures, not reference ranges.

After elapsed time, a strict qualified-team report supplies source cessation, reported fomepizole
and cofactor therapy, acid-base support, and active extracorporeal preparation; pH 7.27, bicarbonate
10 mmol/L, HR 106/min, BP 112/70 mmHg, RR 26/min, persistent blurred vision, and arousable confusion.
Clearance, treatment effect, visual or neurologic recovery, durable acid-base control, renal and
electrolyte safety, airway safety, extracorporeal completion, disposition, and outcome remain open.

## Deliberate boundaries

The learner records reconciliation, coupled-pattern recognition, qualified ownership, supplied-
evidence review, bounded source, antidote, cofactor, acid-base, extracorporeal, surveillance and
airway intent, strict reassessment, and active-risk handoff only. No real history, examination,
monitoring or test acquisition or interpretation, calculation, diagnosis, prescribing, airway care,
dialysis choice, procedure, treatment effect, disposition, prognosis, or outcome is exposed.

## Review need

Clinical reviewers should verify the fixture arithmetic, source timing, visual and acid-base pattern,
antidote and extracorporeal boundaries, later state, and distinction from ethylene glycol,
isopropanol, ketoacidosis, renal failure, lactic acidosis, salicylate, and generic severe-acidemia labs.
