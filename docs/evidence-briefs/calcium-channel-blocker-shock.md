# Calcium-channel blocker toxicity: read the glucose with the shock

## Scope and distinctness

This lab rehearses a declared extended-release diltiazem exposure with bradycardia, complete AV
block, mixed cardiogenic and vasodilatory shock, impaired consciousness, and hyperglycemia. It is
distinct from intrinsic conduction disease, beta-blocker toxicity, isolated hyperglycemia, and a
generic bradycardia or pacing algorithm.

## Fixed fixture

- A 56-year-old man arrives 5 hours after a witnessed extended-release diltiazem-only ingestion. He
  is drowsy but answers briefly. HR is 34/min, BP 68/36 mmHg, RR 18/min, room-air SpO2 97%, and
  T 36.3°C.
- A qualified ECG report supplies complete AV block, atrial rate 78/min, ventricular escape 34/min,
  and QRS 104 ms. A qualified focused cardiac report supplies globally reduced LV contraction,
  low systemic vascular tone, and no pulmonary edema. Glucose is 238 mg/dL, pH 7.29, bicarbonate
  18 mmol/L, lactate 4.6 mmol/L, sodium 137 mmol/L, potassium 4.1 mmol/L, and creatinine 1.0 mg/dL.
  The treating-team report supplies persistent shock after protocol-selected atropine and initial
  vasopressor care.
- A strict fixed 45-minute report supplies clearer mentation, sinus rhythm 64/min, BP 96/58 mmHg,
  glucose 176 mg/dL, potassium 3.4 mmol/L, and lactate 3.0 mmol/L without new hypoxemia or edema.

## Evidence application

The AHA describes severe calcium-channel-blocker poisoning as shock from bradycardia, vasodilation,
or loss of inotropy, with prolonged effects common after sustained-release products. Current
recommendations pair high-dose insulin with vasopressors for life-threatening hypotension, consider
calcium reasonable, and reserve extracorporeal support for pharmacologically refractory shock.
Protocolized insulin care includes glucose, potassium, and volume surveillance. The lab therefore
records qualified intent and surveillance, never a learner-selected product or dose.

DailyMed identifies bradycardia, hypotension, heart block, and cardiac failure after diltiazem
overdose and states that diltiazem does not appear removable by peritoneal dialysis or hemodialysis.
The lab never makes one pulse, ECG interval, glucose value, or reported exposure independently
diagnostic.

## Boundaries

- Exposure, ECG, cardiac-function report, laboratory evidence, prior care, and response are authored.
- No vasopressor, calcium, insulin, glucose, electrolyte, fluid, dose, rate, target, access, airway,
  pacing, decontamination, lipid, methylene blue, ECLS, or rescue control is exposed.
- The fixed response does not prove treatment effect, durable perfusion, completed absorption,
  glucose or electrolyte stability, coingestant exclusion, safe disposition, or outcome.
