# Evidence brief: symptomatic bradycardia

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate return Cardiology visit rehearses stable sinus-bradycardia evaluation: distinguish
chronic symptoms from acute compromise, review reversible context, establish temporal symptom-rhythm
correlation, and record shared pacing evaluation plus ownership without a threshold shortcut.

## Evidence boundary

- For sinus-node dysfunction, no minimum heart rate or pause duration alone establishes a permanent
  pacing indication; temporal correlation between symptoms and bradycardia matters.
- Reversible or physiologic causes and whether rate-slowing therapy is necessary are reviewed before
  pacing decisions. The lab does not tell learners to stop a medication.
- Pacing decisions are patient-centered and preference-sensitive. The controls record referral and
  shared-decision intent, not device eligibility, selection, implantation, programming, or benefit.
- The 2025 acute algorithm separates patients without acute cardiopulmonary compromise from those
  requiring atropine or rescue pacing. This stable outpatient case supplies explicit change triggers.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine; acquire or interpret an ECG, monitor, laboratory test, or image;
diagnose sinus-node dysfunction or medication causality; prescribe, stop, dose, or deliver medication;
deliver oxygen; provide atropine, an adrenergic infusion, or acute pacing; determine pacemaker
eligibility or select, implant, or program a device; determine disposition; predict prognosis,
recurrence, benefit, or outcome; or assess counseling or procedural competence.
