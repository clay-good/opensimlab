## Source synthesis

The 2026 AHA/ASA acute ischemic stroke guideline says trained professionals should determine whether
a deficit is disabling from its patient-specific functional consequences; NIHSS alone is
insufficient. It recommends prompt thrombolysis for eligible disabling deficits but not for
hyperacute mild nondisabling deficits and supports early qualified dual-antiplatelet care in the
latter population. This lab supplies the assessment and records strategy ownership without selecting
or delivering any treatment.

ARAMIS was a multicenter randomized noninferiority trial in China comparing one dual-antiplatelet
regimen with alteplase in selected minor nondisabling stroke. Its primary result supports the current
guideline direction, but its open-label design, crossover, exclusion of possible cardioembolism,
China-only population, and treatment-specific protocol prevent direct universalization. This lab
does not reproduce its regimen or predict its outcomes.

PRISMS did not demonstrate improved 90-day favorable functional outcome with alteplase versus aspirin
in its studied minor nondisabling population, but early termination left the trial inconclusive. The
scenario does not treat PRISMS as an equivalence proof and relies on the integrated 2026 guideline
rather than one trial alone.
