# Intracranial-hypertension evidence brief

## Scope

A fixed adult ICU case 6 hours after severe traumatic brain injury and lesion evacuation. A
parenchymal monitor reports sustained ICP 28 mmHg while MAP 82 mmHg yields CPP 54 mmHg. The lesson
covers recognition, context, first-tier brain protection, individualized hyperosmolar-rescue intent,
and immediate reassessment. It does not teach monitor interpretation, prescribing, a procedure, or
neuroprognostication.

## Evidence translated into the lesson

- The Brain Trauma Foundation recommends treating ICP above 22 mmHg, using ICP together with
  clinical and CT findings, and targeting CPP between 60 and 70 mmHg. It cautions against aggressive
  attempts to maintain CPP above 70 mmHg because of respiratory risk. These are contextual teaching
  boundaries, not autonomous treatment triggers.
- The SIBICC consensus algorithm organizes monitored severe-TBI care into baseline measures and
  escalating tiers. This lesson uses only an ordered first response: experienced-team activation,
  neutral elevated head position and venous drainage, systemic brain protection, individualized
  hyperosmolar rescue, and reassessment. It does not reproduce the algorithm or simulate later tiers.
- The Neurocritical Care Society guideline conditionally favors hypertonic sodium solutions over
  mannitol for initial TBI-related elevated ICP or edema, with low-quality evidence, while recognizing
  mannitol as an effective alternative when hypertonic sodium is unsuitable. Neither therapy should
  be expected to improve neurologic outcome. The lesson therefore records an expert-selected intent
  and safety review without teaching one universal agent, formulation, concentration, dose, or route.
- The fixed response lowers ICP and restores CPP into the authored range while preserving uncertainty
  about durability, examination, imaging, herniation, surgery, recovery, prognosis, and outcome.

## Boundaries and disputed practice

Threshold duration, autoregulation, CPP target, head position, ventilation, sedation, hyperosmolar
agent and dose, fluid strategy, monitoring, drain use, imaging, and surgical escalation depend on the
patient, injury, monitor fidelity, local protocol, and expert team. The browser does not perform or
assess any of them. No copyrighted algorithm, concentration table, or dosing table is reproduced.
