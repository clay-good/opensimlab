# Hyponatremia after rescue: keep correction controlled

This preview rehearses emerging water diuresis after initial emergency treatment, not another
seizure-rescue checklist. The existing emergency-medicine scenario ends with first-hour rescue
and an overcorrection contingency; this lesson makes that later risk evolve and permits recovery
after a missed opportunity. It is unsigned and not clinically reviewed.

## Objectives, hazards, and verification owed

Five objectives cover the original correction window/high-risk plan, explicit surveillance,
response to observed aquaresis, reassessment with retained peak evidence, and continuing-care
handoff. Handoff requires support, risk/cause/potassium review, monitoring, water-loss control,
relowering when indicated by the authored branch, and a fresh later response observation.
It does not require an unnecessary relowering action after successful early prevention.

There is no sodium-normalization target, input calculator, prescription, water-balance solver,
procedural competence claim, guaranteed ODS prevention, immediate ODS catastrophe, or discharge
clearance. Unknown duration is not acute water intoxication. Continuing 24–48-hour sodium,
urine, potassium, neurologic, and cause surveillance remains with the receiving qualified team.
Expert, common-error, recovery, no-action, refused/boundary, region, guidance, replay, private-report,
keyboard, screen-reader, phone, reduced-motion, zoom, and offline evidence must be verified before
completion. Implementation and engineering checks do not supply independent clinical review.
