# Evidence brief: Trauma primary survey

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate adult emergency case rehearses receiving a structured major-trauma handoff,
controlling catastrophic hemorrhage first, continuing a complete `<C>ABCDE` survey, addressing
suspected pelvic bleeding without unnecessary imaging delay, preventing heat loss, and repeating the
survey after interventions before definitive-control handoff.

## Evidence boundary

- NICE NG39 describes a prioritized `<C>ABCDE` primary survey: catastrophic hemorrhage, airway with
  spinal protection, breathing, circulation, disability, exposure and environment.
- Direct pressure is first-line for external bleeding; a tourniquet is appropriate for life-threatening
  major-limb hemorrhage when direct pressure fails. Suspected bleeding from a pelvic fracture after
  blunt high-energy trauma supports a purpose-made pelvic binder.
- Active or suspected active bleeding requires early major-hemorrhage activation, blood-based
  resuscitation, and early tranexamic-acid consideration within the applicable window. Exact products,
  ratios, doses, and access follow the current regional protocol and patient state.
- In unstable suspected hemorrhage, diagnostic imaging should be limited to what directs intervention;
  a negative FAST does not exclude bleeding. Heat loss should be minimized, and findings should be
  documented as improving or deteriorating on reassessment.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine the patient, confirm a pulse or injury, stabilize the spine, apply pressure
or a tourniquet, open an airway, give oxygen or ventilation, examine the chest, obtain access, deliver
blood or medication, apply a pelvic binder, acquire or interpret imaging, log-roll, warm the patient,
perform surgery or interventional radiology, arrange transport, or assess psychomotor, team, diagnosis,
definitive-control, disposition, prognosis, or outcome competence.
