# Evidence brief: Severe hyponatremia with seizure

## Intended rehearsal

This advanced adult emergency-department case rehearses recognizing severe symptomatic hypotonic
hyponatremia after a witnessed seizure, stabilizing in parallel, recording immediate symptom-led
hypertonic-saline intent, stopping after a small first-hour rise with neurologic improvement, and
protecting the next 24 hours with correction ceilings, cause control, and overcorrection surveillance.

## Evidence boundary

- The revised 2022 Society for Endocrinology guidance classifies seizure and deep somnolence as
  severe symptoms and recommends immediate IV hypertonic sodium chloride irrespective of cause.
  This case authors sodium 112 mmol/L, glucose 96 mg/dL, and measured osmolality 238 mOsm/kg.
- The immediate goal is a 5 mmol/L sodium rise within the first hour to reduce danger from cerebral
  edema while limiting overcorrection. The fixed first-hour panel is sodium 117 mmol/L with improved
  alertness; treatment stops rather than chasing a normal value.
- The authored safety boundary limits total rise to 10 mmol/L in the first 24 hours and 8 mmol/L per
  24 hours thereafter. Serial sodium and urine-output surveillance remain necessary because a water
  diuresis can accelerate correction.
- Chlorthalidone and high free-water intake are plausible contributors, not a completed etiologic
  diagnosis. Cause-specific evaluation and a specialist overcorrection plan continue after rescue.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine the patient, collect or validate specimens, interpret laboratory data,
classify volume status, select a regional hypertonic-saline concentration or bolus, prescribe or
deliver treatment, manage an airway or seizure, calculate sodium change, model urine electrolyte
handling, adjudicate the cause, prevent or reverse overcorrection, or assess psychomotor, team,
disposition, recurrence, neurologic injury, prognosis, or outcome competence.
