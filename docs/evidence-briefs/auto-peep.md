# Evidence brief: auto-PEEP and dynamic hyperinflation

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate adult ICU case rehearses recognizing incomplete exhalation across the patient,
flow-time graphic, breathing pattern, pressures, gas exchange, and circulation; separating set,
total, and intrinsic PEEP with a valid passive expiratory-hold proxy; recording a cause-directed
plan that preserves more expiratory time; and proving less trapping on reassessment.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2024 AARC patient-ventilator assessment guideline strongly recommends assessing PEEP and
  auto-PEEP and places that mechanics review inside a whole-patient bedside assessment.
- Persistent expiratory flow when the next breath starts is a clue to incomplete lung emptying. In
  a passive patient, an end-expiratory hold can measure total PEEP; intrinsic PEEP is total minus
  set PEEP. Spontaneous effort, expiratory-muscle activity, airway closure, and heterogeneity can
  make a single static value incomplete or invalid.
- Dynamic hyperinflation can increase triggering work, intrathoracic pressure, hemodynamic
  compromise, and barotrauma risk. Management depends on the cause and can include treating
  obstruction, reducing minute-ventilation demand, and preserving more expiratory time.
- External PEEP is not a universal correction. Its effect depends on expiratory flow limitation,
  patient effort, and direct mechanics reassessment, so this lesson records specialist review only.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine the patient, acquire or interpret real waveforms, perform an expiratory
hold, manipulate an airway or ventilator, diagnose obstruction or dynamic hyperinflation, choose a
mode or setting, titrate external PEEP, prescribe or deliver bronchodilators, sedation or paralysis,
sample blood, perform emergency decompression, or assess respiratory-therapy, prescribing,
procedural, disposition, or outcome competence. All findings and responses are fixed authored
teaching panels.
