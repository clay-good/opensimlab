# Obstructive shock from tension pneumothorax evidence brief

## What this slice teaches

One fixed adult emergency-department vignette asks the learner to join penetrating chest trauma,
markedly reduced unilateral chest movement and air entry, hypoxia, and severe hemodynamic compromise.
The learner escalates, records high-concentration oxygen, confirms immediate left-chest decompression
intent without waiting for imaging, and reassesses oxygenation and circulation.

## Current source floor

The 2025 WSES-AAST thoracic-trauma guideline identifies tension pneumothorax as life-threatening and
requiring immediate recognition and treatment. The 2025 European Resuscitation Council guideline
describes tension pneumothorax as obstructing venous return and impairing gas exchange, supports
clinical examination or POCUS as the diagnostic basis in hemodynamic instability, and calls for
immediate decompression when it is suspected with severe hypotension. The interface does not turn
those recommendations into procedural instruction.

## Authored vignette and modeled behavior

- A spontaneously breathing 29-year-old arrives after penetrating left-chest trauma. The fixed
  pleural drive progressively lowers stroke volume, pressure, end-tidal carbon dioxide, and oxygen saturation.
- Bilateral assessment returns markedly reduced left chest movement and air entry with preserved
  right air entry. It is a scripted result, not an examination or diagnostic model.
- High-concentration oxygen changes the shared inspired-oxygen state but cannot relieve obstructed
  venous return or replace pleural treatment.
- Accepted decompression intent stops the active drive. Residual physiology clears gradually so
  reassessment remains visible; this does not prove real technical success or predict recovery.

## Deliberate exclusions

This is not a diagnostic system, POCUS lab, trauma algorithm, or procedure trainer. It does not
model examination technique, ultrasound or imaging acquisition, pleural gas volume or pressure,
airway pressure, procedural site or equipment selection, needle or open thoracostomy, chest-drain
placement, complications, recurrence, concurrent hemorrhage, tamponade, pulmonary embolism, local
protocol, transport, disposition, or outcome.
