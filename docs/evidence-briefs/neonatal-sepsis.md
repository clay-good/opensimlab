# Neonatal sepsis

## Evidence boundary

Early-onset neonatal sepsis is uncommon but potentially fatal, and its clinical signs are
nonspecific. AAP guidance for infants born at or after 35 weeks emphasizes maternal and newborn
clinical characteristics because laboratory tests alone are not sufficiently sensitive or specific
to guide management. Risk algorithms support structured assessment of well-appearing infants; they
do not overrule new clinical illness.

NICE NG195 identifies blood culture as the reference microbiologic test and recommends obtaining it
before antibiotics, while explicitly warning that culture collection must not delay treatment.
Baseline and serial CRP can contribute to reassessment, but one CRP cannot diagnose or exclude
infection. Empiric antimicrobial selection, investigations, supportive care, and stop decisions
remain locally protocolized qualified-team work.

## Authored case estimate

The fixed case is a 38-week-5-day infant at 10 hours after maternal intrapartum fever, 22-hour
rupture of membranes, unknown GBS status, and maternal antibiotics 1 hour before birth. New
lethargy, poor feeding, temperature 35.9°C, tachycardia, tachypnea, increased work, room-air SpO2
93%, and capillary refill 4 seconds prompt immediate qualified escalation. A fixed 1-hour report
after cultures, locally protocolized empiric antimicrobials, warming, respiratory and circulatory
support supplies partial physiologic improvement with cultures pending. It is not a treatment-effect
claim, diagnosis, exclusion, durable recovery, or outcome prediction.
