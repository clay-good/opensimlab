# Pacemaker and cautery planning evidence brief

## What this slice teaches

One stable adult preoperative vignette asks the learner to review a fixed pacemaker record, join
pacing dependence to an above-umbilicus procedure with anticipated monopolar electrosurgery,
choose a coordinated asynchronous-pacing plan, and document external backup, monitoring, and
post-procedure restoration.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2024 AHA/ACC perioperative cardiovascular guideline recommends a preoperative management
  plan when electromagnetic interference is anticipated, including device type, manufacturer,
  model, pacing dependence, magnet behavior, procedure details, and the planned response.
- For pacing-dependent patients having surgery above the umbilicus with anticipated interference,
  the guideline supports reprogramming or a confirmed magnet strategy that produces asynchronous
  pacing. Magnet behavior is device specific and cannot be assumed.
- The 2024 AHA scientific statement emphasizes procedure location, device type, pacing dependence,
  electrosurgery type and current path, magnet accessibility and response, backup capability, and
  restoration of altered settings before leaving monitored care.

## Modeled behavior

- A device-record review reveals a transvenous dual-chamber pacemaker, complete atrioventricular
  block, pacing dependence, recent normal function, and a documented device-specific magnet response.
- A separate procedure review reveals right shoulder surgery above the umbilicus with anticipated
  monopolar electrosurgery.
- Only after both reviews may the learner record one plan. The expert path coordinates
  asynchronous pacing with the CIED team.
- A final action documents external pacing/defibrillation availability, continuous monitoring, and
  explicit restoration before leaving monitored care.

## Deliberate exclusions

This is not a pacemaker, defibrillator, or electromagnetic-interference simulator. It does not
interrogate, program, pace, sense, apply a magnet, reproduce magnet response, calculate current
paths, place a dispersive electrode, deliver electrosurgery, model inhibition or inappropriate
therapy, teach emergency response, or assess team performance. The device and procedure facts are
fixed teaching fixtures, not a real-patient plan or device order.
