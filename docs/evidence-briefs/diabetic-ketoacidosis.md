# Evidence brief: Diabetic ketoacidosis

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate adult emergency-department case rehearses recognizing moderate DKA, beginning
fluid and serial monitoring intent, correcting potassium before insulin, continuing insulin with
dextrose after glucose improves before ketoacidosis, confirming resolution with plasma ketone and
acid-base criteria, and transitioning with overlap and precipitant correction.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2024 international consensus defines DKA through diabetes or glucose at least 200 mg/dL,
  ketonemia, and metabolic acidosis. The fixed glucose 486 mg/dL, β-hydroxybutyrate 5.4 mmol/L,
  pH 7.16, and bicarbonate 11 mmol/L support the authored moderate classification.
- Initial management integrates isotonic fluid, insulin, potassium, frequent glucose checks, and
  4-hour electrolyte, renal, plasma-ketone, and venous-pH panels while addressing the precipitant.
- When potassium is below 3.5 mmol/L, replacement begins and insulin waits until potassium exceeds
  3.5 mmol/L. The fixed repeat is 3.7 mmol/L; no replacement dose or infusion rate is taught.
- When glucose falls below 250 mg/dL before ketoacidosis resolves, dextrose joins reduced,
  protocol-guided insulin so ketone clearance can continue. The authored interval panel remains
  unresolved at β-hydroxybutyrate 2.2 mmol/L, pH 7.24, and bicarbonate 15 mmol/L.
- Resolution requires plasma ketone below 0.6 mmol/L plus venous pH at least 7.3 or bicarbonate at
  least 18 mmol/L; glucose should ideally be below 200 mg/dL. Anion gap and urine ketones should not
  be used alone. The fixed final panel meets these criteria.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine the patient; acquire or interpret specimens; calculate corrected sodium,
osmolality, deficits, or doses; select or deliver fluids, potassium, insulin, or dextrose; operate
pumps; model kinetics or urine output; manage mixed or special-population crises, complications,
precipitant treatment, subcutaneous-insulin dosing, device testing, education quality, disposition,
recurrence, prognosis, or outcome.
