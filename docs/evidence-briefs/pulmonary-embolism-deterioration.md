# Pulmonary embolism with deterioration evidence brief

## Source floor

- The 2026 multisociety AHA/ACC guideline is the primary boundary. Its acute PE categories distinguish symptomatic disease with RV dysfunction and biomarkers (Category C3), incipient cardiopulmonary failure (D), and persistent hypotension with cardiogenic shock (E1). The respiratory modifier includes respiratory rate at least 30/min.
- The guideline recommends immediate anticoagulation when no absolute contraindication exists, recommends pulmonary embolism response teams for timely care, and considers advanced reperfusion therapies reasonable for Category E1.
- Deep sedation and mechanical ventilation should be avoided when possible in Category C-E acute PE because loss of compensatory sympathetic tone and positive pressure can precipitate hemodynamic collapse.

## Authored vignette

The fictional patient has CTPA-confirmed bilateral main/lobar acute PE, RV enlargement and dysfunction, elevated troponin and BNP, respiratory rate 30/min, SpO2 90%, and initially preserved pressure and perfusion. After bounded oxygen and anticoagulation intents, a deliberate serial reassessment reveals persistent hypotension and hypoperfusion compatible with Category E1. The learner can activate a multidisciplinary PE response and record urgent reperfusion-strategy intent.

## Exclusions

The lab does not acquire or interpret real ECG, CT, echocardiography, ultrasound, or laboratory data. It does not calculate a complete clinical score; select an anticoagulant, dose, or monitoring plan; adjudicate bleeding or reperfusion contraindications; teach airway, thrombolysis, thrombectomy, or embolectomy technique; choose among reperfusion procedures; model transport, transfer, disposition, complications, or outcome.
