# Local anesthetic toxicity: quiet warnings can turn fast

## Teaching claim

This lab rehearses delayed local-anesthetic systemic toxicity after a continuous ropivacaine
catheter. It connects a long exposure clock, short subjective prodrome, seizure, respiratory
depression, conduction change and shock while teaching that the classic CNS-then-cardiac sequence is
not required. It is distinct from the Anesthesia LAST lab, which owns immediate injection toxicity
and explicit weight-banded lipid and reduced-epinephrine controls.

## Current source floor

- The 2018 third ASRA practice advisory describes substantial variability in LAST presentation,
  including simultaneous CNS and cardiac toxicity or cardiac toxicity without a prodrome. It notes
  delayed presentations beyond 15 minutes and occasionally beyond an hour, and identifies continuous
  infusions after 1–4 days as a particular risk. PMID 29356773; DOI
  `10.1097/AAP.0000000000000720`; Tables 3–4.
- The 2025 AHA special-circumstances guideline states that isolated CNS toxicity can rapidly progress
  to cardiovascular toxicity and supports concurrent prevention of hypoxia and acidemia,
  benzodiazepine seizure care, early 20% lipid-emulsion rescue, and refractory ECLS assessment. The
  Toxicology controls expose no dose, formula, device setting, rhythm intervention, or procedure.

## Fixed fixture

A 62-year-old, 52 kg woman has a clinician-placed continuous ropivacaine peripheral nerve catheter
38 hours after ankle fixation. A supplied 12-minute trajectory begins with metallic taste, bilateral
tinnitus, perioral tingling, dysarthria and agitation, then a 70-second generalized convulsion and
drowsiness. HR is 48/min with ventricular ectopy, BP 82/46 mmHg, RR 10/min with shallow breathing,
room-air SpO2 92%, and T 36.7°C. Supplied ECG has QRS 124 ms and intermittent ectopy; pH is 7.28,
PCO2 48 mmHg, bicarbonate 22 mmol/L, lactate 4.1 mmol/L, potassium 3.7 mmol/L, magnesium 1.8 mg/dL,
glucose 106 mg/dL, and creatinine 0.8 mg/dL. Source-delivery details and competing causes stay open.

After elapsed time, a strict qualified-team report supplies source cessation, oxygenation and
ventilation, seizure care, 20% lipid emulsion and modified resuscitation; sinus rhythm 76/min,
BP 104/64 mmHg, RR 16/min, SpO2 98% on supplied support, QRS 104 ms, arousable drowsiness, and no
recurrent visible seizure. Treatment effect, source completeness, durable seizure, rhythm or
perfusion control, recovery, lipid safety, disposition, and outcome remain open.

## Deliberate boundaries

The learner records reconciliation, coupled-pattern recognition, qualified ownership, supplied-
evidence review, bounded rescue intent, strict reassessment, and active-risk handoff only. No real
history, examination, monitoring or test interpretation, catheter or pump handling, diagnosis,
oxygen, ventilation, seizure treatment, lipid dose, cardiac treatment, ECLS, procedure, treatment
effect, disposition, prognosis, or outcome is exposed.

## Review need

Clinical reviewers should verify the delayed clock, prodromal and CNS-cardiac fixture, acid-base and
ECG values, rescue boundary, later state, and distinction from immediate injection LAST, epilepsy,
stroke, infection, metabolic causes, and other sodium-channel blockers.
