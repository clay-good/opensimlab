# Unstable narrow-complex tachycardia evidence brief

## Source floor

- The 2025 AHA adult tachyarrhythmia-with-a-pulse algorithm defines instability through hypotension, acutely altered mental status, shock signs, ischemic chest discomfort, or acute heart failure and directs synchronized cardioversion.
- Initial support includes airway and breathing assessment, oxygen only when hypoxemic, continuous rhythm/blood-pressure/oximetry monitoring, IV access, and a 12-lead ECG when available.
- Sedation should be used whenever feasible, but prompt cardioversion remains the priority. Current guidance defers energy to the specific device recommendation and advises maximum energy when the recommendation is unknown.

## Authored vignette

The fictional patient has a fixed regular narrow-complex tachycardia at 188/min, BP 76/48 mmHg, drowsiness, ischemic chest discomfort, cool mottled extremities, and SpO2 94%. A bounded synchronized-cardioversion intent produces an authored sinus-rhythm response at 92/min with BP 118/72 mmHg and improved mentation and perfusion.

## Exclusions

The lab does not acquire a 12-lead ECG, diagnose the tachycardia mechanism, operate a defibrillator, select energy, verify synchronization markers, perform cardioversion, choose or deliver sedation, adjudicate airway risk, model adenosine or other drugs, treat refractory or recurrent tachycardia, investigate the cause, or predict disposition or outcome.
