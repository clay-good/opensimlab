# Hypernatremic dehydration: circulation, then water balance

This unsigned preview concerns known arginine vasopressin deficiency (AVP-D, formerly central
diabetes insipidus), not a new diagnosis, renal AVP resistance, or a generic sodium calculator.
Content 0.1.1 is a bounded rehearsal, not a completed clinical product. Engineering evidence
does not establish clinical review.

## Source boundary

Consulted August 27, 2026; source review due February 27, 2027, not a clinical sign-off.

- [Society for Endocrinology inpatient guidance, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1530/EC-18-0154),
  G10–G11, decompensated CDI §§1–6: prioritize circulation and fluid replacement; low urine output
  during hypovolemia does not exclude known AVP-D. Reassess urine and sodium when considering
  qualified desmopressin. Combined antidiuresis and water replacement require close surveillance.
- [Current SfE AVP-D guidance page](https://www.endocrinology.org/clinical-practice/clinical-guidance/arginine-vasopressin-deficiency-diabetes-insipidus/),
  “For patients who are seriously unwell” and “Updated Resources 2024”: the page continues to
  link the 2018 guidance and emphasizes urgent monitored care and fluid-first management.
- [Tomkins et al., JCEM 2022](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgac381), inpatient management:
  medication omission and inability to replace water are important preventable hazards.
- The [August 2026 joint-guideline presentation abstract](https://doi.org/10.1093/ejendo/lvag096.042)
  describes the ESE/Endocrine Society guideline as forthcoming, not published guidance. It does
  not replace the above sources or provide a new dosing recommendation here.

These sources inform priorities, not the fictional patient, branch values, response clocks, or
simulation efficacy. No source table, diagram, prescribing algorithm, or long quotation is copied.
The selected pathway is shared across US and GB profiles, not a claim of universal regional
prescribing. Unknown duration is not assumed to be acute sodium loading. No replacement dose,
rate, fluid-deficit calculation, automatic redosing, or brain-injury prediction is provided.

## Authored contract

A fictional 67-year-old man, 72 kg, has established isolated AVP-D. Two scheduled prescribed
desmopressin doses were omitted during an admission with restricted access to drinking water.
The exact duration of hypernatremia is unknown. Supplied sodium is 162 mmol/L, potassium
3.8 mmol/L, and creatinine 1.6 mg/dL. Initial BP 90/54 mmHg, MAP 66 mmHg, HR 112/min, RR
20/min, SpO2 98%, and temperature 37.1°C accompany low urine output of 60 mL/hour. He is awake,
thirsty, and tired. The known diagnosis is supplied; the lesson does not test diagnostic accuracy.

Qualified volume restoration is available immediately without an administrative or laboratory
gate. An authored 15-minute checkpoint restores circulation and unmasks large dilute urine losses.
Blood pressure and pulse show this response live; sodium and urine findings require reassessment.
Water replacement and desmopressin become independently available after the circulation response,
without a fresh-test, monitoring-button, support, or context-review prerequisite. Tailored water replacement and
qualified restoration of desmopressin address different problems; neither a better blood pressure
nor less urine proves that the water deficit or sodium disturbance has resolved. The later
combined-care checkpoint remains hypernatremic and transfers continuing care, not discharge.

Every checkpoint is authored, not a safe waiting interval or predicted drug/fluid kinetics.
Learners can reassess before a checkpoint. Unrequested sodium and urine findings must remain
private; old results remain explicitly historical. Rapid normalization and blanket medication
withholding are retained as learning evidence even after a successful recovery path.

| Authored checkpoint | Fictional findings and limits |
| --- | --- |
| Initial, before requested reassessment | Supplied sodium 162 mmol/L and urine output 60 mL/hour; urine osmolality 100 mOsm/kg is not supplied until requested. |
| 15 minutes after volume restoration | BP 110/68 mmHg, MAP 82 mmHg, HR 92/min; private sodium at least 163 mmol/L, urine output 450 mL/hour, urine osmolality 95 mOsm/kg. |
| 30 minutes without volume restoration | BP 80/46 mmHg, MAP 57 mmHg, HR 124/min; private sodium 164 mmol/L. Delay remains recorded after recovery. |
| 30 minutes after desmopressin | Private urine output 80 mL/hour and urine osmolality 500 mOsm/kg; sodium has not normalized. No automatic redose. |
| 120 minutes after volume, without either water or desmopressin | Private sodium 165 mmol/L; neither hidden evolution nor its peak is exported as observed. |
| 120 minutes after both water and desmopressin start | Sodium decreases by 1 mmol/L from the combined-care starting value, with a floor of 162: prompt 163→162, delayed 164→163, late 165→164. Fresh reassessment is still required. |
| Teaching stops | No volume restoration by 60 minutes or an unfinished rehearsal at 300 minutes ends with instructor takeover, not a predicted clinical outcome. |

Water alone prevents the authored further rise; desmopressin alone reduces urine loss. Neither
resolves the deficit. Final-only reassessment can support qualified handoff but does not earn the
earlier observation objective. Successful handoff preserves current hypernatremia and continuing
treatment ownership. All findings and waits are authored contrasts, not clinical targets or kinetics.

The nine-decision example uses ordinary recorded actions and pauses for reading. Expert, error,
recovery, and no-action fixtures separate observation from treatment and retain mistakes in debrief.
The shared report dialog accepts at most 160 characters; optional context contains only explicit
observed-state scalars and uniquely matched action outcomes, never latent laboratory findings,
free-form engine messages, or a fabricated normal capnogram.

Content 0.1.1 corrects an arbitrary scoring cutoff: volume restoration and fresh observed response
earn circulation credit on either side of the authored 30-minute deterioration. The debrief reports
the actual start time and retains deterioration and delay, rather than converting 0.1 second into
a clinical pass/fail decision. Normalization and withholding mistakes remain distinct evidence.
The patient trajectory and teaching clocks are unchanged; the original report identity is retained.
