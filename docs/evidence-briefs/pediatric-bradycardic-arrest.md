# Pediatric bradycardic arrest transition evidence brief

## Decision boundary

This lab begins after qualified effective ventilation with oxygen is already established. A child
still has sinus bradycardia below 60/min with a central pulse and severe cardiopulmonary compromise.
The learner reconciles supplied support and whole-child evidence, recognizes the pediatric
threshold, activates qualified CPR and resuscitation ownership before pulse loss, then reviews open
causes and the arrest boundary. A strictly elapsed qualified checkpoint reports pulse loss with
organized electrical activity, requiring the nonshockable pathway and an active-resuscitation
handoff without a fictional recovery.

The lab does not repeat the adult unstable-bradycardia lesson's atropine intent, the adult PEA
lesson's learner compression and epinephrine actions, or the persistent-VF lesson's shock and energy
controls. It begins after the generic pediatric respiratory-distress lesson appropriately ends at
earlier rescue escalation. The pediatric bridge from compromised bradycardia with a pulse to PEA is
the only new rehearsal value.

## Fixed teaching facts

- A previously well 6-year-old girl is 20 kg and 115 cm. Several hours of worsening breathing and
  fatigue are reported, but the exact cause remains open.
- She is unresponsive without purposeful movement and has no spontaneous effective breathing.
- A fixed qualified report documents a patent airway, assisted positive-pressure ventilation with
  oxygen, equal visible bilateral chest rise, a continuous capnogram with EtCO₂ 36 mmHg, and SpO₂
  improving from 79% to 95%. These combined authored findings establish the effective-support
  premise; SpO₂ alone does not.
- Despite that support, fixed sinus bradycardia and a central pulse persist at 52/min. BP is 64/36
  mmHg (MAP 45), temperature is 36.8°C, skin is pale, cool, and mottled, refill is 5 seconds, the
  central pulse is weak, and no peripheral pulse is reported. Pulse loss has not yet occurred.
- No trauma, abrupt choking, wheeze, stridor, urticaria, focal unilateral air-entry loss, known
  congenital heart disease, known toxin or medication exposure, hypothermia, or seizure is
  authored. These snapshots do not exclude hypoxia, airway or lung disease, toxins, metabolic or
  neurological disease, heart block, or another dangerous cause.
- At a strictly elapsed qualified checkpoint, organized electrical activity is 46/min with no
  central pulse, a nonpulsatile pleth, unobtainable NIBP, persistent
  unresponsiveness, and continued qualified assisted ventilation. This fixed report establishes
  authored PEA and pulse loss, not persistent bradycardia with a pulse.
- Active nonshockable resuscitation continues at handoff. No ROSC, termination, cause, post-arrest
  state, prognosis, or outcome is reported.

All values, negative findings, timing, and the pulse-loss trajectory are fictional face-valid
fixtures. The sources support the qualitative decision sequence, not these exact values or a
predicted response to care.

## Source synthesis

The 2025 AHA/AAP Pediatric Advanced Life Support guideline recommends starting CPR in infants and
children with HR below 60/min and cardiopulmonary compromise despite effective ventilation with
oxygen. Its synopsis warns that compromised bradycardia with a palpable pulse may precede pulseless
arrest. This lab requires the full combined condition and never turns HR below 60/min alone into a
universal CPR trigger.

The 2025 Pediatric Bradycardia With a Pulse Algorithm places airway, oxygenation, ventilation,
monitoring, and pulse assessment before that CPR branch, keeps possible causes active, and directs a
transition to the Pediatric Cardiac Arrest Algorithm when a later pulse check is negative. The
simulator supplies all examination and support evidence and exposes ownership rather than physical
CPR, ventilation, medication, pacing, or device controls.

The 2025 Pediatric Cardiac Arrest Algorithm places PEA on the nonshockable pathway with ongoing CPR,
qualified medication and airway care, rhythm reassessment, and reversible-cause treatment. This lab
does not expose those psychomotor, dose, or treatment actions and does not invent ROSC.

## Explicit nonclaims

- No child is examined or palpated, and no pulse, airway, ventilation, perfusion, monitor, ECG,
  capnogram, CPR quality, treatment response, or cause is assessed by the learner.
- No oxygen, ventilation, compression, access, drug, concentration, dose, route, flush, pacing,
  shock, energy, device operation, procedure, cause-specific treatment, resuscitation, or post-arrest
  care is selected or delivered.
- The fixed negative snapshots do not exclude a dangerous cause.
- HR below 60/min without the persistent compromise and effective-ventilation context does not
  establish this pathway; improving SpO₂ alone does not prove effective ventilation.
- The fixed PEA transition is not a modeled treatment effect or individual prediction.
- No termination, disposition, ROSC, recovery, prognosis, or outcome is selected, predicted, or
  reported.
