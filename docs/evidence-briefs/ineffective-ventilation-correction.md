# Ineffective newborn ventilation correction

Status: source-checked authoring brief; clinical review remains unsigned.
Checked: August 26, 2026.
Scope: recognition and qualified corrective-response review, not mask-ventilation or airway-procedure instruction.

The 2025 AHA/AAP guideline makes increasing heart rate the primary indicator of effective newborn ventilation, with chest movement a secondary sign. Mask leak and airway obstruction are common during face-mask ventilation. If heart rate remains below 100/min and does not increase, qualified teams should correct ventilation and may need an alternative airway. Chest compressions belong only after heart rate remains below 60/min despite adequate ventilation after corrective steps, preferably through an alternative airway.

The authored case supplies 30 seconds of qualified face-mask ventilation without visible chest movement or heart-rate rise, with HR 82→78/min. A fixed report after qualified leak correction supplies visible chest movement, HR 118/min, and emerging irregular respirations. These values demonstrate a bounded response, not learner technical performance, durable transition, or outcome.
