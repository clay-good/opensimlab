# Upper GI hemorrhage evidence brief

## Scope

A fixed ICU recurrence of nonvariceal peptic-ulcer bleeding after prior endoscopic hemostasis. The
lesson covers recognition, resuscitation coordination, repeat-endoscopy escalation, failure
pathways, and serial reassessment. It does not cover first-presentation risk scoring, variceal
bleeding, medication dosing, blood-product delivery, airway management, procedures, or outcome.

## Evidence translated into the lesson

- The ACG guideline suggests a restrictive red-cell transfusion threshold of 7 g/dL for hospitalized
  upper-GI bleeding, endoscopy within 24 hours after presentation, repeat endoscopy for recurrent
  ulcer bleeding, and transcatheter embolization when endoscopic therapy fails. The lesson uses an
  authored 6.8 g/dL result but explicitly keeps hemodynamics, active bleeding, comorbidity, and local
  protocol in the decision rather than presenting 7 g/dL as universal.
- ESGE recommends hemodynamic resuscitation before early endoscopy, repeat endoscopic treatment for
  clinical recurrent peptic-ulcer hemorrhage, transcatheter angiographic embolization after a failed
  second endoscopic attempt, and surgery when embolization is unavailable or fails. The case orders
  resuscitation and hemostasis escalation concurrently rather than implying one must finish first.
- ESICM shock guidance supports serial tissue-perfusion assessment and dynamic response over isolated
  pressure values. The fixed post-bridge panel therefore improves immediate pressure and perfusion
  while leaving hemostasis, lactate, urine output, and organ trajectory unresolved.

## Boundaries and disputed practice

Transfusion thresholds, airway strategy, timing, drug regimens, and procedural escalation vary with
active bleeding, cardiovascular disease, prior treatment, local resources, and suspected source.
The case is deliberately nonvariceal and does not transfer to cirrhosis or suspected variceal
hemorrhage. It records intents only and cannot assess examination, resuscitation, endoscopy,
embolization, surgery, teamwork, or individual outcomes. No copyrighted table or algorithm is
reproduced.
