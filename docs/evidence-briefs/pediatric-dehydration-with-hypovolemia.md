# Pediatric dehydration with compensated hypovolemia

Status: draft teaching content; clinical review unsigned. Consulted August 25, 2026. Review by August 25, 2027, or sooner if the cited WHO or NICE guidance changes.

## What this lab teaches

This fixed 2-year-old case asks the learner to connect acute watery losses, reduced intake and urine, same-scale weight history, and multiple hydration signs; distinguish compensated volume depletion from current shock; and keep qualified rehydration ownership and ongoing-loss safety review active in parallel. A later authored report shows partial improvement while loss, intake, output, tolerance, and recurrence work remain open.

The learner does not examine or weigh the child, calculate a dehydration percentage, fluid deficit, or maintenance requirement, acquire or interpret tests, diagnose gastroenteritis or another cause, choose or deliver oral or intravenous fluid, a solution, route, volume, rate, access, electrolyte, drug, feeding plan, device, procedure, or treatment, or determine disposition or outcome.

## Evidence boundary

The 2024 WHO guideline supports low-osmolarity oral rehydration solution for children with acute watery diarrhoea and dehydration. NICE CG84 supports whole-child assessment across responsiveness, urine, skin color and temperature, eyes, mucosa, heart and respiratory rates, pulses, refill, turgor, and blood pressure; oral rehydration in small frequent amounts; continued breastfeeding with phase-appropriate feeding; regular clinical reassessment; and conditional route escalation for shock, deterioration despite oral therapy, or persistent vomiting. NICE NG29 supplies the general hospital boundary for isotonic intravenous resuscitation and immediate reassessment when that escalation is required.

These sources do not turn one sign or one weight change into a universal severity grade. WHO does not establish NICE's older under-5 operational volume regimen, and NICE's general IV-fluid and gastroenteritis documents have differed in bolus wording across updates. This lab therefore exposes no product, composition, percentage, bolus, route, volume, rate, deficit, maintenance formula, electrolyte correction, or discharge rule.

## Authored values and non-claims

The child, losses, same-scale weight history, exact vital signs, hydration signs, current no-shock classification, qualified-care record, and minute-60 trajectory are fictional face-valid fixtures. The 0.6 kg weight change is one authored clue, not a measured 4.8% intravascular deficit or a universal dehydration grade. The later changes do not prove oral-rehydration causality, complete deficit correction, durable recovery, discharge readiness, recurrence risk, or outcome.
