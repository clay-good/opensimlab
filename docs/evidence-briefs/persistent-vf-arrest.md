# Evidence brief: persistent VF arrest

## Intended rehearsal

This advanced emergency-department case reuses the shared bounded cardiac-arrest engine to rehearse
one third shockable-rhythm cycle after 2 unsuccessful biphasic shocks: resume compression intent,
record 1 mg IV/IO epinephrine while compressions are active, use the declared device setting, deliver
one modeled shock, and recognize the authored return of spontaneous circulation.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2025 AHA adult cardiac-arrest algorithm identifies VF/pulseless VT as shockable and emphasizes
  continuous high-quality CPR with minimal interruptions, oxygenation/ventilation, monitor-defibrillator
  use, vascular access, epinephrine after initial shocks fail, rhythm checks, and reversible causes.
- Epinephrine is 1 mg IV/IO every 3–5 minutes. This case accepts one fixed dose during the third cycle.
- Biphasic defibrillation follows the manufacturer recommendation, commonly 120–200 J initially; if
  unknown, maximum available is used. The fictional device declares 200 J. This is not a universal
  energy prescription.
- Amiodarone or lidocaine may be considered for VF/pVT unresponsive to defibrillation, but this case
  stops before that branch.

## Explicit exclusions

Screen controls record intent and cannot assess compression depth, recoil, rate, interruptions,
ventilation, airway skill, pad placement, synchronization state, charging, clearing, shock safety,
vascular access, medication delivery, reversible-cause treatment, antiarrhythmics, team performance,
individual conversion probability, or post-cardiac-arrest care.
