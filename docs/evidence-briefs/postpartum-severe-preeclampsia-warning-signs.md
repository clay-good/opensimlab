# Evidence brief: postpartum severe preeclampsia warning signs

## Decision this lesson rehearses

Recognize persistent severe postpartum hypertension as an immediate treatment emergency and a
supplied presumptive preeclampsia-with-severe-features pattern. Listen to the patient's neurologic
and upper-abdominal warning symptoms, activate qualified protocol-based pressure treatment as soon
as possible and within 60 minutes of the first severe reading, and review dangerous obstetric and
non-obstetric alternatives in parallel. Preserve unresolved seizure, stroke, pulmonary,
hematologic, renal, hepatic, postpartum and longer-term risk through reassessment and handoff. This
is deliberately distinct from the Anesthesia lab that confirms and treats severe pressure
immediately before an already-decided urgent delivery.

## Source basis

1. American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists. *Gestational Hypertension and
   Preeclampsia: ACOG Practice Bulletin No. 222*. Obstet Gynecol 2020;135:e237-60; reaffirmed
   2026. Consulted August 26, 2026. This supports severe-range pressure and severe-feature context,
   including neurologic symptoms, thrombocytopenia, renal or hepatic dysfunction, pulmonary edema,
   and the emergency treatment and seizure-prophylaxis boundary.
2. Alliance for Innovation on Maternal Health. *Severe Hypertension in Pregnancy Patient Safety
   Bundle*. Revised 2022; current official page consulted August 26, 2026. This supports identifying
   current or recent pregnancy across care settings, accurate pressure assessment, listening to and
   investigating reported symptoms, standard laboratory review, escalation and consultation,
   multidisciplinary planning, and respectful, supportive, patient-centered care.
3. Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine. *Special Statement: Quality metric for timely postpartum
   follow-up after severe hypertension*. 2022; reaffirmed 2025. Consulted August 26, 2026. This
   provides general context that severe postpartum hypertension may worsen and that prompt follow-up
   matters after a childbirth hospitalization. It does not set follow-up requirements or select
   disposition for this de novo day-6 presentation.

## Implemented teaching bounds

- The opening report is authored, not measured by the learner: a 32-year-old woman 6 days after a
  term vaginal birth has 4 hours of severe headache, flashing visual spots and persistent right
  upper-quadrant or epigastric pain. Qualified staff report pressures of 178/114 and 174/112 mmHg 15
  minutes apart, HR 96/min, RR 20/min, pulse-coherent room-air SpO2 97%, T 36.7°C, platelets
  88 × 10^9/L, creatinine 1.2 mg/dL from 0.7, and AST 96 U/L and ALT 88 U/L, both more than twice
  the supplied local upper limit of normal. Urine protein-to-creatinine ratio and hemolysis
  evaluation are pending. She is alert and conversant. No seizure, focal deficit, chest pain, dyspnea, hypoxemia,
  fever, major bleeding or cardiac arrest is authored.
- The fixed later qualified-team report, authored as 30 minutes after activation, is also authored:
  BP 152/98 mmHg, HR 92/min,
  RR 18/min, SpO2 98% on room air and T 36.7°C, with improved but persistent headache and visual
  spots, no reported seizure, repeat laboratory results pending, and neurologic, pulmonary,
  hematologic, renal, hepatic and longer-term risk unresolved.
- The six learner controls only record reconciliation, recognition, qualified ownership, supplied
  immediate qualified protocol activation, supplied evidence review, fixed later review and
  handoff. The pressure change is
  not individualized physiology or proof of a product, dose, route, target or treatment effect.
  The single later pressure is no longer severe-range but remains hypertensive and does not prove
  durable control or resolution.

## Explicit exclusions

The browser does not take history; measure or repeat pressure; select cuff or position; examine;
assess reflexes, clonus, edema, volume, lungs, fundus, abdomen or neurologic state; acquire or
interpret urine, blood, ECG, imaging or another test; calculate a score or ratio; diagnose or
exclude preeclampsia, HELLP, eclampsia, PRES, stroke, venous thrombosis, hemorrhage, infection or
another cause; select or deliver an antihypertensive, magnesium, oxygen, fluid, diuretic, analgesic,
antiseizure drug or other treatment; choose a product, dose, rate, target, route or access; manage
an airway or seizure; perform a procedure; separate a mother and newborn; decide feeding, transfer,
admission, discharge, follow-up or future-pregnancy care; or predict treatment effect, organ
recovery, recurrence, cardiovascular risk, fertility, maternal outcome or newborn outcome.
