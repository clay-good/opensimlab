# Evidence brief: mucus plugging

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate adult ICU case rehearses supporting oxygenation, recognizing convergent retained-
secretion indicators, recording safe as-needed artificial-airway clearance intent, proving the
response, and escalating a persistent focal abnormality without making bronchoscopy routine.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2022 AARC guideline identifies breath sounds, visible artificial-airway secretions, and a
  sawtooth ventilator flow waveform as the best adult indicators for artificial-airway suctioning.
- Adult patients should be preoxygenated. Routine saline instillation should generally be avoided,
  suction should be as brief as possible, and shallow suction is preferred before deeper technique.
- Suctioning can cause desaturation, bleeding, hemodynamic change, dysrhythmia, mucosal injury, and
  infection. The lesson therefore records intent and response rather than simulating technique.
- Routine bronchoscopy for secretion removal is not recommended. Persistent focal physiology still
  warrants experienced assessment and indicated imaging, with alternate airway and lung causes open.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine the patient, inspect equipment, acquire graphics or mechanics,
preoxygenate, suction, choose a catheter or pressure, remove secretions, image, perform bronchoscopy,
diagnose mucus plugging or collapse, program a ventilator, deliver medication, or assess airway,
respiratory-therapy, procedural, disposition, or outcome competence. Findings are authored panels.
