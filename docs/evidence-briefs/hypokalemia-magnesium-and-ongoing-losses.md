# Hypokalemia: replace, reassess, and address losses

Content version 0.1.0; renal/electrolyte preview. Sources checked August 27, 2026.
Independent clinical approval remains pending. Educational simulation, not patient care.

## Clinical boundary

Severe hypokalemia with supplied magnesium deficiency requires qualified replacement, appropriate
monitoring, and review of continuing losses and contributors. Potassium treatment must not wait
for magnesium normalization or an administrative acknowledgment. The
[NHS SPS potassium guidance](https://sps.nhs.uk/articles/hypokalaemia/) supports monitored hospital
care for severe deficits, individualized delivery safeguards, renal assessment, and serial review.
The [NHS SPS magnesium guidance](https://sps.nhs.uk/articles/treating-acute-hypomagnesaemia-in-adults/)
supports concurrent electrolyte assessment and distinguishes serum improvement from store repletion.

The [2021 ESC collaborative review](https://academic.oup.com/ehjcvp/article/7/6/557/6270921)
describes magnesium deficiency as a contributor to difficult potassium correction and qualitative
ECG changes. It is a review, not a formal ESC guideline. The
[RCUK 2025 special-circumstances guidance](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/special-circumstances-guidelines)
supports appropriate potassium and magnesium care. Cardiac-arrest regimens are not transferred to
this perfusing adult. No product, dose, rate, access, route, or universal fluid policy is selected.
