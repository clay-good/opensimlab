# ICU handoff with hidden deterioration evidence brief

## Scope

A fixed adult shift-change handoff whose verbal label says “stable” while dated bedside trends show
worsening septic shock: increasing reported vasoactive support, falling pressure and end-tidal
carbon dioxide, rising heart rate and lactate, prolonged refill, and declining urine output with
source control still pending. The lesson covers receiver readiness, structured content, active
cross-check, illness-severity correction, escalation and contingencies, receiver synthesis, explicit
ownership, and immediate reassessment. It does not assess communication skill or perform care.

## Evidence translated into the lesson

- AHRQ TeamSTEPPS treats handoff as transfer of information, authority, and responsibility. Its
  I-PASS structure makes illness severity, patient summary, action list, situation awareness and
  contingency planning, and receiver synthesis explicit.
- The Joint Commission recommends standardized critical content, dated vital signs and laboratory
  results, an opportunity for questions, receiver scrutiny, and clear transfer of responsibility.
  The lesson therefore refuses acceptance before the receiver cross-checks the current bedside state.
- In the multicenter pediatric I-PASS study, bundle implementation was associated with fewer medical
  errors and preventable adverse events without longer oral handoffs. This was a bundled before-and-
  after intervention, not proof that one mnemonic or click prevents harm.
- A later 32-hospital effectiveness-implementation study across diverse settings was associated with
  fewer reported handoff-related adverse events and better inclusion of key verbal and written
  elements. The lesson preserves local adaptation and does not present I-PASS as a universal script.

## Boundaries and disputed practice

This is not a validated handoff instrument or a communication-performance assessment. It does not
measure accuracy outside fixed text, voice, language, nonverbal behavior, interruption handling,
hierarchy, workload, staffing, bedside examination, documentation, EHR use, patient or family
participation, real acknowledgment, escalation, treatment, source control, disposition, or outcome.
No copyrighted handoff template is reproduced.
