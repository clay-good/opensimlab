# Evidence brief: PEA arrest

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate emergency-department case rehearses recognizing organized electrical activity
without a mechanical pulse, starting modeled compression intent, recording one adult 1 mg IV/IO
epinephrine action during compressions, and keeping PEA on the nonshockable pathway while reversible
causes remain in view. It intentionally does not invent a cause-specific treatment or ROSC.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2025 AHA adult cardiac-arrest algorithm places pulseless electrical activity with asystole on
  the nonshockable branch. CPR, bag-mask ventilation with oxygen, monitor-defibrillator attachment,
  IV/IO access, epinephrine as soon as feasible, repeated 2-minute rhythm cycles, and treatment of
  reversible causes proceed in parallel.
- Adult cardiac-arrest epinephrine is 1 mg IV/IO every 3–5 minutes. This bounded first cycle accepts
  one fixed dose and does not model pharmacokinetics or individual outcome.
- Defibrillation is not indicated for PEA. The focused interface therefore names the nonshockable
  branch and does not offer energy selection.
- Potential reversible causes include hypovolemia, hypoxia, acidosis, potassium disturbance,
  hypothermia, tension pneumothorax, tamponade, toxins, and pulmonary or coronary thrombosis. No
  single cause is diagnosed or treated in this vignette.

## Explicit exclusions

Screen controls do not assess compression depth, recoil, rate, pauses, ventilation, airway skill,
pulse-check technique, vascular access, medication preparation or delivery, diagnostic acquisition,
cause-specific treatment, repeated cycles, team performance, termination decisions, ROSC, or
post-cardiac-arrest care.
