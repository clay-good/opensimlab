# Critical-care status epilepticus evidence brief

## Scope and distinction

This advanced ICU case begins after the emergency lab ends. An adequate benzodiazepine and urgent
antiseizure load are reported, visible convulsions have stopped, consciousness has not returned,
and fixed continuous EEG still reports seizures. The lesson covers refractory-status recognition,
continuous EEG and anesthetic escalation, systemic guardrails, cause review, and reassessment. It
does not repeat first-line dosing or simulate EEG interpretation, medication delivery, or airway care.

## Evidence translated into the lesson

- ENLS 5.0 defines refractory status as ongoing seizures despite emergent and urgent control therapy.
  It recommends intubation, continuous-infusion anesthetic therapy, and continuous EEG in this state.
  The case starts at that boundary and records a pathway only; it does not select an agent or dose.
- The ACNS continuous-EEG consensus recommends cEEG for diagnosing nonconvulsive seizures and status
  and assessing treatment efficacy. The case makes absent movement explicitly insufficient and uses
  an authored cEEG report rather than asking the browser to teach EEG interpretation.
- ACNS 2021 terminology standardizes critical-care EEG descriptions but does not make a screen
  button diagnostic. The authored report is fixed, and treatment target and duration remain expert
  decisions rather than universal burst-suppression rules.

## Boundaries and disputed practice

Continuous-anesthetic choice, dosing, EEG target, treatment duration, weaning, and cause-directed
therapy vary by patient, etiology, organ function, local protocol, and expert interpretation. The
case does not claim one agent, burst suppression, or fixed seizure-free duration is universally
correct. Controls cannot assess examination, EEG, medication preparation or delivery, ventilation,
hemodynamic support, cause diagnosis, procedures, teamwork, prognosis, or outcome. No copyrighted
algorithm or dosing table is reproduced.
