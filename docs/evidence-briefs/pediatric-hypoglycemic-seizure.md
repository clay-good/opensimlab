# Pediatric hypoglycemic seizure evidence brief

## Decision boundary

This lab rehearses whole-child reconciliation, recognition of supplied severe hypoglycemia after a brief stopped convulsion, immediate qualified rescue ownership, cause and recurrence-risk review, serial reassessment, and active-risk handoff. It does not teach a glucose product, concentration, dose, route, access, anticonvulsant, airway maneuver, feeding plan, diagnostic panel, or disposition recipe.

The lab is deliberately distinct from febrile seizure and pediatric status epilepticus. The child is afebrile, the convulsion stopped after about 90 seconds before the learner surface opened, and a qualified glucose result supplies the reversible metabolic finding. The learner does not time or suppress an ongoing seizure. No fever, meningism, trauma, focal deficit, known diabetes, or reported insulin or glucose-lowering medicine exposure is authored, but those snapshots do not close the cause.

## Fixed teaching facts

- A previously well 5-year-old boy is 18 kg and 110 cm. A witnessed generalized convulsion lasted about 90 seconds and stopped before the learner surface opened.
- He is drowsy, localizes, opens his eyes to voice, and is not safe to swallow. He breathes spontaneously. Temperature is 36.6°C, HR 132/min, RR 24/min, BP 98/62 mmHg (MAP 74), clean pulse-coherent room-air SpO₂ 99%, extremities are warm, pulses are normal volume, and capillary refill is 2 seconds.
- A qualified team supplies glucose 34 mg/dL. The learner does not obtain or interpret that result or diagnose the seizure cause.
- Immediate experienced pediatric rescue, airway-safety, monitoring, escalation, cause evaluation, and caregiver communication are available before the learner acts. The ownership action activates that work without exposing a treatment control.
- Cause and recurrence review remains broad because the child has no known diabetes or reported insulin or glucose-lowering medicine exposure. Intake or fasting, illness, ingestion or exposure, metabolic, endocrine, hepatic, and other causes remain open.
- The fixed minute-20 report after qualified rescue is awake, follows commands, uses age-appropriate speech, and remains tired, with no recurrent convulsion; temperature 36.7°C, HR 106/min, RR 20/min, BP 100/64 mmHg (MAP 76), room-air SpO₂ 99%, and supplied glucose 86 mg/dL.
- The later report does not prove causal treatment effect, durable euglycemia, complete neurological recovery, etiologic closure, recurrence exclusion, discharge readiness, or outcome.

All patient values and their timing are fictional face-valid fixtures. They are not predicted kinetics and must not be copied into clinical care.

## Source synthesis

The Pediatric Endocrine Society guideline addresses persistent or otherwise unexplained hypoglycemia in neonates, infants, and children without diabetes. It supports prompt recognition, attention to neuroglycopenic manifestations including seizures, and cause-directed evaluation when hypoglycemia is unexplained or recurrent. It does not validate this fictional acute trajectory, one universal diagnostic threshold, or the minute-20 response.

The Royal Children's Hospital Melbourne hypoglycaemia guideline supplies the closest operational whole-child context for a child without diabetes: severe or prolonged hypoglycaemia risks neurological injury, management should not wait for a diagnostic workup, enteral treatment depends on safe consciousness, and unexplained episodes warrant cause evaluation. The lab converts those principles only into qualified ownership and review controls.

The Royal Children's Hospital Melbourne acute-seizure guideline notes that most seizures resolve within 5 minutes and emphasizes reversible-cause review. That supports keeping this stopped 90-second event distinct from an ongoing status-epilepticus medication lesson.

The ISPAD 2022 hypoglycemia guideline provides corroborating severe-event terminology and recurrence, monitoring, and caregiver-safety context, but its population is children and adolescents with diabetes. This child has no known diabetes, so the citation does not establish his cause or validate a diabetes-specific rescue plan.

## Explicit nonclaims

- No real child is examined, monitored, tested, diagnosed, treated, or dispositioned.
- No glucose threshold is presented as universally diagnostic outside the supplied whole-child context.
- No glucose, dextrose, glucagon, carbohydrate, fluid, anticonvulsant, other drug, concentration, route, dose, rate, access, infusion, feeding plan, oxygen, device, airway maneuver, procedure, or treatment is selected or delivered.
- The fixed negative findings are snapshots, not permanent exclusions of infection, trauma, exposure, metabolic, endocrine, hepatic, neurological, safeguarding, or other causes.
- The fictional minute-20 change does not establish expected kinetics or causal treatment effect.
- Durable euglycemia, complete neurological recovery, etiology, recurrence risk, disposition, prognosis, and outcome remain open.
