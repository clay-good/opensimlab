# Postoperative handoff evidence brief

## What this slice teaches

One stable transfer-to-PACU vignette asks the learner to establish receiver readiness, share a
fixed patient/course block and current-state block, name unresolved risks with actions, timing,
ownership, and escalation, require receiver synthesis, and transfer responsibility only after
explicit acknowledgment.

## Evidence boundary

- AHRQ TeamSTEPPS defines handoff as transfer of information together with authority and
  responsibility. It emphasizes clear verbal communication, an opportunity to question and
  clarify, explicit timing and ownership, receiver synthesis, and acknowledgment before the sender
  relinquishes responsibility.
- The AHRQ I-PASS structure organizes illness severity, patient summary, action list, situation
  awareness and contingency planning, and synthesis by the receiver.
- The Joint Commission calls for a standardized critical-content process, opportunities to ask and
  respond to questions, receiver scrutiny, and clear exchange of information during transitions.

## Modeled behavior

- Receiver identity, monitoring connection, shared attention, and invitation for questions are one
  accepted readiness event.
- Patient/course and current state are separate fixed content blocks and may be shared in either
  order after readiness.
- Risks, required actions, timing, ownership, and escalation unlock only after both core blocks.
- Receiver synthesis unlocks next; explicit acceptance is the only event that changes the teaching
  state to transferred responsibility.

## Deliberate exclusions

This is not a validated handoff instrument or communication-performance assessment. It does not
measure voice, language, accuracy, omissions inside fixed text, nonverbal behavior, interruptions,
distraction, workload, staffing, hierarchy, bedside setup or examination, documentation, EHR use,
patient or family participation, real acknowledgment, clinical actions, deterioration, or outcome.
