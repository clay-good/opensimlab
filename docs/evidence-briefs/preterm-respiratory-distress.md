# Preterm respiratory distress

## Evidence boundary

The 2025 American Heart Association and American Academy of Pediatrics neonatal-resuscitation
guideline supports initial continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP), rather than routine tracheal
intubation and mechanical ventilation, for a spontaneously breathing preterm newborn who needs
respiratory support immediately after birth. Preductal pulse oximetry should guide supplemental
oxygen. For infants born before 32 weeks, an initial oxygen concentration of 30%–100% may be
reasonable; the wide range reflects uncertain evidence, so this lesson authors a conservative 30%
start and subsequent qualified-team titration rather than teaching one universal setting.

For infants born before 34 weeks, current international consensus recommends a plastic bag or wrap
and suggests a head covering to reduce hypothermia, with careful temperature monitoring because
combined warming measures can cause hyperthermia.

## Authored case estimate

The fixed case is a 1.25 kg infant born at 29 weeks 4 days who is breathing spontaneously but has
grunting, retractions, and preductal SpO2 62% at 90 seconds. Qualified staff provide thermal
protection, pulse oximetry, CPAP, and oxygen beginning at 30%. A fixed 10-minute report supplies
ongoing CPAP, oxygen titrated to 35%, SpO2 90%, HR 148/min, RR 62/min, and temperature 36.6°C.
These are defensible teaching values, not a patient-specific prescription or predicted response.
