# Cholinergic poisoning: protect the team, then clear the air

## Scope and distinctness

This lab rehearses an accidental dermal and inhalational organophosphate exposure with secondary-
contamination risk, muscarinic secretions and bronchospasm, nicotinic fasciculations and weakness,
CNS change, and respiratory failure. It is distinct from opioid poisoning, isolated bronchospasm,
intrinsic bradycardia, anticholinergic syndrome, and a generic airway algorithm.

## Fixed fixture

- A 46-year-old greenhouse worker arrives 45 minutes after organophosphate concentrate splashed onto
  his clothing in an enclosed mixing area. He remains in wet clothes, confused, miotic, diaphoretic,
  lacrimating, copiously salivating, vomiting, wheezing, fasciculating, and weak. HR is 48/min,
  BP 86/50 mmHg, RR 30/min with shallow tiring breaths, room-air SpO2 86%, and T 36.7°C.
- Qualified assessment supplies bronchorrhea, bronchospasm, weak cough, shallow ventilation, and no
  isolated structural obstruction. Plasma cholinesterase is markedly below its laboratory reference
  interval, pH 7.27, PCO2 52 mmHg, bicarbonate 23 mmol/L, lactate 3.6 mmol/L, sodium 139 mmol/L,
  potassium 4.0 mmol/L, glucose 112 mg/dL, and creatinine 0.9 mg/dL.
- A strict fixed 30-minute report supplies completed qualified clothing removal and dermal
  decontamination, active qualified airway support and assisted ventilation without a supplied device
  or setting, markedly reduced secretions and wheeze, SpO2 96%, HR 82/min,
  BP 104/64 mmHg, and clearer mentation while fasciculations and proximal weakness persist.

## Evidence application

The AHA recommends immediate atropine, early endotracheal intubation, benzodiazepines for seizure or
agitation, appropriate caregiver PPE, and dermal decontamination for life-threatening organophosphate
or carbamate poisoning. Pralidoxime is reasonable for life-threatening organophosphate poisoning;
cholinesterase-metabolized neuromuscular blockers are not recommended. The lab records only qualified
intent and a fixed later report, never learner-selected products, doses, devices, or techniques.

EPA guidance describes the coupled muscarinic, nicotinic, and CNS manifestations and emphasizes that
secretions plus respiratory muscle failure drive mortality. The lab therefore keeps bronchorrhea,
bronchospasm, weakness, ventilation, exposure, and secondary contamination visible together and does
not let drying secretions stand in for neuromuscular recovery.

## Boundaries

- Exposure, observations, respiratory and laboratory evidence, qualified care, and response are
  authored.
- No PPE item, clothing removal, washing method, atropine, pralidoxime, benzodiazepine, dose, route,
  access, suction, airway device, ventilation setting, neuromuscular blocker, or delivery is exposed.
- The fixed response does not prove treatment effect, complete decontamination, durable ventilation,
  strength recovery, co-worker safety, safe disposition, or outcome.
