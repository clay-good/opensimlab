# Evidence brief: hyperkalemic conduction disturbance

## Intended rehearsal

This advanced monitored Cardiology lesson begins after emergency hyperkalemia treatment has already
been reported. It rehearses reconciling serial potassium and conduction reports, distinguishing
calcium-associated membrane stabilization from potassium lowering, preserving glucose and rebound
surveillance, and avoiding premature permanent-device conclusions while a reversible metabolic
disturbance is corrected.

## Evidence boundary

- Hyperkalemia can cause bradycardia and conduction disturbance, but one ECG pattern or potassium
  value does not establish a universal severity threshold or prove that hyperkalemia is the sole
  cause.
- With ECG toxicity, calcium is used for myocardial membrane stabilization. An improved ECG after
  calcium does not mean potassium has been shifted or removed.
- Insulin-glucose treatment requires structured glucose surveillance. Serial potassium review is
  required because an early fall can be incomplete and rebound can follow temporary shifting.
- Potassium removal and contributor control remain separate from shifting. Kidney function,
  medication and acute-illness context require named ownership.
- Hyperkalemia is a potentially reversible cause of bradycardia or atrioventricular conduction
  disturbance. Permanent-device conclusions should not be inferred from a pretreatment rhythm while
  the reversible disturbance is being corrected; persistent disturbance still requires expert
  reassessment.

The fixed values and responses are authored scenario facts, not treatment thresholds, live kinetics,
or individual predictions. The Emergency Medicine hyperkalemia lab owns initial treatment actions;
this Cardiology lab owns post-treatment conduction attribution, surveillance, device restraint, and
handoff.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine; acquire or independently interpret specimens, ECG, monitoring, or
imaging; diagnose one cause; select or deliver calcium, insulin, glucose, beta agonist, binder,
dialysis, medication change, or glucose rescue; calculate a dose or target; model live potassium or
glucose kinetics; decide pacing eligibility; select, implant, program, or test a device; determine
disposition or prognosis; or predict recurrence, benefit, or outcome.
