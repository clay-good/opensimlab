# Magnesium sulfate toxicity recognition evidence brief

Status: development draft, not clinically reviewed. Checked August 26, 2026.

## Fixture boundary

The authored postpartum case supplies documented magnesium exposure, declining urine output and renal function, progressive drowsiness, slurred speech, weakness, absent patellar reflexes, shallow bradypnea, and a unit-reconciled magnesium result. Qualified staff have already stopped and isolated the infusion. The learner recognizes and coordinates only; no examination, monitoring or laboratory interpretation, diagnosis, airway care, antidote, dose, treatment, newborn assessment, disposition, or outcome is controlled.

## Evidence carried into the lab

- Current U.S. DailyMed prescribing information states that magnesium is renally excreted, renal impairment increases toxicity risk, and toxicity can include diminished or absent tendon reflexes, weakness, hypotension, breathing difficulty or respiratory paralysis, conduction change, and cardiac arrest. The case uses the level only as supporting evidence alongside direct clinical findings.
- AIM's severe-hypertension bundle calls for facility-wide protocols that include advance preparation for magnesium toxicity and respectful, multidisciplinary response.
- WHO emergency guidance couples respiratory rate, urine output, oxygen saturation, and deep-tendon-reflex monitoring with withholding magnesium when toxicity signs appear, airway and ventilation support for respiratory compromise, and calcium-antidote availability. Local protocols and qualified clinicians own all product and dose decisions.

## Deliberate nonclaims

The exposure, examination, laboratory values, and fixed 5-minute report are fictional. The early respiratory change does not prove calcium effect, complete reversal, magnesium clearance, renal recovery, safe restart, newborn safety, disposition, or outcome. Cognitive completion is not clinical, airway, medication, or emergency competence.
