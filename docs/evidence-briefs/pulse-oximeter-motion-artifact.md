# Evidence brief: pulse-oximeter motion artifact

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate adult ICU case rehearses treating pulse oximetry as an estimate with a signal
path: compare the displayed saturation and pulse rate with pleth quality, local perfusion, the whole
patient, and another oxygenation signal before escalating treatment for an isolated number.

## Evidence boundary

- Motion and reduced perfusion can cause faulty, absent, or delayed pulse-oximeter readings. Device
  behavior differs, so the case uses one declared teaching display rather than a performance claim.
- Pulse-rate agreement and pleth quality help judge whether the displayed saturation is credible,
  but neither proves oxygenation by itself.
- The fixed arterial panel corroborates this authored state. A clean capnogram supports ventilation
  but cannot exclude hypoxemia, dyshemoglobinemia, or another oxygenation problem.
- Skin pigmentation and other optical limitations remain clinically important, but this case does
  not use pigmentation as the explanation for the fixed abrupt motion-linked display change.
- When a real patient is unstable, support and escalation occur in parallel with signal checking.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not inspect, reposition, or replace a probe; examine a patient or perfusion; sample
or analyze blood; configure or validate a monitor; diagnose artifact, hypoxemia, or dyshemoglobinemia;
deliver oxygen or treatment; determine disposition; or predict outcome. Display behavior, arterial
results, patient observations, and the response are fixed authored proxies.
