# Pediatric safeguarding concern evidence brief

## Decision boundary

This lab begins with a physiologically stable, independently mobile 2-year-old and a supplied injury
distribution that is not adequately explained by one reported forward fall. The learner reconciles
development, history, injuries, immediate safety, and the whole child; recognizes a safeguarding
concern without diagnosing abuse; activates qualified safeguarding and immediate-safety ownership;
then completes medical-alternative and information-boundary review before a strictly later
protected safety state and completes an elapsed unresolved-risk handoff.

The lesson does not investigate a caregiver, elicit a disclosure, adjudicate credibility, teach a
jurisdiction-specific reporting rule, calculate TEN-4-FACESp, or diagnose physical abuse. Its only
new rehearsal value is recognizing concern and preserving protected multidisciplinary ownership.

## Fixed teaching facts

- A previously well, reportedly developmentally typical and independently walking and running
  2-year-old girl is 12 kg and 88 cm.
- Her caregiver reports one forward trip onto a carpeted level floor about 2 hours earlier.
- She is awake, interactive, uses age-appropriate speech and play, and stays near her caregiver.
  Temperature is 36.8°C, HR 108/min, RR 22/min, BP 96/60 mmHg (MAP 72), room-air SpO₂ 99%, pulses
  are warm and normal volume, and refill is 2 seconds.
- A fixed qualified examination describes one oval bruise approximately 2 × 1 cm on the posterior
  left pinna, three separate similarly shaped 1–2 cm bruises clustered over the right lateral torso
  below the axilla, and two small anterior-shin bruises. Bruise age is not inferred from color.
- No active bleeding, respiratory distress, shock, altered consciousness, vomiting, seizure, focal
  neurological finding, abdominal distension, or reported loss of consciousness is authored.
- A supplied experienced-team statement says the single forward carpet fall does not adequately
  account for the ear and lateral-torso distribution. This establishes concern requiring further
  evaluation, not abuse, perpetrator identity, or a credibility ruling.
- No bleeding disorder, anticoagulant exposure, chronic illness, recent major trauma, or cultural
  skin practice is reported. These history snapshots do not exclude a bleeding condition,
  accidental mechanism, cultural practice, occult injury, history limitation, or another medical or
  safeguarding explanation.
- At strict-later minute 15, she remains awake, interactive, warm, and stable with HR 104/min,
  RR 22/min, BP 98/62 mmHg (MAP 74), room-air SpO₂ 99%, and temperature 36.8°C in a supervised
  clinical setting with named pediatric and safeguarding ownership. Injury assessment, medical
  alternatives, information gathering, immediate safety, other-child risk, and the locally governed
  multi-agency pathway remain active.
- No abuse determination, perpetrator attribution, test result, referral completion, legal report,
  custody action, disposition, prognosis, or outcome is supplied. Stability does not establish
  reassurance or discharge readiness.

All histories, values, injuries, negative findings, timing, and later state are fictional face-valid
fixtures. The sources support qualitative concern and response boundaries, not these exact details or
an abuse determination.

## Source synthesis

NICE CG89 identifies patterned, clustered, ear, torso, facial, and otherwise unsuitably explained
bruising as features that warrant suspicion or consideration of maltreatment. This simulator uses
ear and torso locations plus a supplied unsuitable-explanation statement to establish a concern only.
The child is independently mobile, so the December 2025 premobility definition is not the trigger.

NICE NG76 places the child at the center, directs practitioners to named safeguarding leadership and
locally governed multi-agency procedures, and cautions that concern sharing must not increase risk.
The WHO clinical handbook similarly integrates identification, safe communication, objective history
and finding documentation, immediate support and safety, and reporting considerations under
applicable systems. The learner surface records ownership rather than communication, documentation,
referral, reporting, legal, or investigation competence.

The prospective multicenter TEN-4-FACESp validation study found that certain bruising regions and
patterns in bruised children younger than 4 years can identify children needing further evaluation.
The investigators explicitly describe the rule as a screening aid that does not diagnose abuse or
replace clinical judgment. This case fits the study's age-and-bruising applicability, but the learner
does not calculate or apply the rule and the scenario does not reproduce its performance estimates.

## Explicit nonclaims

- The learner does not examine or interview the child or caregiver; identify, measure, photograph,
  map, or date bruises; elicit a disclosure; acquire or interpret a test or image; or assess injury,
  medical alternatives, immediate safety, communication, documentation, or credibility.
- No abuse diagnosis, perpetrator attribution, legal finding, referral or report submission,
  jurisdiction or law selection, custody action, caregiver confrontation or separation, procedure,
  treatment, disposition, prognosis, or outcome is selected or performed.
- TEN-4-FACESp is not calculated, and neither a positive nor negative screen diagnoses or excludes
  abuse.
- Bruise color does not establish injury age. Stable physiology does not establish low safeguarding
  risk, exclude occult harm, or establish discharge readiness.
- Fixed negative histories and findings do not exclude accidental injury, a bleeding condition,
  cultural practice, occult injury, abuse, neglect, or another medical or safeguarding explanation.
- Reporting duties and safeguarding pathways vary by jurisdiction, profession, and setting; the lab
  claims only locally governed qualified ownership.
