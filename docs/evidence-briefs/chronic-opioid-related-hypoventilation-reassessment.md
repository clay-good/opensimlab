# Chronic opioid-related hypoventilation reassessment

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate Respiratory Medicine lab rehearses connecting chronic prescribed-opioid
exposure and longitudinal sleep symptoms with fixed awake and attended sleep-study evidence,
preserving important alternative contributors, and coordinating patient-centered ownership across
prescriber, sleep, respiratory, pharmacy, and primary-care teams.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2019 AASM position statement identifies sleep-related hypoventilation, central sleep apnea,
  and obstructive sleep apnea as distinct sleep-disordered breathing patterns associated with
  chronic opioid therapy. It supports appropriate screening, diagnostic testing, treatment, and
  collaboration. Association does not prove opioid-only causality in this fictional patient.
- The AASM diagnostic-testing guideline recommends polysomnography rather than home sleep apnea
  testing when chronic opioid use or suspected sleep-related hypoventilation is present. This lab
  supplies a fixed attended-study report; the learner does not acquire, score, or interpret it.
- The 2022 CDC opioid guideline supports individualized risk review, attention to sleep-disordered
  breathing and co-exposures, patient-centered collaboration, and avoiding abrupt or
  noncollaborative opioid discontinuation outside a life-threatening situation. The browser does
  not calculate morphine equivalents or select a drug, dose, taper, or naloxone intervention.
- One quiet awake SpO₂ cannot establish adequate ventilation during sleep. The authored awake gas,
  nocturnal carbon-dioxide trend, symptoms, exposure, separately reported breathing events, and
  open alternatives remain a whole pattern rather than one universal threshold.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine; acquire, score, or interpret a blood gas, sleep study, oximetry,
capnography, spirometry, or another test; diagnose hypoventilation, central or obstructive sleep
apnea, or a cause; calculate morphine equivalents; choose, stop, substitute, or taper a drug;
select or deliver naloxone, oxygen, positive-airway-pressure mode or settings, ventilation, or
treatment; determine disposition or prognosis; or predict response or outcome. All findings are
fixed authored reports.
