# Evidence brief: Acute ischemic stroke

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate adult emergency-department case rehearses recognizing a disabling acute stroke,
establishing the last-known-well time, checking glucose and immediate physiologic threats,
activating the stroke system, reviewing authored CT and CTA findings, recording one fixed
local-protocol tenecteplase intent, activating thrombectomy transfer without waiting for treatment
response, and handing off the surveillance findings and clocks.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2026 AHA/ASA guideline endorses rapid IV thrombolysis for eligible patients with disabling
  deficits within 4.5 hours, regardless of stroke-score value. The fixed 70-minute presentation is
  in that window and does not require advanced perfusion imaging for this thrombolysis decision.
- The guideline endorses either alteplase or tenecteplase in the 4.5-hour window. This vignette uses
  a local-protocol tenecteplase branch only. For the authored 80 kg patient, 0.25 mg/kg is 20 mg;
  the teaching calculation carries the established 25 mg maximum.
- Noncontrast CT is authored as showing no intracranial hemorrhage, and CTA is authored as showing
  a left M1 large-vessel occlusion. The learner does not acquire or interpret either study.
- Endovascular thrombectomy is standard treatment for eligible large-vessel occlusion. The
  thrombectomy-capable-center pathway proceeds without waiting to see whether thrombolysis changes
  the deficit. The simulator does not perform patient selection, transfer, or thrombectomy.
- BP is authored as 168/94 mmHg, below the established 185/110 mmHg pre-thrombolysis threshold. No
  blood-pressure intervention is offered, and no real eligibility conclusion should be transferred
  from this fixed case to a patient.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not perform a history, neurologic examination, stroke score, glucose or pressure
measurement, imaging acquisition or interpretation, contraindication assessment, consent,
vascular access, laboratory testing, medication preparation or delivery, blood-pressure treatment,
transport, endovascular selection or procedure, reperfusion assessment, hemorrhage or angioedema
recognition, post-reperfusion management, rehabilitation planning, team performance, disposition,
prognosis, or outcome. No neurologic improvement is authored after treatment intent.
