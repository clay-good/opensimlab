# Hypocalcemia: ionized calcium and kidney context

Content version 0.1.0; renal/electrolyte preview. Sources checked August 27, 2026.
Independent clinical approval remains pending. Educational simulation, not patient care.

## What the learner must distinguish

A reassuring albumin-adjusted estimate does not erase low measured ionized calcium and symptoms.
This lesson follows a fictional patient with advanced kidney disease and recent denosumab exposure.
It separates monitored rescue, delivered continuing calcium care, and longer-term mineral and
medication follow-up. It is distinct from the postoperative endocrine hypocalcemia lesson.

[Gauci et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18400941/) compared calcium measurements in
691 nondialysis patients with CKD stages 3–5. Albumin adjustment did not improve classification
against ionized calcium; low albumin increased overestimation after correction. The indexed
full-text discussion and analytical methods support using a measured actual-pH ionized result
in this supplied discordant case. Appropriate sample handling matters. This does not validate
the exact fictional values or establish a new universal laboratory policy.

[Society for Endocrinology guidance, 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5314808/)
supports urgent monitored calcium for significant symptomatic hypocalcemia and continuing
cause-directed care. Its adjusted-total threshold is not used to override the supplied symptoms
and low measured ionized result. The guidance specifically cautions against large-volume calcium
infusions in end-stage renal failure or dialysis. This patient has nondialysis CKD4, not an
automatic exemption from individualized volume and treatment review. No infusion recipe is copied.
The [2019 addendum](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8117371/) emphasizes differing calcium
preparations. Neither product equivalence, access selection, nor a dose calculation is simulated.

The [FDA's January 2024 safety communication](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-communications/fda-adds-boxed-warning-increased-risk-severe-hypocalcemia-patients-advanced-chronic-kidney-disease)
supports attention to denosumab exposure in advanced CKD and prolonged monitoring. The supplied
21-day exposure lies within its reported higher-risk period, not proof of individual causation.
The [current Prolia prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=49e5afe9-a0c7-40c4-af9f-f287a80c5c88),
revised June 2026, supports CKD-MBD expertise and individualized calcium/activated-vitamin-D care.
Its routine weekly/monthly surveillance schedule is not an acute-treatment interval. Future
medication decisions need qualified coordination, including fracture-risk safeguards if therapy
is discontinued. Neither automatic permanent cessation nor restarting treatment is a learner action.

US/GB controls share this selected fiction, not a completed regional treatment matrix. The FDA
communication is a safety source, not a general acute-care guideline. Source material is
paraphrased; no copyrighted treatment figure or prescription table is reproduced.

## Authored patient, defaults, and response

The fictional patient is a 72-year-old woman, 64 kg and 161 cm tall, ASA class 3. Documented CKD4
includes eGFR 22 mL/min/1.73 m²; she is not receiving dialysis. Her most recent denosumab injection
was 21 days earlier. No medication dose, missed supplement, hypoparathyroidism, vitamin-D result,
or acute kidney injury is inferred from that context.

Pretreatment total calcium is 7.2 mg/dL, albumin 2.0 g/dL, and the supplied albumin-adjusted
estimate 8.8 mg/dL. Measured ionized calcium is 0.86 mmol/L at actual sample pH 7.40, not an
ionized value mathematically corrected to pH 7.40. Magnesium is 0.80 mmol/L and phosphate
6.2 mg/dL. The prior ECG report supplies QTc 520 ms. Total calcium, albumin, pH, magnesium,
phosphate, eGFR, and QTc remain historical; later checks do not generate new versions of them.
Normal supplied magnesium does not require a magnesium-treatment action.

She is awake with painful carpopedal spasm and perioral tingling. Initial HR is 102/min,
BP 138/78 mmHg, MAP 98 mmHg, RR 22/min, SpO2 98%, and temperature 36.8°C. At 15 minutes after
rescue, ionized calcium becomes 0.96 mmol/L, HR 90/min, RR 18/min, and spasm is absent. Without
continuing calcium care, the 45-minute post-rescue contrast is ionized calcium 0.88 mmol/L,
HR 104/min, RR 22/min, and recurrent spasm.

At 60 minutes after delivered continuing calcium care, ionized calcium becomes 1.03 mmol/L,
HR 86/min, RR 18/min, and spasm is absent. Continuing care is available immediately after rescue,
without waiting for its early response or a new laboratory click. Mineral-care and follow-up
acknowledgments do not drive this response. No rapid activated-vitamin-D efficacy or denosumab
reversal is modeled. Perioral tingling persists; BP, MAP, oxygen saturation, and temperature stay
fixed. Rhythm is sinus throughout, with no modeled QT interval or new ECG interpretation.

Without rescue, the five-minute contrast is HR 112/min and RR 24/min with unchanged ionized
calcium and persistent spasm. Instructor takeover occurs at 30 minutes without rescue or at
the 180-minute session boundary otherwise. Sorted checkpoints preserve chronology across sparse
clock jumps. All intervals and numerical responses are authored contrasts, not safe waiting
periods, treatment deadlines, drug duration, expected correction, or outcome predictions.

Compatibility defaults are fixed stroke volume 70 mL, hemoglobin 11 g/dL, blood volume 4,200 mL,
and unit arterial/baroreflex factors. Inactive ventilation defaults are 450 mL, 22/min, FiO2 0.21,
and fresh gas flow 10 L/min. Airway difficulty 0.1 and the healthy respiratory compatibility
profile are not measured performance or treatment settings. Exhaled CO2 and FiO2 are unavailable
as monitored findings. No randomized calcium response, binding equation, renal-clearance solver,
or calibrated symptom threshold is supplied.

## Observation, handoff, and private reporting

Ionized-only, symptom-only, and full assessments retain separate timestamps. Partial checks cannot
refresh the full panel. Monitored rescue has no administrative prerequisite. Continuing calcium
does not wait for context, mineral-care, follow-up, or new measurement acknowledgments.

The ten-decision example pauses before each choice, with two authored observation periods.
Handoff requires delivered rescue and continuing calcium, qualified support, context, monitoring,
mineral care, longer-term follow-up, and a current full assessment after a rescue response. It may
transfer current recurrence while a newly delivered continuing-care response remains pending.
Normal calcium, every older panel, and an error-free history are not prerequisites. Debrief credit
for the continuing response requires its actual later full checkpoint, not merely another click
after care was requested. Earlier observed recurrence and refused shortcuts remain in history.

The shared centered Report a Problem dialog accepts at most 160 characters. Recent context is
unchecked by default and previewed before sending. It contains uniquely attributable recognized
actions, seven live vital signs, and at most 30 explicit observed/care equipment scalars. Hidden
results, timers, alertness prose, arbitrary payloads, assignments, and reflections are excluded.
Turnstile, Worker/D1 validation, abuse controls, retention, and weekly review use shared operations.
Publish the additive Worker identity catalog before new client content; local checks send no
production reports.

Independent clinical review, four exact-version quality records, full inclusive-runtime validation,
and production reporting evidence remain pending. Browser choices cannot assess examination,
