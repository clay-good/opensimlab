# Evidence brief: massive pulmonary embolism

## Intended rehearsal

This advanced ICU case rehearses immediate recognition of confirmed acute pulmonary embolism with
Category E2R refractory cardiopulmonary failure, an RV-sensitive support review, activation of an
ECMO-capable rescue system, and reassessment without treating temporary support as clot treatment.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2026 AHA/ACC acute-PE guideline replaces the broad “massive” label with Category E. E2 means
  refractory cardiogenic shock or prolonged arrest; the respiratory modifier identifies
  hypoxemic or ventilatory failure. The conventional title remains for curriculum continuity.
- The guideline states that VA-ECMO is reasonable for acute PE with Category E2 refractory shock
  when appropriate resources are available. It supports hemodynamics and oxygenation; it does not
  remove the embolus or establish one universal destination.
- Once VA-ECMO is running, the usefulness of additional advanced PE therapy is not established.
  The lab therefore keeps thrombolysis, catheter, thrombectomy, and surgical decisions expert,
  bleeding-, resource-, and trajectory-dependent rather than selecting a routine adjunct.
- ELSO and ESICM guidance support multidisciplinary candidacy, safe systems, perfusion monitoring,
  repeated organ assessment, and complication surveillance. No cannulation or device management
  skill is represented here.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine, acquire or interpret monitoring, CT, echo, laboratory, or hemodynamic
data, diagnose, deliver oxygen, ventilation, anticoagulation, fluid, or drugs, select access or
dose, perform CPR, cannulate, initiate or manage ECMO, perform thrombolysis, thrombectomy, or
embolectomy, transfer, determine disposition, predict outcome, or assess PE, shock, perfusion,
ECMO, prescribing, imaging, catheter, surgical, or resuscitation competence.
