# Umbilical cord prolapse urgent-birth coordination evidence brief

Status: development draft, not clinically reviewed. Checked August 26, 2026.

## Fixture boundary

The authored case begins after spontaneous membrane rupture, fetal bradycardia, and qualified digital confirmation of an overt prolapsed cord. Cervical dilation, a high cephalic presenting part, and non-imminent vaginal birth are supplied. Qualified manual elevation and left-lateral head-down positioning are already underway. The learner never examines, interprets monitoring, handles the cord, relieves pressure, selects medication or anesthesia, or performs birth or newborn care.

## Evidence carried into the lab

- RCOG Green-top Guideline No. 50 (2014, reviewed December 2024 and extended for 2 years) supports immediate help and theatre preparation; minimal exposed-cord handling; qualified presenting-part elevation by hand or bladder filling; knee-chest or left-lateral head-down positioning; avoiding cord replacement to continue labour; and preventing temporary measures from delaying birth. Birth mode and urgency depend on fetal-heart pattern and whether vaginal birth is imminent, with maternal safety taking priority over fixation on a time target.
- RCOG patient information, updated August 2025, supplies patient-facing language: this is an emergency because compression can reduce fetal oxygen delivery, the situation is frightening, the team should explain what is happening, and emergency caesarean or rapid vaginal birth may be appropriate to the individual situation.
- NICE NG229 treats fetal-heart monitoring as one source of information rather than a standalone diagnosis and supports expedited birth after an acute event such as cord prolapse. The lab therefore supplies the trace interpretation and does not let the learner diagnose from one number.

## Deliberate nonclaims

The fixed 6-minute report is fictional and does not prove benefit from pressure relief, set a universal deadline, adjudicate anesthesia or birth mode, or predict fetal, newborn, or maternal outcome. Cognitive completion is not psychomotor or clinical competence.
