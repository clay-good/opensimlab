# Evidence brief: delayed emergence differential

## Learning boundary

- **Target learner:** anesthesia learners organizing the first assessment of delayed emergence.
- **Environment:** one stable, intubated adult 20 minutes after anesthetic delivery ended.
- **Primary objective:** preserve immediate support, review common recorded causes systematically,
  identify a new lateralizing examination pattern, and escalate urgently without assigning a diagnosis.
- **Why simulation adds value:** each accepted step reveals only the next fixed findings, making
  premature anchoring and routine observation visible without reproducing tests or treatment.

## Evidence and applicability

1. Cascella M, Bimonte S, Di Napoli R. *Delayed Emergence from Anesthesia: What We Know and How We
   Act*. Local Reg Anesth. 2020;13:195-206. PMCID PMC7652217; DOI 10.2147/LRA.S230728. Consulted
   2026-08-24. The review describes residual anesthetic and adjuvant effects as common causes,
   includes metabolic and neurologic causes in the differential, and places airway protection,
   examination, and systematic cause review before further diagnostic testing when the cause remains unclear.
2. Tzabazis A, Miller C, Dobrow MF, Zheng K, Brock-Utne JG. *Delayed emergence after anesthesia*.
   J Clin Anesth. 2015;27:353-360. PMID 25912729; DOI 10.1016/j.jclinane.2015.03.006. Consulted
   2026-08-24. This review supports a structured differential spanning pharmacologic, metabolic,
   and neurologic causes rather than attribution to delayed drug clearance alone.

These reviews support the categories and ordering principle. They do not supply this fictional
patient, the exact values, the examination pattern, a diagnostic conclusion, or an outcome.

## Modeled variables and exact fixture

- The tracheal tube and delivered ventilation remain established. Oxygenation, end-tidal carbon
  dioxide, circulation, and temperature are declared stable during the immediate support review.
- The exposure review declares volatile delivery and infusions off, no benzodiazepine, a last small
  opioid dose more than one hour earlier, and a quantitative train-of-four ratio of 0.95.
- The bounded metabolic review reveals glucose 102 mg/dL, arterial carbon dioxide 41 mmHg, sodium
  139 mEq/L, and temperature 36.7°C. These are authored findings, not engine-simulated tests.
- The focused examination reveals left-arm localization, absent right-arm response, and a new
  leftward gaze preference. The expert choice records urgent neurologic evaluation while airway
  support continues. The engine rejects skipped, repeated, unknown, and out-of-lesson requests.

## Exclusions and unsafe inferences

The browser does not measure consciousness, reproduce history gathering or physical examination,
simulate drug concentrations, reversal, laboratory tests, blood gas sampling, imaging, neurologic
diagnosis, treatment selection, team workflow, transfer, complications, or outcome. The normal fixed
values do not exhaust metabolic causes. The lateralizing pattern changes urgency but does not establish
its cause. This vignette is not an assessment algorithm or a substitute for local emergency pathways.

## Review needs

Required review domains are adult anesthesia, emergence and recovery, neurologic emergencies,
pharmacology, simulation education, accessibility, and patient safety. The content remains unsigned
draft work until those reviews are recorded against the exact content version.
