# Hypothermia and rewarming evidence brief

## What this slice teaches

One stable adult intraoperative vignette makes a falling core-temperature trend visible, asks the
learner to confirm the measurement, restore active surface warming, include a fixed 700 mL
crystalloid exposure in the warming plan, and observe temperature recover through 36.5°C.

## Evidence boundary

- NICE CG65 defines inadvertent perioperative hypothermia as core temperature below 36.0°C,
  recommends repeated perioperative temperature measurement, active warming for qualifying adult
  surgical patients, and warming intravenous fluid exposures of 500 mL or more.
- Sessler’s thermoregulation review describes anesthetic impairment of thermoregulation and the
  characteristic redistribution-then-heat-loss course. It does not supply a universal patient
  trajectory.

## Modeled behavior

- A scripted event moves temperature from a 36.7°C baseline toward a fixed 35.5°C teaching target.
- Accepted active surface warming changes the target to 36.6°C and produces gradual recovery.
- A low-temperature alarm appears below 36.0°C.
- Temperature change is attributed explicitly as fixed perioperative heat loss or active warming.

## Deliberate exclusions

This is not a two-compartment heat model. It does not calculate redistribution, metabolic heat,
ambient loss, warmed-fluid energy transfer, forced-air settings, probe accuracy, skin contact,
shivering, comfort, complications, medication kinetics, emergence, or disposition. The fixed
cooling and rewarming rates are teaching calibrations, not individual predictions.
