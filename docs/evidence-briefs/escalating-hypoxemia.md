# Evidence brief: escalating hypoxemia

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate adult ICU case rehearses responding to a credible saturation decline while
systematically checking the oxygen source, circuit, tracheal tube, lungs, pressures, capnography,
and circulation. It then asks the learner to escalate unresolved parenchymal hypoxemia through the
local ventilator and imaging pathway and to reassess the whole patient.

## Evidence boundary

- Current UK Defence Medical Services invasive-ventilation guidance combines immediate oxygenation,
  lung-protective ventilation, optimal PEEP, troubleshooting, and escalation when hypoxemia worsens.
- The AHRQ Patient Safety Network recommends continuous capnography, immediate backup oxygenation
  when ventilator failure is suspected, and a systematic displacement, obstruction, pneumothorax,
  and equipment review rather than changing support blindly.
- This authored panel deliberately leaves the parenchymal cause unresolved. A credible pleth,
  persistent capnogram, stable tube depth, patent suction path, connected circuit, bilateral air
  entry, pressure pattern, and stable circulation narrow immediate threats but do not prove a
  diagnosis or make imaging optional.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not measure saturation, examine the chest, handle a circuit, pass a catheter,
confirm tube position, obtain a gas or image, diagnose the cause, hand-ventilate, program a
ventilator, select individualized PEEP or oxygen targets, perform recruitment, bronchoscopy,
decompression, tube exchange, proning, or ECMO, or assess respiratory-therapy, airway, procedural,
disposition, prognosis, or outcome competence. All findings and responses are fixed teaching panels.
