# Evidence brief: Exertional heat stroke

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate adult emergency case rehearses recognizing exertional heat stroke from CNS
dysfunction plus measured core hyperthermia, supporting ABCs without delaying cooling, prioritizing
whole-body cold-water immersion, stopping active cooling below 39°C, and continuing surveillance for
delayed multiorgan injury after the temperature target is reached.

## Evidence boundary

- Heat stroke is severe heat illness with CNS dysfunction and marked core hyperthermia. The case
  authors exertion, encephalopathy, and rectal temperature 41.3°C while keeping mimics visible.
- The 2024 Wilderness Medical Society guideline recommends ice-water immersion for heat stroke and
  the coldest available water when ice-water immersion is not possible. Rapid cooling is the priority.
- The 2024 Society of Critical Care Medicine guideline prioritizes methods that reach below 39°C
  within 30 minutes. The fixed panel reaches 38.9°C after 14 minutes, then active cooling stops.
- Temperature improvement does not close the case. Renal, hepatic, coagulation, muscle, electrolyte,
  glucose, urine, and neurologic abnormalities can evolve; antipyretics and dantrolene are not treatments.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine the patient, measure rectal temperature, obtain glucose or sodium, exclude
mimics, remove clothing, provide airway or circulatory support, perform or supervise immersion, choose
water temperature, measure cooling rate, prevent drowning or aspiration, give fluids, coordinate real
transport, run labs, diagnose or treat organ injury, or assess psychomotor, team, disposition, recovery,
prognosis, or outcome competence.
