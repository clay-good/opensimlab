# Pacemaker capture failure evidence brief

## What this slice teaches

One fixed adult monitored-cardiac-unit vignette asks the learner to distinguish ventricular pacing
artifacts from electrical and mechanical capture, recognize symptomatic hypotension, activate acute
bradycardia rescue and device expertise without waiting for complete troubleshooting, and review a
bounded device-system and cause report. A later experienced-team report restores capture, but the
lesson keeps system integrity and definitive planning open.

## Current source floor

- The 2025 AHA adult advanced-life-support guideline and bradycardia algorithm frame acute
  bradycardia by symptoms and tissue perfusion, require reversible-cause review, and preserve
  transcutaneous or expert temporary transvenous pacing as rescue pathways for persistent
  hemodynamic compromise. They also emphasize the difficulty of confirming and maintaining both
  electrical and mechanical capture.
- The 2018 ACC/AHA/HRS bradycardia guideline requires effective pacing capture to be confirmed by a
  pulse or arterial waveform rather than ECG appearance alone and treats transcutaneous pacing as a
  short bridge rather than definitive device management.
- The 2021 ESC pacing guideline supports structured review of battery, lead, capture, and technical
  issues and additional expert in-person assessment when device malfunction is suspected.
- The 2017 multisociety HRS lead-management consensus emphasizes impedance trends beside sensing,
  stored-electrogram, and capture evidence. A fixed impedance cutoff alone has limited sensitivity
  and does not prove a lead mechanism or choose revision or extraction.

## Authored vignette and response

- The patient has a pulse at 32/min, BP 84/52 mmHg, abrupt presyncope, and cool perfusion. Six of 10
  reported ventricular pacing artifacts lack a following paced QRS; pulse and pleth follow actual
  QRS complexes rather than isolated artifacts.
- A fixed experienced-team interrogation report supplies prior and current output, threshold,
  impedance, battery, atrial-lead, stored-electrogram, and dependency facts. The abrupt threshold and
  impedance changes support concern only in combination; they are not reusable malfunction cutoffs.
- Fixed potassium, magnesium, pH, oxygenation, pocket, and chest-radiograph reports narrow but do not
  close lead, connector, generator, lead-myocardial-interface, ischemic, inflammatory, metabolic,
  medication, procedural, or measurement causes.
- After rescue activation, device-system review, cause review, and elapsed time, an experienced team
  reports a device-specific temporary programming change with electrical and mechanical capture at
  70/min, BP 114/68 mmHg, warm perfusion, and resolved presyncope. No setting is exposed, and another
  elapsed handoff preserves capture surveillance, rescue triggers, unresolved integrity, and
  definitive electrophysiology work.

## Deliberate exclusions

This is not an ECG, pulse-assessment, resuscitation, interrogation, programming, magnet,
transcutaneous-pacing, transvenous-pacing, lead-management, or procedure trainer. It does not select
an output, rate, pulse width, mode, drug, access, temporary system, lead revision or extraction,
generator replacement, disposition, prognosis, durable repair, or outcome. Pulse loss opens the
separate cardiac-arrest pathway.
