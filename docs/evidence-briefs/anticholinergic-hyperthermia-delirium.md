# Anticholinergic hyperthermia and delirium

## Teaching claim

This lab teaches recognition and escalation of a declared isolated antimuscarinic poisoning pattern:
delirium, mydriasis, dry flushed skin and mucosa, urinary retention, reduced bowel activity, sinus
tachycardia, and life-threatening hyperthermia. It does not teach a mnemonic as diagnosis, infer an
unknown exposure, prescribe cooling technique, or make physostigmine automatic.

## Authoritative basis

- American Heart Association, *2025 Guidelines for CPR and ECC, Part 10: Adult and Pediatric
  Special Circumstances of Resuscitation*, Section 16.2. For core temperature above 40°C from
  medication or drug overdose, active cooling should begin with standard resuscitation; rapid cooling
  and a 38.6°C stop point are addressed. Dantrolene is not useful for non-malignant-hyperthermia
  causes. Consulted 2026-08-26.
- National Library of Medicine, StatPearls, *Anticholinergic Toxicity*. The whole clinical pattern,
  ECG and complication surveillance, supportive care, benzodiazepines for agitation, cooling for
  significant hyperthermia, and specialist-bounded physostigmine use in severe isolated central and
  peripheral anticholinergic poisoning inform this fixture. Consulted 2026-08-26.

## Fixed fixture

A 29-year-old woman arrives 3 hours after a supplied benztropine-only ingestion. Qualified findings
are severe agitated delirium, mydriasis, dry mouth, hot flushed dry skin, urinary retention, reduced
bowel sounds, HR 138/min, BP 132/78 mmHg (MAP 96), RR 24/min, room-air SpO2 98%, and core temperature
40.3°C. A supplied ECG reports sinus tachycardia with QRS 86 ms and no terminal rightward aVR pattern.
Supplied laboratory values are pH 7.36, PCO2 38 mmHg, bicarbonate 21 mmol/L, lactate 3.2 mmol/L,
sodium 142 mmol/L, potassium 3.8 mmol/L, glucose 118 mg/dL, creatinine 1.1 mg/dL, and CK 820 U/L.

After elapsed time, a strict qualified-team report supplies active cooling and supportive care,
qualified sedation, temperature 38.6°C, HR 106/min, BP 124/72 mmHg (MAP 89), calmer but still confused
mentation, and persistent urinary retention. These authored changes do not prove individualized
treatment effect, exposure purity, durable temperature control, renal safety, seizure exclusion,
safe disposition, or outcome.

## Deliberate boundaries

- The supplied exposure and normal-width QRS support this authored fixture; they do not make an
  unknown real-world ingestion "pure" or establish physostigmine eligibility.
- The learner records qualified rapid-cooling, supportive-care, sedation/seizure, serial laboratory,
  bladder, and toxicologist-led antidote-eligibility intent only. No cooling method, fluid, sedative,
  physostigmine product, dose, rate, target, route, access, catheter, or delivery is exposed.
- Dry skin and absent clonus, hyperreflexia, rigidity, diaphoresis, and fasciculations help compare
  authored syndromes but do not exclude sympathomimetic, serotonergic, malignant-hyperthermia,
  infection, environmental, endocrine, neurologic, or mixed causes in a real patient.

## Review need

Clinical reviewers should verify the fixture's temperature, ECG and laboratory values; the cooling,
sedation, urinary-retention and physostigmine boundaries; and the separation from sodium-channel,
serotonergic, sympathomimetic, cholinergic, malignant-hyperthermia and non-toxicologic hyperthermia.
