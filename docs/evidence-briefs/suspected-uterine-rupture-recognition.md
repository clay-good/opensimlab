# Suspected uterine rupture recognition evidence brief

Status: development draft, not clinically reviewed. Checked August 26, 2026.

## Fixture boundary

The authored case supplies prior low-transverse caesarean birth, persistent pain between contractions, abrupt fetal-heart abnormality, loss of station, cessation of uterine activity, bleeding, tenderness, and evolving maternal compromise. Qualified staff have already stopped oxytocin. The learner activates and coordinates only; no examination, CTG interpretation, diagnosis, resuscitation, anesthesia, delivery, surgery, or outcome is controlled.

## Evidence carried into the lab

- RCOG Green-top Guideline No. 45 identifies abnormal CTG as the most consistent feature, often combined with abdominal pain, while the classic pain/bleeding/fetal-heart triad occurs in fewer than 10% of complete ruptures. Other associated findings include tenderness, bleeding, hematuria, cessation of uterine activity, maternal compromise, loss of station, and changed abdominal contour. Early recognition, expeditious laparotomy, and neonatal readiness matter.
- NICE NG192 classifies suspected uterine rupture as a category-1 immediate threat and says rapid birth must still account for the condition and safety of the woman and fetus. The scenario therefore avoids turning the usual 30-minute category target into a biologic deadline.
- ACOG Practice Bulletin No. 205, reaffirmed 2024, supports individualized counselling and the requirement that labour after caesarean occur where emergency response is available. It does not provide the scenario's fixed values.

## Deliberate nonclaims

The fixed 7-minute report is fictional. It does not confirm rupture, prove a treatment effect, prescribe an anesthetic or operation, determine repair versus hysterectomy, or predict maternal, fetal, newborn, or fertility outcome. Cognitive completion is not clinical or operative competence.
