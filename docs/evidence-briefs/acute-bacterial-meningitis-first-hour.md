# Acute bacterial meningitis first hour

## Decision boundary

This lab teaches the first-hour tension in suspected acute bacterial meningitis: preserve high-yield
blood and CSF diagnostics when safe, avoid routine imaging before lumbar puncture in the exact alert
nonfocal stable state, and never allow delayed tests or imaging to delay qualified empiric
antimicrobial and adjunctive care. The learner activates experienced infection, neurological,
resuscitation, nursing, laboratory, pharmacy, and locally appropriate precaution ownership. The
learner does not examine, image, perform LP, select a regimen, prescribe, or deliver treatment.

The case is distinct from generic sepsis and shock labs because it centers the changing LP/imaging
safety boundary, CSF pattern, neurological complications, hearing follow-up, and pathogen, contact,
and public-health handoff. It is distinct from the next encephalitis lab because the initial and
strict-later reports preserve orientation, GCS 15, symmetric function, and no seizure or focal sign.

## Evidence and authored values

The 2025 WHO guideline recommends prompt LP when safe, CSF cellular and biochemical investigations,
parallel blood cultures, nonroutine cranial imaging, and empiric intravenous antimicrobial treatment
as early as possible; diagnostics should precede treatment when feasible but must not delay it. It
also supports adjunctive corticosteroid initiation with the first antibiotic dose in applicable
non-epidemic suspected bacterial meningitis and formal hearing screening before discharge or within
4 weeks if not possible. NICE NG240 gives an explicit no-routine-imaging rule and names new focal
features, abnormal pupils, GCS 9 or less, progressive consciousness decline, airway or respiratory
compromise, shock, uncontrolled seizure, bleeding risk, purpura, and evolving-lesion risk as reasons
to defer LP, stabilize, or image.

The 28-year-old patient, 14-hour and 6-hour clocks, examination, physiology, inflammatory markers,
coagulation, glucose, blood-culture activity, LP safety state, CSF opening pressure, cell count,
differential, protein, glucose, ratio, lactate, Gram stain, supplied qualified care, and 45-minute
clinical report are authored teaching evidence. The CSF pattern strongly supports acute bacterial
meningitis but does not identify an organism, establish susceptibility, prove response, permanently
exclude deterioration, or predict complications, hearing, recovery, disposition, prognosis, or
outcome.
