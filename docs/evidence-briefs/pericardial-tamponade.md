# Evidence brief: pericardial tamponade

## Intended rehearsal

This advanced Cardiology lesson begins after urgent image-guided pericardiocentesis has already
occurred. The learner reconciles the pretreatment medical-tamponade record with the current stable
state, reviews the reported drainage response, keeps etiology open, plans serial surveillance, and
hands off pending results and recurrence or deterioration triggers after an elapsed reassessment.

Emergency Medicine already owns penetrating-trauma tamponade recognition and immediate trauma and
surgical control intent. This Cardiology lab owns post-drainage medical-tamponade reasoning. It does
not repeat traumatic collapse or teach pericardiocentesis.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2025 ESC guideline defines tamponade as a clinical syndrome caused by an effusion impairing
  cardiac filling and reducing output. Diagnosis integrates history, symptoms, signs, and
  echocardiographic confirmation; Beck's triad may be absent, particularly with medical tamponade.
- The guideline recommends urgent drainage according to clinical presentation, without delay in an
  unstable patient, using image guidance and experienced operators. Etiology influences prognosis
  and subsequent management. Neoplasm is common, but cancer history or fluid appearance alone does
  not establish malignant pericardial involvement.
- The ESC and ACC guidance support echocardiography as first-line imaging and an etiology-directed,
  longitudinal approach to complicated pericardial disease. One effusion diameter, drainage volume,
  output, or brief stable interval is not used here as a universal diagnostic, catheter-removal, or
  outcome threshold.

## Authored vignette

A 64-year-old woman with active lung adenocarcinoma is reviewed 2 hours after reported urgent
image-guided pericardiocentesis. Before drainage she had 10 days of progressive dyspnea and
orthopnea, HR 116/min, BP 88/64 mmHg, RR 24/min, SpO₂ 96% on room air, alert mentation, cool
extremities, elevated JVP, and authored pulsus paradoxus 16 mmHg. A fixed echo reported a 30 mm
circumferential effusion, right-atrial systolic and right-ventricular early-diastolic collapse, and
a plethoric IVC.

The prior team reports draining 420 mL of serosanguineous fluid and leaving a pericardial catheter.
Current fixed findings are HR 88/min, BP 116/72 mmHg, RR 18/min, SpO₂ 97% on room air, improved
dyspnea, alert mentation, warm perfusion, and an 8 mm residual effusion without chamber collapse.
Cytology and selected microbiology remain pending. After elapsed review, a fixed later report gives
HR 90/min, BP 114/70 mmHg, RR 18/min, SpO₂ 97% on room air, 55 mL additional reported catheter
output, and a 9 mm residual effusion without chamber collapse. The short stable interval does not
prove etiology, cure, freedom from recurrence, catheter-removal readiness, disposition, or outcome.

## Exclusions

The browser does not take a history or examine; acquire or interpret ECG, monitoring, echo, other
imaging, catheter findings, output, or specimens; diagnose tamponade or its cause; select or deliver
fluid, medication, drainage, surgery, or another treatment; choose an approach or device; manipulate
or remove a catheter; manage pericardial-decompression syndrome, bleeding, infection,
effusive-constrictive disease, or recurrence; determine disposition or prognosis; or predict outcome.
