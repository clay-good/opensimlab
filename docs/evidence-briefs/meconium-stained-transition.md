# Meconium-stained newborn transition

Status: source-checked authoring brief; clinical review remains unsigned.
Checked: August 26, 2026.
Scope: selective-airway-clearing recognition during vigorous transition, not suction, airway, ventilation, or meconium-aspiration treatment instruction.

The 2025 AHA/AAP guideline does not recommend routine oral, nasal, oropharyngeal, or endotracheal suctioning at birth, whether fluid is clear or meconium stained. A newborn who breathes well or cries can remain in protected skin-to-skin care and does not need suction solely because meconium is present. Unnecessary suction can induce bradycardia, apnea, desaturation, and airway injury. Mouth or nose suction may be considered when ventilation is required and the airway appears obstructed; intubation and tracheal suction may help when apparent tracheal obstruction makes ventilation ineffective despite corrective steps.
