# STEMI recognition and first actions evidence brief

## Source floor

- The 2025 US multisociety acute-coronary-syndrome guideline keeps rapid reperfusion-system activation and antiplatelet therapy central to STEMI care. Treatment choices still require patient, bleeding, contraindication, and system context.
- The AHA STEMI systems-of-care policy supports regional protocols, one-call activation, automatic receiving-center acceptance, and planned transport between referring and receiving settings.
- AHA first-aid guidance supports immediate EMS activation for heart-attack symptoms rather than private transport. It does not support routine oxygen for acute coronary syndrome without hypoxemia.

## Authored vignette

A fictional patient develops 22 minutes of ongoing pressure, diaphoresis, and nausea in a non-PCI cardiology clinic. A fixed diagnostic 12-lead report states an inferior STEMI pattern. BP is 128/76 mmHg, HR 62/min, and SpO₂ 96% on room air, without authored shock, acute heart failure, sustained arrhythmia, new murmur, aortic-dissection pattern, active bleeding, aspirin allergy, or recent aspirin use. Right-ventricular involvement and evolving bradyarrhythmia, atrioventricular block, or another complication remain open receiving-pathway questions.

The learner reconciles the fixed pattern, screens danger in parallel, activates EMS and the regional reperfusion pathway without waiting for biomarkers, records protocol-bounded aspirin and monitored-transport intent, then reassesses and hands off exact trajectory data. This is not the Emergency Medicine PCI-capable vignette: it rehearses safe recognition and system routing from an outpatient non-PCI setting, not ED antithrombotic preparation for immediate primary PCI.

## Exclusions

The lab does not examine; acquire or interpret an ECG, biomarker, or another test; diagnose a real patient; prescribe or deliver aspirin or another drug; select a P2Y12 inhibitor, anticoagulant, fibrinolytic, or reperfusion strategy; perform PCI or another procedure; determine disposition; or predict complications or outcome. The reported response is fixed and does not prove treatment effect.
