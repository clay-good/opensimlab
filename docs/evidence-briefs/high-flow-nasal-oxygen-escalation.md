# High-flow nasal oxygen escalation

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate Respiratory Medicine lab rehearses escalating persistent de novo nonhypercapnic
hypoxemia from verified conventional oxygen to a closely monitored high-flow nasal oxygen trial,
then using an elapsed whole-patient and blood-gas response to preserve failure guards and
airway-capable rescue readiness. It is a support-selection lesson, not oxygen-device training.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2026 ATS guideline strongly recommends HFNC for adults with acute hypoxemic respiratory
  failure and conditionally recommends NIV or CPAP, with close monitoring for escalation. The lab
  uses that hierarchy for one authored, spontaneously breathing, nonhypercapnic patient; it does not
  claim HFNO is always the correct modality.
- The 2022 ERS guideline conditionally suggests HFNC over conventional oxygen in hypoxemic acute
  respiratory failure and conditionally suggests HFNC over NIV in that population. It separately
  favors trialing NIV before HFNC in COPD hypercapnic failure, which is why that physiology remains
  in the preceding bilevel-NIV lab.
- Prolonging failing noninvasive support can delay needed invasive support. Early improvement in
  comfort, speech, work, respiratory rate, oxygenation, and gas exchange therefore supports only
  monitored continuation, never automatic success or reduced observation.
- Reservoir-mask flow does not establish a precise delivered FiO₂. The monitor's nominal proxy is
  not used to calculate a PaO₂/FiO₂ ratio, and no ROX calculation or cutoff is exposed.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine; acquire or interpret blood gas, imaging, oximetry, capnography, or
another test; diagnose; inspect an oxygen source or device; calculate ROX or a PaO₂/FiO₂ ratio;
select or deliver oxygen, medication, suction, mask ventilation, intubation, or treatment; choose a
device, cannula, fit, flow, temperature, humidification, FiO₂, oxygen target, NIV mode, interface,
pressure, PEEP, backup rate, trigger, or cycle; operate respiratory equipment; perform proning or
another procedure; determine weaning, disposition, or prognosis; or predict durable success or
outcome. Conventional support, qualified HFNO delivery, and both physiologic panels are fixed reports.
