# Hypoxemic community-acquired pneumonia evidence brief

## Teaching claim

This bounded lesson rehearses urgent reassessment of a spontaneously breathing adult with a radiographically supported community-acquired pneumonia pattern and corroborated hypoxemia. The learner integrates oxygenation, respiratory work, mentation, perfusion, pneumonia evidence, dangerous alternatives, whole-patient severity, testing and empiric-treatment ownership, and higher-acuity handoff.

## Evidence anchors

- The 2019 ATS/IDSA adult CAP guideline defines severe CAP as 1 major criterion or at least 3 minor criteria. The fixed case authors 3 minor criteria: RR at least 30/min, PaO₂/FiO₂ no greater than 250, and multilobar infiltrates. The count informs higher-acuity judgment but is not a browser disposition rule.
- ATS/IDSA guidance supports prompt empiric treatment and indication-sensitive microbiologic testing. Actual choices depend on severity, local resistance, allergy, organ function, recent exposures, viral results, contraindications, and current local guidance.
- The 2025 ATS focused update changes selected diagnostic and treatment questions. This lesson therefore records patient-specific intent and ownership without exposing a universal antibiotic, duration, steroid, viral-positive, or imaging recipe.

## Authored calibration

Symptoms, examination observations, pulse-oximeter quality, room-air blood gas, chest radiograph, laboratory reports, resistant-pathogen risk history, and severe-CAP criteria are fixed teaching data. They support pattern and urgency rehearsal; the learner does not acquire or interpret them. No treatment-response panel follows, because rapid improvement cannot be attributed to an antibiotic and this slice does not model oxygen-device selection.

## Boundaries

The browser does not examine, sample, image, calculate a patient-care score, diagnose a pathogen, prescribe or deliver oxygen or treatment, select a support device or setting, choose an antimicrobial regimen, perform a procedure, determine disposition, or predict outcome. Real care requires verified patient data, current local protocols, qualified respiratory and critical-care teams, antimicrobial stewardship, continuous reassessment, and patient-specific judgment.
