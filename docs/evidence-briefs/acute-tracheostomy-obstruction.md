# Acute tracheostomy obstruction

## Intended rehearsal

This advanced Respiratory Medicine lab rehearses anatomy-first recognition of urgent tracheostomy
patency failure, immediate expert help and oxygenation to both possible airways in a patient whose
native upper airway is documented patent, review of a declared dual-cannula device pathway, and
elapsed reassessment and handoff. It is not suction, tube-change, or ventilation training.

## Evidence boundary

- The National Tracheostomy Safety Project patent-upper-airway algorithm begins with airway-expert
  help, assessment of both mouth and tracheostomy, oxygen to both face and tracheostomy when the
  patient is breathing, removal of an inner tube when present, catheter-passage assessment, and
  waveform capnography. This lab represents those steps as fixed experienced-team findings and
  actions, never learner physical technique.
- The multidisciplinary NTSP guideline emphasizes that a tracheostomy patient can have 2 possible
  airways, that oxygenation takes priority, and that early simple reversible causes are addressed
  before more invasive rescue. The lab therefore declares the upper-airway anatomy and exact device
  rather than generalizing this branch to laryngectomy or every tracheostomy.
- The AARC adult tracheostomy guideline supports multidisciplinary tracheostomy teams and protocol
  bundles. It supports the named-owner, equipment-readiness, emergency-plan, and handoff lane, not a
  universal acute maneuver sequence.
- The engine keeps tracheostomy patency distinct from an oral tracheal tube. The authored occlusion
  reduces canonical gas flow, capnography, oxygenation, and ventilation; the qualified-team action
  restores that path. The fixed 2-minute report supports immediate restoration only.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine; acquire or interpret capnography, oximetry, imaging, or another test;
diagnose; select oxygen source, interface, flow, fraction, target, humidification, drug, ventilation,
or treatment; remove a cap, valve, cannula, or tube; pass a catheter; suction; change a cuff; exchange
or reinsert a tube; ventilate the face or stoma; intubate; perform bronchoscopy or another procedure;
deliver treatment; decide disposition or prognosis; or predict durable resolution or outcome. The
case does not apply to laryngectomy, a fresh stoma, another tube design, upper-airway obstruction,
ventilator dependence, or loss of pulse or breathing.
