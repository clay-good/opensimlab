# Beta-blocker toxicity: perfusion is more than pulse rate

## Scope and distinctness

This lab rehearses a declared immediate-release metoprolol exposure with symptomatic bradycardia,
cardiogenic shock, impaired consciousness, and low glucose. It is distinct from intrinsic conduction
disease, isolated hypoglycemia, calcium-channel-blocker toxicity, digoxin toxicity, and a generic
bradycardia or pacing algorithm.

## Fixed fixture

- A 38-year-old woman arrives 2 hours after a witnessed metoprolol-tartrate-only ingestion. She is
  drowsy but arousable after vomiting. HR is 42/min, BP 72/40 mmHg, RR 18/min, room-air SpO2 97%,
  and T 36.5°C.
- A qualified ECG report supplies sinus bradycardia, PR 220 ms, and QRS 92 ms. A qualified focused
  cardiac report supplies globally reduced LV systolic contraction without pulmonary edema.
  Supplied glucose is 62 mg/dL, pH 7.31, bicarbonate 19 mmol/L, lactate 3.8 mmol/L, sodium
  138 mmol/L, potassium 4.2 mmol/L, and creatinine 0.8 mg/dL. The treating-team report supplies
  persistent shock after protocol-selected atropine and initial vasopressor care.
- A strict fixed 45-minute report supplies clearer mentation, HR 58/min, BP 98/60 mmHg, glucose
  104 mg/dL, potassium 3.5 mmol/L, and lactate 2.8 mmol/L without new hypoxemia or pulmonary edema.

## Evidence application

The AHA describes beta-blocker poisoning as a pattern of reduced contractility, bradycardia, AV nodal
blockade, hypotension, and sometimes hypoglycemia. Current recommendations pair vasopressors with
high-dose insulin/euglycemia therapy when hypotension is refractory, consider glucagon for symptomatic
bradycardia or hypotension, and reserve extracorporeal support for refractory cardiogenic shock. The
lab therefore records qualified intent and surveillance, never a learner-selected product or dose.

DailyMed identifies severe bradycardia, hypotension, cardiogenic shock, AV block, heart failure,
bronchospasm, hypoxia, and impaired consciousness as possible metoprolol-overdose manifestations.
The lab never makes one pulse, ECG interval, glucose value, or reported exposure independently
diagnostic.

## Boundaries

- Exposure, ECG, cardiac-function report, laboratory evidence, prior care, and response are authored.
- No vasopressor, glucagon, insulin, glucose, electrolyte, fluid, dose, rate, target, access, airway,
  pacing, dialysis, lipid, ECLS, or rescue control is exposed.
- The fixed response does not prove treatment effect, durable perfusion, glucose or electrolyte
  stability, coingestant exclusion, safe disposition, or outcome.
