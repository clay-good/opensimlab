# HHS: follow the whole trajectory

This preview rehearses hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state (HHS) recognition, cautious correction, and reassessment in an older adult with heart and kidney disease. It teaches why lower glucose and rising sodium cannot be interpreted separately from osmolality, circulation, urine output, and cognition. There is no learner dosing or fluid calculator.

Sources checked August 26, 2026:

- The 2024 international consensus describes a glucose fall no faster than 90–120 mg/dL/h, sodium fall no faster than 10 mmol/L in 24 hours, and osmolality decline of 3–8 mOsm/kg/h. A sodium rise during glucose correction does not alone justify hypotonic fluid. Older adults and people with heart or kidney disease need individualized cautious fluid replacement. HHS resolution includes osmolality below 300 mOsm/kg, improved cognition, adequate urine output, and corrected hyperglycemia, not glucose alone. [Fluid therapy and resolution sections](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00125-024-06183-8).
- JBDS emphasizes early senior review, fluid-led initial correction unless significant ketonemia requires another pathway, serial osmolality and fluid balance, and prevention of hypoglycemia, hypokalemia, thrombosis, pressure injury, and fluid overload. Its glucose ceiling is 5 mmol/L/h and its resolution definition requires cognition back to the premorbid state. [Sections 4.3–4.6 and recovery](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10107355/).

The fictional 4-hour panel has glucose 900→540 mg/dL, sodium 146→149 mmol/L, and BUN 56→42 mg/dL. Authored total osmolality is 362→343 mOsm/kg, using 2 × sodium + glucose/18 + BUN/2.8; effective osmolality is 342→328 mOsm/kg. These imply average declines of 90 mg/dL/h and 4.75 mOsm/kg/h, compatible with both sources. They are illustrative checkpoints, not modeled kinetics or proof that correction stayed safe between measurements. Persistent hyperosmolality, urine output 0.4 mL/kg/h, and cognition below baseline keep resolution open.

Local protocols differ in insulin timing, glucose targets, sodium-correction coefficients, monitoring intervals, and resolution definitions. No universal fluid, insulin, electrolyte, anticoagulant, dose, rate, route, nutrition, or disposition prescription is supplied. The references support factual teaching boundaries; no source diagram, algorithm, or text is reproduced. Independent exact-version clinical and face-validity review remain pending.

## Guidance and worked example

Six observed-state prompts read the learner's own recorded steps and nothing else. This lesson can
be failed twice with the same error, so they refuse the reassurance in both places it is offered:
ketones of 1.1 mmol/L and a pH of 7.36 answer whether this is ketoacidosis rather than whether it
is serious, and a lower glucose with a better pressure and a falling osmolality is not resolution
while hyperosmolality, urine output of 0.4 mL/kg/h, and cognition below baseline persist. No prompt
says whether she is getting better. Unassisted is silent; coached withholds the single non-urgent
beat, the authored four-hour interval.

The optional worked example (controller 0.1.0) performs the same six ordinary decisions through the
real engine, pausing between each, and finishes at handoff on what is still moving rather than on
what has improved. It treats the quoted average falls of 90 mg/dL/h of glucose and 4.75 mOsm/kg/h
of total osmolality as a description of this authored contrast rather than a target or a safe
interval between measurements. The prompts carry no external link, because these sources are
declared as full citations and a URL built from a citation is a construction rather than a lookup.
