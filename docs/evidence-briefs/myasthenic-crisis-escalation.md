# Myasthenic crisis escalation

## Decision boundary

This lab teaches rapid fatigable bulbar and respiratory deterioration in established generalized
myasthenia. Preserved oxygen saturation, near-normal carbon dioxide, or one respiratory-mechanics
value does not establish safety. Serial whole-patient change triggers early qualified neurocritical
and airway-capable ownership. A later fixed report supplies the need for invasive ventilation and
therefore establishes the authored manifest-crisis transition. The learner does not manage the
airway, ventilator, IVIG, plasma exchange, antimicrobials, or another treatment.

The case is distinct from the Respiratory Medicine ALS lab, which owns slow longitudinal decline,
home respiratory-support evaluation, cough planning, preferences, and multidisciplinary follow-up.

## Evidence and authored values

International consensus defines impending crisis as rapid worsening likely to lead to crisis and
manifest crisis by ventilatory support required for respiratory or bulbar dysfunction. Current
reviews emphasize that bulbar weakness can compromise the upper airway and that no single prediction
tool is sufficiently sensitive and specific. Trajectory, cough, secretion handling, neck strength,
paradoxical breathing, speech, and serial pulmonary-function measures should be integrated.

The 45-year-old patient, 36-hour and 6-hour clocks, FVC and MIP values, blood gases, fever, opacity,
and minute-30 deterioration are authored teaching evidence. They are not diagnostic thresholds or
patient-specific recommendations. The opacity is compatible with infection or aspiration but proves
neither. The supplied qualified-team intubation is a scenario fact, not a learner procedure or proof
of response, weaning success, recovery, prognosis, or outcome.
