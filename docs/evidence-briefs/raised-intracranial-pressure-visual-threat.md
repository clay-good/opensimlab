# Raised intracranial pressure with visual threat

## Decision boundary

This lab teaches subacute raised-pressure recognition and sight preservation, not monitored traumatic
ICP treatment. New pressure-pattern headache, pulsatile tinnitus, transient visual obscurations,
diplopia, specialist-confirmed papilledema, and measured visual function prompt urgent neurological
and neuro-ophthalmic ownership. Supplied MRI with venography excludes authored structural and venous
causes before a qualified LP. The learner does not examine, test, diagnose, prescribe, perform LP,
select a vision procedure, or deliver treatment.

The case remains distinct from the next herniation lab: GCS stays 15, pupils remain equal, physiology
is stable, and no Cushing pattern, posturing, or new persistent motor deficit is authored. The urgent
threat is worsening peripheral visual function despite preserved 20/20 central acuity.

## Evidence and authored values

The 2018 consensus guideline emphasizes specialist confirmation of papilledema when uncertain,
formal visual acuity and field assessment, urgent brain imaging with venography to exclude cerebral
venous sinus thrombosis, LP after safe imaging, and urgent qualified surgical management when visual
function declines. The 2020 update reinforces that demographics and a single opening-pressure value
are insufficient in isolation, normal CSF composition and secondary-cause exclusion matter, and
rapidly progressive visual loss requires urgent sight-preservation intervention.

The 31-year-old patient, symptoms, weight history, examinations, photography, OCT, perimetry, MRI,
venography, LP position, opening pressure 34 cm CSF, normal CSF, qualified care, and strict 24-hour
field decline are authored teaching evidence. They do not diagnose a real patient, establish an
idiopathic cause, choose a medicine or procedure, prove visual rescue, or predict recovery,
disposition, prognosis, or outcome.
