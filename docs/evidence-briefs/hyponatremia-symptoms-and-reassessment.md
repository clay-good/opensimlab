## Selected pathway and diagnostic caution

The selected [Society for Endocrinology 2022 guidance](https://www.endocrinology.org/media/xhrhxhxm/emergency-management-of-severe-and-moderately-severely-symptomatic-hyponatraemia-in-adult-patients-2022.pdf)
supports symptom-led rescue and qualified additional correction when an initial +5 mmol/L rise
does not improve symptoms. Its treatment and diagnostic figures were rendered and inspected:
they informed parallel investigation and continued expert review, not an automatic +6 stopping rule.
No dose, product concentration, access, route, or infusion rate is prescribed.

The [European joint 2014 guideline](https://www.ese-hormones.org/publications/directory/ese-clinical-guideline-for-the-management-of-hyponatraemia/),
checked against the [official ERBP field version](https://www.european-renal-best-practice.org/sites/default/files/u33/short%20version%20hyponatraemia%20English%20FINAL.pdf),
requires contextual interpretation of contemporaneous blood and urine findings. Diuretic exposure
confounds a simple SIAD inference. Unknown duration remains unknown despite recent symptoms.
Its moderately symptomatic correction schedule differs from the selected 2022 first-hour pathway;
other qualified individualized schedules are not globally graded unsafe. US/GB controls share this
selected fiction and do not establish a completed regional matrix.

[Sterns et al., CJASN 2024](https://doi.org/10.2215/CJN.0000000000000244) reinforces correction
safeguards and continued sodium and urine-output surveillance. Correction ceilings are not routine
targets. Poor intake alone is not severe malnutrition or a high-risk classification. This is a
review, not a new guideline. The lesson is distinct from initial seizure rescue and from the
endocrine post-rescue aquaresis/overcorrection scenario.
