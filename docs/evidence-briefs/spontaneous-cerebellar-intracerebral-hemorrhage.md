# Spontaneous cerebellar intracerebral hemorrhage evidence brief

## Decision boundary

This Neurology lab begins after stroke-system activation with supplied CT. A previously independent
adult is initially awake with an 11 mL cerebellar hemorrhage and fourth-ventricle effacement but no
hydrocephalus, brainstem compression, or herniation. The learner reconciles the trajectory, reviews
the fixed posterior-fossa context, recognizes the confined-space escalation boundary, and activates
qualified neurocritical, neurosurgical, and airway-capable ownership before a strict-later fixed
neurological, airway, and imaging deterioration. Another elapsed handoff preserves active risk.

Emergency Medicine owns deteriorating warfarin-associated thalamic ICH, reversal intent, pressure
control, and transfer. Critical Care owns monitored post-traumatic ICP/CPP and hyperosmolar rescue.
This lesson owns early posterior-fossa danger without drug, pressure, monitor, drain, surgery,
airway, treatment, or outcome controls.

## Fixed teaching facts

- A previously independent 67-year-old woman, 68 kg, develops witnessed abrupt vertigo, vomiting,
  dysarthria, and severe truncal ataxia 75 minutes earlier. No trauma or antithrombotic exposure is
  authored.
- A supplied qualified examination reports that she is awake, oriented, unable to sit or stand
  unsupported, and has dysarthria and right-limb dysmetria. No weakness, sensory loss, seizure, or
  loss of consciousness is authored. She breathes spontaneously with a present cough.
- Temperature is 36.7°C, HR 78/min in sinus rhythm, RR 18/min, BP 168/92 mmHg (MAP 117), room-air
  SpO₂ 97%, and supplied glucose 112 mg/dL.
- Fixed CT reports an 11 mL right cerebellar ICH with fourth-ventricle effacement but no initial
  hydrocephalus, brainstem compression, or herniation.
- At a strict later report, she is increasingly drowsy after recurrent vomiting, opens her eyes to
  repeated voice, continues spontaneous breathing, and has a weaker supplied cough. Temperature is
  36.7°C, HR 82/min, RR 20/min, BP 176/96 mmHg (MAP 123), and room-air SpO₂ 95%.
- Fixed repeat CT reports expansion to 14 mL with new obstructive hydrocephalus and brainstem
  compression. No herniation is authored. Qualified ownership is already active.
- Etiology, further expansion, edema, airway course, treatment, procedure, disposition, prognosis,
  and outcome remain open.

All exact values and the later trajectory are fictional face-valid fixtures. The sources support
the qualitative escalation boundary, not these exact values, one universal procedure, functional
benefit, or an individual prediction.

## Source synthesis

The 2022 AHA/ASA guideline emphasizes confined posterior-fossa risk and recommends urgent surgical
removal, with or without ventricular drainage, to reduce mortality when cerebellar ICH is accompanied
by neurological deterioration, brainstem compression, obstructive hydrocephalus, or volume at least
15 mL. Evidence for functional-outcome improvement is uncertain and largely nonrandomized. This
scenario never turns that recommendation into a learner procedure choice.

The 2024 AHA/ASA performance measures translate the guideline into a systems-of-care expectation
for deteriorating cerebellar ICH at neurosurgical-capable centers while retaining patient, medical,
capability, and goals-of-care exceptions. The lesson teaches early qualified ownership, not
automatic surgery or a universal treatment plan.

## Explicit nonclaims

- The learner does not take a history; examine; calculate GCS, NIHSS, ICH score, hemorrhage volume,
  or another score; measure glucose or vitals; acquire or interpret imaging or tests; diagnose
  hemorrhage, expansion, hydrocephalus, brainstem compression, or etiology.
- No drug, blood-pressure target, reversal, oxygen, airway device, ventilation, drain, surgery,
  transfer mechanics, procedure, or treatment is selected or delivered.
- Eleven or 14 mL is not an isolated universal procedural threshold. Absent initial hydrocephalus
  does not establish safety, and a weaker cough does not define one airway procedure.
- Negative snapshots do not permanently exclude trauma, antithrombotic exposure, seizure, another
  cause, expansion, edema, herniation, or deterioration.
- No disposition, prognosis, neurological recovery, functional benefit, durable stability, or
  outcome is selected, predicted, or reported.
