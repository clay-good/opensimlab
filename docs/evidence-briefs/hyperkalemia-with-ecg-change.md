# Evidence brief: Hyperkalemia with ECG change

## Intended rehearsal

This advanced adult emergency-department case rehearses confirming severe hyperkalemia and ECG
toxicity, protecting the myocardium before shifting potassium, pairing insulin-glucose with glucose
surveillance and an adjunct beta-2 agonist, controlling drivers, arranging potassium removal and a
dialysis contingency, and reassessing ECG, potassium, glucose, kidney state, and rebound risk.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2023 UK Kidney Association guideline defines potassium at least 6.5 mmol/L as severe and
  recommends emergency treatment and expert help. A 12-lead ECG and cardiac monitoring are advised
  from potassium 6.0 mmol/L. This case authors a confirmed 7.1 mmol/L result and ECG toxicity.
- IV calcium salt is used when ECG changes are present to stabilize the myocardium. It does not
  lower serum potassium. The intent click changes nothing; a separate later treating-team report
  supplies the improved ECG while potassium remains 7.1 mmol/L.
- Insulin-glucose shifts potassium intracellularly and requires structured glucose surveillance;
  a nebulized beta-2 agonist may be an adjunct but not sole therapy. The case records no dose.
- Shifting is temporary. Potassium removal, driver control, renal expertise, and a dialysis
  contingency remain necessary. Shifting and removal lanes may proceed in parallel after immediate
  myocardial-protection intent, followed only after elapsed time by serial potassium, glucose, ECG,
  and rebound review.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine the patient, validate a specimen, acquire or interpret ECG or laboratory
data, select calcium salt or dose, prescribe or deliver insulin-glucose or a beta-2 agonist, prevent
or treat hypoglycemia, calculate potassium movement, choose or deliver fluid, binder or diuretic,
obtain dialysis access, perform dialysis, manage arrest, model later rebound, or assess team skill,
disposition, recurrence, prognosis, or outcome.
