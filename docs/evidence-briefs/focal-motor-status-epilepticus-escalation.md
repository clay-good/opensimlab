# Focal motor status epilepticus escalation

## Training boundary

This lab rehearses one easy-to-miss transition: a focal-to-bilateral convulsion becomes less
dramatic after supplied qualified initial rescue care, but continuous stereotyped unilateral face
and arm clonus remains visible and meaningful responsiveness has not returned. Reduced movement is
not authored seizure resolution.

The event is deliberately 18 minutes long so recognition does not depend on inventing one universal
focal-motor-status clock. The ILAE framework classifies status by semiology and acknowledges that
operational time points vary by seizure type and remain incompletely evidenced. The 2024 ACEP adult
policy is treatment-focused on generalized convulsive status, but its evidence review defines
clinically apparent seizure activity as visible movements judged consistent with focal or generalized
seizures. Together these sources support urgent recognition and qualified ownership while preserving
the treatment boundary.

## Authored trajectory

- A previously independent 58-year-old has one continuous evolving 18-minute event.
- Initial left face and arm clonus progressed to bilateral convulsions. After supplied qualified
  initial rescue care, bilateral movement recedes but overt left face and arm clonus continues.
- Initial T 37.2°C, HR 118/min, RR 22/min between motor bursts, BP 132/80 mmHg (MAP 97),
  pulse-coherent room-air SpO2 96%, glucose 104 mg/dL, central pulse, spontaneous chest rise, warm
  perfusion, and 2-second refill are fixed reports.
- At fixed minute 26, visible focal clonus and absent meaningful recovery persist; T 37.3°C,
  HR 122/min, RR 23/min between motor bursts, BP 128/78 mmHg (MAP 95), and room-air SpO2 95% are
  authored snapshots, not predicted physiology or treatment effect.
- Structural, vascular, infectious, immune, toxic, metabolic, medication-related, epilepsy-related,
  and nonepileptic alternatives remain open.

## What the learner can do

Six serial actions reconcile the supplied trajectory, recognize overt focal motor status, activate
qualified seizure and airway-capable ownership, review whole-patient safety and open causes, inspect
a strict later visible-motor report, and hand off active seizure and uncertainty. The last two steps
require separate elapsed ticks. Exactly one action is shown at each of seven states.

## Hard limits

- No learner history, examination, seizure timing, monitoring, glucose acquisition, test, imaging,
  laboratory, or EEG acquisition or interpretation is represented.
- No medicine, product, dose, route, access, oxygen, airway device, ventilation, infusion,
  anesthetic, or procedure is selected or delivered.
- The lab never diagnoses nonconvulsive status from impaired recovery alone and supplies no EEG
  state. The next Neurology lab retains the no-visible-movement, EEG-based recognition boundary.
- No cause, injury, treatment effect, movement cessation, electrographic control, durable recovery,
  disposition, prognosis, recurrence exclusion, or outcome is claimed.
