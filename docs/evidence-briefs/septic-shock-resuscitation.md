# Septic-shock resuscitation

## Intended rehearsal

Reassess persistent ICU septic shock after reported early antimicrobials, initial crystalloid, and
vasopressor support. Join serial tissue perfusion, lactate context, a fixed dynamic fluid-response
test, lung tolerance, individualized hemodynamic support, and urgent source control rather than
using MAP or fluid volume alone as closure.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2026 Surviving Sepsis Campaign treats sepsis and septic shock as emergencies, advises
  frequent ongoing reassessment during fluid resuscitation, supports dynamic measures over static
  measures alone, serial lactate in context, capillary refill as an adjunct, norepinephrine as the
  first-line vasopressor, and early source control when an anatomic source requires it.
- The 2025 ESICM circulatory-shock guideline emphasizes serial tissue-perfusion assessment,
  dynamic predictors of fluid responsiveness, targeted rather than liberal fluid, and further
  hemodynamic characterization in nonresponders.
- This authored patient has a fixed 2% stroke-volume change with passive leg raise and new diffuse
  B-lines after reported initial fluid. Those are case facts supporting a no-blind-repeat-fluid
  decision here, not universal thresholds or a bedside measurement performed by the screen.

## Exclusions

The lab does not examine the patient; acquire or interpret pressure, perfusion, laboratory,
ultrasound, or ventilator data; calculate a dose; select or deliver fluid, antimicrobial,
vasopressor, inotrope, steroid, oxygen, or ventilation; perform biliary drainage or another
procedure; diagnose the pathogen or shock phenotype; determine disposition; or predict outcome.
