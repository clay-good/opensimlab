# Shoulder dystocia cognitive sequence evidence brief

## Decision this lab supports

Recognize the emergency after the head has delivered and gentle traction has failed, activate a prepared team and clock, avoid harmful force, and keep a flexible qualified maneuver sequence, newborn readiness, communication, documentation, and post-birth risk in one calm mental model.

## Evidence used

- ACOG Practice Bulletin 178, *Shoulder Dystocia* (2017, reaffirmed 2024), describes an unpredictable emergency for which a systematic approach, simulation, and documentation can improve performance.
- RCOG Green-top Guideline 42 (2012) defines shoulder dystocia by the need for additional maneuvers after head delivery and failed gentle traction. It notes that a head-to-body interval over 60 seconds has been proposed as an objective definition but is not routinely collected, so this lab does not wait for that threshold.
- The 2024 DGGG/OEGGG/SGGG guideline requires early communication, experienced leadership, stopping pushing during repositioning, no fundal pressure, and avoidance of forced traction or external rotation. It explicitly states that first-line maneuver choice depends on the obstetric situation and supports McRoberts with or without suprapubic pressure.

## Authored boundaries

The presentation, failed gentle traction, qualified maneuvers, 2-minute-10-second case-specific birth, and every maternal or newborn report are fixtures. Controls record cognition and coordination only. They do not teach psychomotor competence, make a universal maneuver order, or simulate delivery, injury, resuscitation, hemorrhage, treatment effect, disposition, or outcome.

## Review status

Draft and unsigned. The RCOG 2012 guideline remains the published edition while a replacement is in development; the lab therefore avoids disputed numeric deadlines and binds its current maneuver-sequence boundary to the more recent 2024 DGGG/OEGGG/SGGG guideline and ACOG's reaffirmed bulletin.
