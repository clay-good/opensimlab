# Evidence brief: suspected amniotic fluid embolism pattern

## Decision this lesson rehearses

Recognize a supplied rapid maternal cardiorespiratory-collapse-and-coagulopathy pattern shortly
after birth, keep amniotic fluid embolism and dangerous alternatives open, and activate coordinated
qualified obstetric, anesthesia, critical-care, cardiopulmonary, hemorrhage, newborn and support
ownership immediately. The lesson preserves a pulse throughout so that pregnancy-associated cardiac
arrest remains a separate catalog decision.

## Source basis

1. Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine Patient Safety and Quality Committee. *Checklist for initial
   management of amniotic fluid embolism—Updated 2026*. Pregnancy 2026;2:e70364. Consulted August
   26, 2026. This supports the sudden peripartum cardiorespiratory-collapse pattern, low-clutter
   cognitive aid, coordinated facility response, hemorrhage and coagulopathy readiness, separate
   arrest pathway, and patient, family and staff support.
2. Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine; Pacheco LD, Saade G, Hankins GDV, Clark SL. *Amniotic fluid
   embolism: diagnosis and management*. Am J Obstet Gynecol 2016;215:B16–B24. PMID 26987420.
   This supports differential recognition, the absence of one confirmatory laboratory test,
   multidisciplinary care, cardiopulmonary support and early coagulation and bleeding assessment.
3. Clark SL, Romero R, Dildy GA, et al. *Proposed diagnostic criteria for the case definition of
   amniotic fluid embolism in research studies*. Am J Obstet Gynecol 2016;215:408–412. PMID
   27372270. This supports the classic combined cardiorespiratory and overt-DIC research phenotype
   and, critically, the warning that these criteria are for consistent research reporting rather
   than a learner-applied bedside diagnostic score.
4. American Heart Association. *2025 Guidelines for CPR and ECC: Adult and Pediatric Special
   Circumstances of Resuscitation*, section 19.4. Consulted August 26, 2026. This supports early
   qualified hemorrhage-system ownership for life-threatening suspected AFE, resource-dependent
   rescue planning, and avoiding an unproven routine atropine-ondansetron-ketorolac regimen. Products,
   devices, doses and arrest actions remain outside learner controls.

## Implemented teaching bounds

- The opening report is authored 12 minutes after birth and placental delivery: 8 minutes earlier,
  a 32-year-old woman developed abrupt dyspnea, cyanosis, hypotension and reduced responsiveness
  before major visible bleeding. Qualified staff report a central pulse, HR 132/min, BP 74/42 mmHg,
  RR 34/min, pulse-coherent room-air SpO2 78%, T 36.8°C, a firm uterus and 180 mL cumulative measured
  loss at event onset. A sample drawn when loss was 240 mL supplies platelets 68 × 10^9/L from 221,
  fibrinogen 105 mg/dL from 430, PT 18.6 seconds, INR 1.7, aPTT 52 seconds, hemoglobin 10.6 g/dL from
  12.1 and lactate 5.4 mmol/L. The newborn is with a qualified neonatal team.
- No arrest, seizure, fever, rash, wheeze, focal neurologic deficit or focal surgical bleeding source
  is authored. Pulmonary thromboembolism, air embolism, myocardial or other cardiac disease,
  anesthetic complication, anaphylaxis, sepsis, hemorrhage and other causes remain open.
- The fixed report authored as 12 minutes after activation preserves a central pulse with HR
  124/min, BP 86/50 mmHg, RR 28/min, pulse-coherent SpO2 94% on unspecified qualified-team support
  and T 36.7°C. She opens her eyes to voice but does not reliably follow commands; perfusion remains
  cool and mottled. Cumulative vaginal loss is 850 mL and rising, the uterus remains firm, vascular-
  access sites ooze, and repeat platelets are 44 × 10^9/L, fibrinogen 72 mg/dL, PT 23.4 seconds,
  INR 2.2, aPTT 68 seconds, hemoglobin 8.8 g/dL and lactate 6.0 mmol/L. This is persistent shock,
  respiratory compromise and progressive coagulopathy and hemorrhage, not recovery.
- The six controls only record whole-pattern reconciliation, nonexclusive pattern recognition,
  coordinated qualified response activation, supplied evidence review, fixed later review and
  active-risk handoff. They expose no treatment, maneuver, diagnostic test or delivery choice.

## Explicit exclusions

The browser does not examine; assess a pulse, airway, breathing, circulation, uterus, bleeding or
newborn; measure or interpret vitals, loss, monitoring, ECG, echocardiography, coagulation, blood,
imaging or another test; diagnose or exclude amniotic fluid embolism or an alternative; apply the
research case definition as a bedside score; select or deliver oxygen, ventilation, intubation,
fluid, vasopressor, inotrope, pulmonary vasodilator, uterotonic, tranexamic acid, blood component,
fibrinogen replacement, drug, dose, route, rate, target, access, CPR, defibrillation, ECMO,
resuscitative hysterotomy, surgery or another procedure; choose transfer or disposition; or claim
treatment effect, durable respiratory or hemodynamic recovery, bleeding or coagulopathy control,
maternal outcome or newborn outcome.
