# Guillain-Barré respiratory decline

## Decision boundary

This lab teaches early recognition of rapidly worsening respiratory, bulbar, and autonomic risk in
an authored probable Guillain-Barré pattern. Preserved oxygen saturation, initially normal carbon
dioxide, a score, or one respiratory-mechanics value does not establish safety. The learner connects
functional speed, cough and secretion handling, swallowing, neck and facial weakness, breathing
pattern, serial measures, gas exchange, and autonomic lability; activates qualified neurocritical,
respiratory, airway-capable, nursing, and cardiac-monitoring ownership; reviews a fixed later decline;
and hands off unresolved risk. The learner does not diagnose, intubate, ventilate, select IVIG or
plasma exchange, or treat a rhythm or blood pressure.

The case is distinct from the Respiratory Medicine ALS lab, which owns slow longitudinal decline,
home-support and cough planning, preferences, and follow-up. It is also distinct from the myasthenic-
crisis lab, which owns rapidly fatigable junctional weakness in established myasthenia and a supplied
ventilation requirement that establishes manifest crisis.

## Evidence and authored values

The 2023 EAN/PNS guideline supports serial respiratory assessment, including FVC and single-breath
count, attention to bulbar and aspiration risk, cardiac monitoring for dysautonomia, and escalation
based on the whole trajectory. A 2024 critical-care review emphasizes that no individual prediction
tool is sufficiently sensitive and specific and that bulbar, neck, cough, paradoxical-breathing,
gas, and serial pulmonary-function evidence must be integrated.

The 33-year-old patient, 14-day post-diarrheal interval, 5-day sensory symptoms, 48-hour weakness
clock, functional losses, examination, reflexes, FVC, single-breath count, MIP, blood gases, CSF,
electrodiagnostic description, monitored heart-rate and pressure ranges, and fixed 4-hour decline are
authored teaching evidence. They are not diagnostic thresholds or patient-specific recommendations.
The CSF and electrodiagnostic reports support the authored probable pattern but do not independently
prove diagnosis or exclude dangerous alternatives. Autonomic ranges establish risk, not a mandate to
provoke or automatically treat each value. No airway choice, treatment response, durable stability,
recovery, disposition, prognosis, or outcome is supplied.
