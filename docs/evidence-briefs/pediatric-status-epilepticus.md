# Pediatric status epilepticus after first-line care evidence brief

## Decision boundary

This lab begins in the narrow space between first-line convulsive-status treatment and refractory
critical-care management. A child remains in bilateral generalized convulsive status after 2
supplied, qualified, documented appropriate first-line benzodiazepine doses. The learner reconciles
the clock and prior care, recognizes persistent convulsive status, activates qualified second-line
ownership without delay, keeps airway, dangerous-cause, monitoring, and refractory contingencies
active in parallel, reviews one fixed later report, and hands off unresolved risk.

The lab does not repeat the adult emergency lesson's product-and-dose action and does not enter the
critical-care lesson's fixed electrographic refractory status, continuous anesthetic, or EEG-target
work. It also differs from the stopped pediatric hypoglycemic- and febrile-seizure lessons: visible
bilateral convulsions are ongoing when this learner surface opens.

- A previously well 6-year-old girl is 20 kg and 115 cm. A first witnessed bilateral generalized

- The supplied experienced-team record verifies 2 documented appropriate weight-based first-line
  benzodiazepine doses at seizure minutes 5 and 10. Product, concentration, dose, route, access,
  preparation, and delivery details are intentionally absent and are not learner-selected,

- She has a pulse and spontaneous chest rise, but respiratory rate is not reliably countable during

- No fever, nonblanching rash, reported trauma, known ingestion, known epilepsy, or known diabetes

  structural, toxic, metabolic, medication, and other causes remain open.
- Qualified pediatric, neurology, nursing, pharmacy, airway-capable, critical-care, laboratory,
  imaging, and safeguarding teams own immediate second-line care, support, evaluation, surveillance,

- In the fixed minute-25 qualified report, no visible convulsions have been seen since minute 18.

fixtures. They are not predicted pediatric physiology or treatment response and must not be copied
into clinical care.

## Source synthesis

The current Royal Children's Hospital Melbourne acute-seizure guideline says most seizures stop
within 5 minutes, recommends pharmacological management when total duration is at least 5 minutes
or unknown, requires concurrent assessment and management, and says to count prehospital doses and
use no more than 2 appropriate benzodiazepine doses before advancing the pathway. It also keeps
reversible-cause assessment and individualized plans active. The scenario uses those principles but
does not reproduce its medication table or algorithm.

NICE NG217 recommendations 7.1.1-7.1.12 treat convulsive seizures lasting 5 minutes or more as an
emergency, advise a second benzodiazepine dose if the seizure persists 5-10 minutes after the first,
and move to intravenous second-line therapy after 2 benzodiazepine doses. NICE lists multiple
second-line options and preserves expert escalation if they fail. This lab records ownership only;
it does not choose a universal option.

The American Epilepsy Society evidence-based guideline supports a time-phased response with
stabilization and first-line benzodiazepine therapy followed by second-line therapy for persistent
convulsive status. Its child-and-adult scope supports the pathway boundary, not the fictional
patient values, qualified-care record, or visible-seizure response.

## Explicit nonclaims

- No child is examined, seizure-timed, monitored, glucose-tested, diagnosed, treated, counseled, or
  dispositioned by the learner.
- No first-line or second-line product, concentration, dose, route, access, preparation, infusion,
  or delivery is exposed or selected.
- No oxygen, suction, airway device, airway maneuver, ventilation, procedure, or treatment is
  selected or delivered.
- No laboratory test, EEG, imaging, lumbar puncture, or other investigation is acquired or
  interpreted.
- Fixed negative snapshots do not exclude infection, structural disease, trauma, ingestion,
  metabolic disturbance, medication cause, non-epileptic events, or another dangerous cause.
- Stopped visible movement does not establish electrographic control, treatment effect, recovery,
  recurrence exclusion, or freedom from refractory status.
- The lab does not assess team, airway, medication, seizure-first-aid, or caregiver-communication
  performance and does not determine disposition, prognosis, or outcome.
