# Neonatal bradycardia after adequate ventilation

Status: source-checked authoring brief; clinical review remains unsigned.
Checked: August 26, 2026.
Scope: compression-branch recognition and qualified-team coordination, not ventilation, compression, airway, access, or medication instruction.

The 2025 AHA/AAP guideline recommends chest compressions when a newborn heart rate remains below 60/min despite 30 seconds of ventilation that inflates the lungs, evidenced by chest movement. Ventilation should be optimized first, preferably through an alternative airway. During compressions, ventilation through an endotracheal tube is recommended; a laryngeal mask may be a reasonable alternative for newborns at least 34 weeks when intubation is not possible or unsuccessful. If heart rate has not increased to at least 60/min after optimized ventilation and compressions, intravascular epinephrine is recommended.

The authored case will supply persistent HR 48/min after qualified alternative-airway ventilation with visible chest movement, then a fixed qualified-team report after 60 seconds of coordinated compressions and ventilation with HR 74/min. The response value is a defensible teaching estimate, not a universal expected treatment effect. It keeps the epinephrine threshold visible without claiming that medication was indicated or delivered in the authored response.
