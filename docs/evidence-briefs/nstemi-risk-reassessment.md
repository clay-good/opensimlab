# NSTEMI risk reassessment

## Intended rehearsal

Reconcile a changing symptom, ECG, and high-sensitivity-troponin trajectory; repeatedly screen for
very-high-risk features; distinguish confirmed NSTEMI from one isolated abnormal result; and record
an inpatient invasive-strategy discussion whose timing remains patient-, risk-, region-, and
system-specific.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2025 ACC/AHA multisociety acute-coronary-syndrome guideline recommends an invasive approach
  during hospitalization for intermediate- or high-risk NSTE-ACS and further risk stratification
  when ischemic risk is low.
- The 2023 ESC acute-coronary-syndrome guideline uses serial high-sensitivity troponin, ECG, and
  clinical assessment; recommends immediate invasive management for very-high-risk NSTE-ACS; and
  recommends inpatient invasive management, with an early strategy considered for high-risk
  characteristics.
- The two guidelines do not supply one interchangeable timing rule for every patient and health
  system. This lab therefore records the shared risk structure and preserves the applicable local
  pathway rather than presenting one universal deadline.

## Authored vignette

A 67-year-old woman had 25 minutes of central pressure 5 hours ago and is pain-free now. Fixed
high-sensitivity troponin rises from 18 to 146 ng/L above the assay-specific 99th percentile. A
fixed initial 12-lead report shows horizontal ST depression in V4-V6 and the repeat report shows
new lateral T-wave inversion. HR is 88/min, BP 132/78 mmHg, SpO2 97% on room air, and there is no
authored shock, recurrent or refractory pain, acute heart failure, life-threatening arrhythmia,
cardiac arrest, mechanical complication, or recurrent dynamic ST change. The authored conclusion
is confirmed NSTEMI with high-risk features but no current very-high-risk feature.

## Exclusions

The screen does not examine; acquire or interpret an ECG, troponin, imaging, or another test;
calculate a GRACE, ischemic, or bleeding score; diagnose a real patient; select, dose, prescribe,
or deliver medication; choose access, angiography, PCI, or surgery; determine universal invasive
timing, transfer, disposition, prognosis, infarct size, complications, or outcome.
