# Evidence brief: unstable bradycardia

## Intended rehearsal

This introductory emergency-department case rehearses recognizing that bradycardia is clinically
inappropriate and associated with cardiopulmonary compromise, recording immediate assessment and
support, recording a fixed atropine intent, and reassessing the whole patient. It is a deterministic
fictional response, not a treatment recommendation or dosing calculator.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2025 AHA adult bradycardia algorithm describes bradyarrhythmia as typically below 50/min and
  defines compromise with hypotension, acutely altered mental status, shock signs, ischemic chest
  discomfort, or acute heart failure.
- Initial support includes a patent airway, oxygen, assisted breathing when necessary,
  cardiorespiratory monitoring, pulse monitoring, and vascular access. Vascular access was added to
  the algorithm in the AHA change notice updated February 6, 2026.
- For persistent bradycardia with compromise, the algorithm uses atropine first. Its fixed adult IV
  dose is 1 mg, repeated every 3–5 minutes to a 3 mg total maximum.
- If atropine is ineffective, transcutaneous pacing and/or a dopamine or epinephrine infusion may
  be used, with expert consultation and transvenous pacing considered. This vignette stops before
  those branches and does not imply that atropine always works.
- Evaluation and treatment of reversible causes occur alongside stabilization. The case does not
  determine whether medication effect, ischemia, hypoxia, electrolyte disturbance, or another cause
  is responsible.

## Authored defaults

The fictional patient has a palpable pulse, sinus bradycardia at 38/min, BP 78/46 mmHg, drowsiness,
ischemic chest discomfort, cool mottled extremities, delayed capillary refill, and SpO₂ 91% on room
air. After the ordered support bundle and one fixed 1 mg IV atropine intent, the authored response is
sinus rhythm 68/min, BP 112/70 mmHg, SpO₂ 96%, alert mentation, resolving discomfort, and improved
perfusion. These values teach one observable reassessment, not individual response probability.

## Explicit exclusions

The case does not model live ECG acquisition, medication preparation or delivery, repeated atropine,
transcutaneous or transvenous pacing, electrical or mechanical capture, sedation or discomfort from
pacing, dopamine or epinephrine infusion, definitive causal diagnosis, recurrence, disposition, or
outcome.
