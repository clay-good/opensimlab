# Evidence brief: croup with stridor at rest

## Learning contract

This lab rehearses calm whole-child upper-airway review, alternative-red-flag awareness,
qualified-team treatment intent, time-aware reassessment, recurrence escalation, and handoff in one
authored croup trajectory. It is not a diagnosis, medication, oxygen, nebulizer, airway, procedure,
or disposition algorithm.

## Evidence anchors

- The Canadian Paediatric Society's 2026 statement centers severity on stridor at rest, indrawing,
  behavior, and color; minimizes fright; supports corticosteroid care and nebulized epinephrine for
  moderate or severe croup; and describes improvement within 10–30 minutes with an effect that wanes
  by about 2 hours. Observation and recurrence-free reassessment matter before discharge is considered.
- The Royal Children's Hospital Melbourne guideline emphasizes keeping the child with a caregiver,
  minimizing examination and distress, using whole-child severity rather than stridor loudness, and
  recognizing hypoxia as a late sign. Typical croup is clinical; dangerous alternatives and poor
  response reopen senior airway review.
- The 2023 Cochrane review found glucocorticoids improve croup symptoms and reduce return visits or
  readmissions. Its dose-comparison uncertainty is one reason this learner surface contains no
  molecule, dose, route, concentration, or repeat-treatment control.

## Authored fixture

The child, prodrome, findings, qualified care, 20-minute improvement, and later recurrent stridor at
rest are fixed. The healthy-child engine profile is source-composed scaffolding, not a validated
croup or upper-airway obstruction model. Improvement and recurrence do not prove treatment effect,
durable recovery, discharge readiness, prognosis, or outcome.
