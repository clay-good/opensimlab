# COPD exacerbation transition reassessment evidence brief

## Teaching claim

This bounded hospital-day-3 lesson rehearses the difference between improvement and readiness after a COPD exacerbation. The learner reconciles verified prior treatment and current recovery, preserves uncertainty around residual exertional desaturation and long-term oxygen needs, assigns medication and inhaler-technique review, coordinates pulmonary rehabilitation and follow-up, and hands off unresolved work.

## Evidence anchors

- GOLD 2026 Figure 4.10 and its transition text support checking clinical and functional recovery, maintenance treatment and technique, oxygen needs, self-management, rehabilitation, comorbidities, and early and later follow-up after an exacerbation.
- The 2023 official ATS pulmonary-rehabilitation guideline strongly recommends pulmonary rehabilitation after hospitalization for a COPD exacerbation.
- The authored rest and corridor saturation reports are reassessment inputs, not a universal long-term oxygen qualification or prescription.

## Authored calibration

The baseline, admission course, delivered treatment, serial arterial gases, radiograph, hospital-day-3 findings, corridor assessment, medication reconciliation, and technique report are fixed teaching data. They are chosen to make physiologic improvement coexist with unresolved function and transition work. They do not estimate a typical patient trajectory or predict discharge or outcome.

## Boundaries

The browser does not examine, acquire or interpret tests, deliver treatment, prescribe oxygen or medication, select an inhaler, perform or grade technique, enroll rehabilitation, guarantee services, assess a home, determine discharge, or predict readmission or outcome. Real decisions require verified patient data, local pathways, qualified teams, shared planning, and longitudinal reassessment.
