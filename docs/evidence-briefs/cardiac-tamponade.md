# Cardiac tamponade evidence brief

## What this slice teaches

One fixed adult emergency-department vignette asks the learner to join penetrating central-chest
trauma with worsening perfusion, narrowing pressure, and falling end-tidal carbon dioxide despite
preserved bilateral air entry. A fixed POCUS statement supports immediate trauma and surgical
definitive-control intent, followed by serial reassessment.

## Current source floor

The 2025 European Resuscitation Council special-circumstances guideline identifies cardiac
tamponade as a frequent cause of arrest in penetrating chest trauma and places immediate surgical
capability at the center of traumatic tamponade treatment. The current Resuscitation Council UK
traumatic cardiac-arrest/peri-arrest algorithm likewise prioritizes simultaneous reversible-cause
treatment, including tamponade relief and damage-control pathways. This pre-arrest vignette records
urgent system escalation only and does not translate either source into procedural instruction.

## Authored vignette and modeled behavior

- A fixed pericardial-pressure drive progressively lowers stroke volume, cardiac output, pressure,
  and end-tidal carbon dioxide without imposing unilateral ventilation loss or a pleural hypoxia trajectory.
- Whole-patient review reveals an authored compatible mechanism and perfusion pattern. The next
  control reveals fixed pericardial fluid with right-sided chamber collapse.
- POCUS acquisition, views, artifacts, interpretation uncertainty, and differential findings are absent.
- Accepted definitive-control intent stops the active drive. Residual effects clear over a fixed
  teaching interval so reassessment is visible; recovery does not prove technical success.

## Deliberate exclusions

This is not a diagnostic system, POCUS lab, trauma algorithm, or procedure trainer. It does not
model pericardial blood volume or pressure, examination or imaging acquisition, pericardiocentesis,
thoracotomy, incision or access choice, equipment, transport, team performance, repair, concurrent
hemorrhage or pleural injury, aortic injury, pulmonary embolism, arrest, complications, recurrence,
disposition, or outcome.
