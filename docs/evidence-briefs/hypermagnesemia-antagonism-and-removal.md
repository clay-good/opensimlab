# Hypermagnesemia: support is not clearance

Content version 0.1.0; renal/electrolyte preview. Sources checked August 27, 2026.
Independent clinical approval remains pending. Educational simulation, not patient care.

## What the learner must distinguish

Calcium can counter toxicity without removing magnesium. Respiratory support, temporary calcium
antagonism, stopping further exposure, and delivered magnesium removal are separate decisions.
None waits for an administrative acknowledgment or another laboratory click. The selected case
is distinct from the obstetric magnesium-infusion recognition lesson and from magnesium replacement.

The evidence supports those distinctions, not this fictional response curve:

- [Nishikawa et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29988705/): 15 selected emergency-dialysis
  patients, excluding maintenance dialysis. All received magnesium oxide. Serious clinical findings
  and multidisciplinary judgment informed removal, not a universal magnesium cutoff.
- [Bansal et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26915350/): one Renacidin-associated case,
  not oral exposure. Attempted calcium antagonism did not prevent a magnesium rise. The report does
  not establish a predictable calcium-response duration or common postdialysis rebound.
- [Jou et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38152602/): two magnesium-hydroxide cases support
  exposure and renal/intestinal-context review. Falling magnesium did not ensure clinical recovery.
  These cases do not validate a universal hydration or diuresis strategy.
- [Current Hospira labeling](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=03ebeabb-8386-4af4-3086-bdf3c3fc4a5a),
  updated August 24, 2026, with prescribing information revised June 2026: renal risk, respiratory
  support, and calcium antagonism inform safety boundaries. No parenteral/obstetric regimen is
  transferred into this oral-exposure lesson.

## Authored patient and effects

The fictional patient is a 78-year-old woman, 64 kg, 160 cm, ASA class 3. Known CKD4 includes a
documented eGFR of 18 mL/min/1.73 m²; she is not receiving dialysis. Magnesium hydroxide was used
for constipation for 14 days, without a supplied dose. Five days without a bowel movement and
reduced urine prompt review, not an established obstruction, new clearance estimate, or AKI diagnosis.

Pretreatment magnesium is 4.6 mmol/L, potassium 4.8 mmol/L, calcium 2.24 mmol/L, sodium 138 mmol/L,
and glucose 108 mg/dL. Only magnesium is remeasured in this model. No new kidney function, other
electrolytes, glucose, ECG interval, or whole-body magnesium balance is generated.

Initial findings are drowsiness, absent reflexes, severe weakness, HR 44/min, BP 86/48 mmHg,
MAP 61 mmHg, RR 8/min, SpO2 90%, and temperature 36.3°C. Breathing support independently changes
the displayed supported total RR to 14/min and SpO2 to 96%. These remain supported findings after
improvement; no ventilator settings or automatic withdrawal are supplied.

Calcium immediately changes circulation to HR 62/min, BP 104/60 mmHg, and MAP 75 mmHg. It does
not lower magnesium or restore reflexes or breathing in this model. Its authored benefit expires
at 30 minutes, allowing recurrent clinical toxicity without a magnesium rise. Qualified repeat
care is available after individualized clinical review, not an automatic redosing instruction.

At 60 minutes after delivered removal, magnesium becomes 2.4 mmol/L, HR 68/min, BP 110/66 mmHg,
and MAP 81 mmHg. Reflexes are present and severe weakness improves, but residual weakness remains.
Without breathing support, RR is still 10/min and SpO2 92%. Temperature stays 36.3°C and rhythm
stays sinus throughout; no PR, QRS, or QT interval is modeled.

Without calcium or removal, a five-minute contrast worsens HR to 38/min and BP to 78/42 mmHg
(MAP 54). Unsupported breathing becomes RR 6/min and SpO2 86%; subsequent calcium alone does
not fix it. Instructor takeover occurs at 30 minutes without any of the three urgent-care
actions, or at the 180-minute session boundary otherwise. All numbers and clocks are authored
contrasts, not expected kinetics, safe waits, treatment thresholds, or clinical outcomes.

Compatibility defaults are fixed stroke volume 70 mL, hemoglobin 10.8 g/dL, blood volume 4,200 mL,
unit arterial/baroreflex factors, and airway difficulty 0.1. Inactive ventilation defaults are
450 mL, 8/min, FiO2 0.21, and fresh gas flow 10 L/min. The healthy respiratory compatibility profile
is not normal clinical function. Exhaled CO2 and FiO2 are unavailable as monitored findings.

## Observation, transfer, and privacy

Magnesium-only, neuromuscular-only, and full assessments keep separate timestamps. Full findings
become stale when clinical state changes; calcium bookkeeping after a removal response does not
invent a new deterioration. Earlier observed recurrence and refused shortcuts remain in history.

Handoff requires current full findings and continuing ownership of exposure control, respiratory
support, specialist review, delivered removal, and surveillance. Removal response can be pending.
An observed removal response does not require unnecessary late calcium, normal magnesium, or
recreation of missed early panels. A completed handoff is not discharge or respiratory recovery.
