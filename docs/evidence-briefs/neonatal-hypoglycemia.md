# Neonatal hypoglycemia

## Evidence boundary

There is no universally accepted glucose concentration that separates normal neonatal transition
from clinically important hypoglycemia or predicts neurologic injury. The American Academy of
Pediatrics' 2011 operational approach for at-risk late-preterm and term infants is currently marked
under review. The British Association of Perinatal Medicine's 2024 framework uses the glucose value,
clinical signs, risk group, and repeat trajectory together and is intended for local adaptation.

Both approaches support immediate qualified escalation when a newborn has abnormal clinical signs
and a clearly low measured glucose. This lesson therefore avoids a borderline threshold. It authors
an infant of a diabetic mother with jitteriness, poor feeding, and a laboratory plasma glucose of
32 mg/dL (1.8 mmol/L) at 2 hours. The learner recognizes urgent coupled clinical and biochemical
risk, but does not diagnose the cause, select a protocol, or deliver feeding, dextrose gel, IV
dextrose, or any other treatment.

## Authored follow-up estimate

A fixed qualified-team report 30 minutes after locally protocolized treatment supplies plasma
glucose 54 mg/dL (3.0 mmol/L), no currently observed jitteriness, normal temperature, and ongoing
supported feeding assessment. This is a bounded teaching estimate, not a universal treatment
effect. A single improved value does not prove durable glucose stability, neurologic safety, feeding
success, cause, discharge readiness, or outcome.
