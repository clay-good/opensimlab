# Persistent dyspnea after pulmonary embolism evidence brief

## Teaching claim

This bounded 4-month follow-up lesson rehearses structured evaluation of persistent dyspnea and functional limitation after confirmed pulmonary embolism. The learner reconciles verified anticoagulation and prior function, reviews current safety and exertional limitation, integrates fixed cardiac and perfusion evidence without diagnosing CTEPD or CTEPH, coordinates pulmonary-vascular evaluation and continued treatment ownership, and hands off unresolved work.

## Evidence anchors

- The 2026 multisociety acute-PE guideline recommends follow-up by 3 months and evaluation of persistent dyspnea or functional impairment after at least 3 months of therapeutic anticoagulation.
- In persistently symptomatic patients, transthoracic echo plus lung perfusion imaging is reasonable; echo alone is insufficient to include or exclude CTEPD.
- Anticoagulation should continue during CTEPD evaluation unless contraindicated by high bleeding risk, while diagnosis and management require qualified pulmonary-vascular expertise.
- The 2022 ESC/ERS pulmonary-hypertension guideline supports V/Q imaging in suspected CTEPH and comprehensive expert work-up after at least 3 months of therapeutic anticoagulation.

## Authored calibration

The confirmed acute event, treatment record, prior and current function, supervised walk, oximetry, ECG rhythm, echo, and V/Q SPECT are fixed teaching reports. Persistent symptoms plus mismatched defects and RV findings raise concern but do not establish CTEPD, pulmonary hypertension, chronic clot anatomy, operability, or cause. The negative snapshots do not permanently exclude recurrence or another diagnosis.

## Boundaries

The browser does not examine, acquire or interpret tests, diagnose CTEPD or CTEPH, choose or deliver anticoagulation, select duration, adjudicate bleeding, prescribe oxygen or rehabilitation, choose pulmonary-hypertension therapy, perform or select surgery or balloon angioplasty, determine operability or disposition, or predict recovery, recurrence, prognosis, or outcome.
