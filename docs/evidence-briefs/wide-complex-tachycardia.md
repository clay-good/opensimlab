# Evidence brief: wide-complex tachycardia

## Intended rehearsal

This advanced monitored-cardiology case rehearses a pulsed, currently stable, regular monomorphic
wide-complex tachycardia: diagnostic uncertainty, harm avoidance, one authored expert-selected
medication pathway, escalation after elapsed nonresponse, and post-conversion ownership.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2025 AHA adult tachyarrhythmia pathway uses whole-patient instability, not rate alone.
- Stable WCT supports expert consultation and a monitored antiarrhythmic pathway; adenosine is only
  a conditional option for stable regular monomorphic WCT. Verapamil and diltiazem can cause harm.
- This fixed case reports a treating-team procainamide course after QT and heart-failure review, then
  persistent WCT. It does not teach one universal agent, dose, infusion rate, or comparative efficacy.
- Persistent monomorphic WCT can proceed to synchronized cardioversion. Polymorphic and pulseless
  rhythms belong to different pathways.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine; acquire or interpret an ECG or test; diagnose a mechanism; place
access; select beyond the authored path, calculate, prepare, or deliver medication; operate a
defibrillator; choose energy or sedation; perform cardioversion or ablation; decide ICD therapy or
disposition; predict recurrence, prognosis, or outcome; or assess psychomotor competence.
