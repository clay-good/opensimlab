# DKA resolution and transition readiness

## Teaching decision

This Endocrine and Metabolic Medicine preview lesson owns the late-treatment question: whether diabetic ketoacidosis has actually resolved and whether transition away from intravenous insulin is ready. It does not duplicate the Emergency Medicine lesson's initial diagnosis, potassium gate, fluid, insulin, and dextrose sequence.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2024 international consensus report defines DKA resolution as plasma ketone below 0.6 mmol/L plus venous pH at least 7.3 or bicarbonate at least 18 mmol/L. It cautions that hyperchloremic normal-anion-gap acidosis is common during successful treatment and may be mistaken for persistent DKA.
- The consensus report recommends capillary glucose every 1–2 hours and electrolytes, phosphate, creatinine, beta-hydroxybutyrate, and venous pH every 4 hours during treatment until resolution. It also requires 1–2 hours of overlap between subcutaneous insulin and discontinuation of intravenous insulin.
- The ADA 2026 hospital standard requires fluids, insulin, electrolytes, close monitoring, a timely bridged transition to maintenance subcutaneous insulin, and treatment of the precipitating cause. It describes basal insulin 2–4 hours before stopping intravenous insulin to reduce rebound hyperglycemia and recurrent ketoacidosis.

The sources differ in how they phrase the overlap window. This lesson authors basal insulin 2 hours before a later transition-readiness report, which sits inside both cited windows. It does not teach a universal product, dose, route, fluid, electrolyte-replacement plan, glucose target, nutrition plan, or disposition threshold.
