# Hypertensive emergency

This advanced Cardiology lesson practices recognizing a renal-retinal hypertensive emergency, framing controlled monitored pressure reduction, reviewing fixed elapsed panels, and handing off unresolved work. It does not teach a drug, dose, infusion rate, universal pressure target, or rapid-normalization recipe.

## Authored case

A 58-year-old woman with hypertension and a 3-week refill interruption has 3 days of headache and bilateral blurred vision. Correctly repeated pressures remain 236/132 and 232/130 mmHg after an initial 238/134 mmHg. Fixed findings show bilateral flame hemorrhages, cotton-wool spots, optic-disc edema, creatinine 2.1 mg/dL from 0.9 mg/dL 6 months earlier, proteinuria, and microscopic hematuria. These renal-retinal findings, not marked pressure alone, establish the authored emergency.

The current record has no pulmonary edema, ACS, aortic, focal neurologic, seizure, altered-mentation, pregnancy, or toxicologic pattern. These are current snapshots and deterioration triggers, not permanent exclusions. A fixed 45-minute report and a separate fixed 3-hour handoff remain authored experienced-team care, not learner-delivered treatment or proof of resolution.

## Evidence boundary

- Jones DW, Ferdinand KC, Taler SJ, et al. 2025 high blood pressure guideline. *Circulation*. doi:10.1161/CIR.0000000000001356.
- Bress AP, Anderson TS, Flack JM, et al. Acute-care elevated blood pressure scientific statement. *Hypertension*. 2024;81:e94-e106. doi:10.1161/HYP.0000000000000238.

The lesson is intentionally distinct from acute pulmonary edema, acute coronary syndrome, acute aortic syndrome, intracranial hemorrhage, ischemic stroke, and pregnancy-related hypertension. Those syndromes require their own current pathways. Completion does not confer examination, test-interpretation, prescribing, infusion, procedure, disposition, or prognostic competence.
