# Atrial fibrillation with rapid response

## Intended rehearsal

Separate hemodynamic stability from heart rate alone; review uncertain duration, prior history,
ventricular function, comorbidities, and contributors; record patient-specific acute rate-control
intent; keep stroke prevention and cardioversion context on a separate lane; and reassess a fixed
lower-rate response that remains atrial fibrillation.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2023 ACC/AHA/ACCP/HRS guideline recommends acute rate-control choices for stable AF with
  rapid ventricular response based on ventricular function and contraindications, while unstable
  AF follows an immediate synchronized-cardioversion pathway.
- The guideline treats thromboembolic-risk assessment and anticoagulation as separate from symptom
  or rate control, and AF duration affects the safety context around cardioversion.
- The 2023 AHA scientific statement organizes acute AF around triggers, rate or rhythm management,
  and anticoagulation, with rhythm monitoring and risk-factor modification continuing after the
  acute encounter.

## Authored vignette

A 69-year-old woman noticed palpitations 6 hours ago, but her last symptom-free check was 3 days
ago, so AF duration is uncertain. A fixed diagnostic 12-lead report names AF with an irregular
narrow-complex rate of 142/min. BP is 119/71 mmHg, SpO2 is 97% on room air, and she is alert and
warm without authored hypotension, shock, ischemic discomfort, acute heart failure, syncope, or
altered mentation. A fixed LVEF report is 55%. Fixed laboratory and contributor review does not
identify one acute cause. After the ordered review and plan intents, the fixed response remains AF
at 96/min with improved palpitations and stable pressure and perfusion.

## Exclusions

The screen does not take a history or examine; acquire or interpret an ECG, laboratory test, or
imaging; calculate a thromboembolic or bleeding score; diagnose a real patient; choose a rate
target; select, dose, prescribe, or deliver medication or anticoagulation; determine cardioversion
eligibility; perform cardioversion or ablation; determine disposition, recurrence, prognosis, or
outcome.
