# Pediatric septic shock after reassessed fluid

Status: draft teaching content; clinical review unsigned. Consulted August 25, 2026. Review by August 25, 2027, or sooner if the cited pediatric sepsis guidance or Phoenix criteria change.

## What this lab teaches

This fixed 4-year-old case asks the learner to recognize persistent infection-associated cardiovascular dysfunction after 2 individually reassessed fluid aliquots, treat new congestion findings as stop-and-reassess warnings rather than proof of causation, and keep experienced shock rescue and source-control planning active in parallel. A later authored report shows partial movement while shock and congestion remain active.

The learner does not examine the child, calculate Phoenix, acquire or interpret tests or imaging, diagnose a source, choose or deliver antimicrobials, fluid, access, vasoactives, oxygen, devices, procedures, or treatment, or determine disposition or outcome.

## Evidence boundary

The 2026 Surviving Sepsis Campaign pediatric guideline supports lactate measurement; cultures before antimicrobials when this does not materially delay treatment; antimicrobial therapy as soon as possible and ideally within 1 hour for suspected septic shock; 10–20 mL/kg fluid aliquots in systems with intensive care; reassessment after every bolus; stopping fluid when shock resolves or overload signs develop; clinical review of heart rate, pressure, refill, extremity temperature, pulses, consciousness, and urine; peripheral vasoactive initiation rather than delaying for central access; and urgent source control when an amenable source is diagnosed. It does not establish one universal MAP percentile target, whether vasoactives must begin before or after 40 mL/kg, or a preferred first-line choice between epinephrine and norepinephrine.

The 2024 Phoenix consensus defines pediatric sepsis as suspected infection plus a Phoenix score of at least 2 and septic shock as sepsis with at least 1 cardiovascular point. For age 2 to younger than 5 years, MAP 32–44 mmHg contributes 1 cardiovascular point; lactate 5–10.9 mmol/L contributes 1; and one vasoactive contributes 1. Phoenix is supplied here as classification after overt organ dysfunction, not as an early screening or learner-calculation task.

## Authored values and non-claims

The child, suspected intra-abdominal source, exact timings, two 10 mL/kg aliquots, vital signs, GCS, urine output, lactate, crackles, hepatomegaly, and minute-90 trajectory are fictional face-valid fixtures. The lab does not claim that 20 mL/kg is a universal stopping point, that fluid caused congestion, that one vasoactive is preferred, that MAP 60 mmHg is a target, or that the later changes were caused by a particular treatment.
