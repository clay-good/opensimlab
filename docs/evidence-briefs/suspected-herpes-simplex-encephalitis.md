# Suspected herpes simplex encephalitis

## Decision boundary

This lab teaches the tension between immediate qualified empiric antiviral care and incomplete
etiologic certainty. Fever plus new behavior, memory, language, and focal-seizure change establishes
an authored encephalitic syndrome. Qualified neurological, infection, neurocritical, airway-capable,
nursing, seizure, pharmacy, laboratory, imaging, and neurophysiology ownership begins while CSF,
MRI, EEG, and etiologic work proceed in parallel. The learner does not examine, test, interpret raw
studies, select a regimen, prescribe, perform a procedure, or deliver treatment.

The case is distinct from bacterial meningitis because altered parenchymal brain function and focal
seizure dominate without meningism. It is distinct from status epilepticus because one 90-second
focal event stopped before the learner surface and the strict 60-minute EEG sample contains no
electrographic seizure. Persistent risk still requires seizure surveillance.

## Evidence and authored values

The 2026 German Society of Neurology guideline recommends immediate intravenous acyclovir without
delay when HSV encephalitis is suspected and supports CSF, MRI, and EEG evaluation. The IDSA
encephalitis guideline recommends MRI when available, EEG to identify nonconvulsive seizures, CSF
analysis and HSV PCR, empiric acyclovir pending diagnostics, and repeat HSV PCR 3 to 7 days later
when an initial result is negative but the compatible clinical syndrome or temporal localization
persists.

The 37-year-old patient, 30-hour and 12-hour clocks, focal seizure, examination, physiology, blood
and CSF values, qualified empiric care, temporal MRI, specialist EEG, specimen timing, early negative
HSV PCR, and strict 4-hour clinical report are authored teaching evidence. They do not prove HSV,
exclude another infectious, autoimmune, vascular, postictal, toxic-metabolic, neoplastic, or other
cause, prove seizure control or treatment response, determine repeat-test timing for a real patient,
or predict recovery, disposition, prognosis, or outcome.
