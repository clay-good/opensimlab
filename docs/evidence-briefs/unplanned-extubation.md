# Evidence brief: unplanned extubation

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate adult ICU case rehearses immediate support after unplanned extubation, a rapid
whole-patient tolerance assessment, recognition of an authored failing trajectory, prompt skilled
reintubation intent, placement confirmation, and a prevention-focused handoff.

## Evidence boundary

- Unplanned extubation requires high-intensity monitoring for reintubation need. Upper-airway
  obstruction, secretion burden, exhaustion, impaired consciousness, and cardiopulmonary failure
  favor reintubation; the event alone does not make the decision automatic.
- The fixed case converges on failure: severe work, worsening hypoxemia and hypercapnia, weak airway
  protection, secretion burden, and declining alertness.
- Current ATS guidance strongly supports HFNC or NIV for preoxygenation before intubation. The older
  ERS/ATS guideline cautions that rescue NIV in established post-extubation respiratory failure may
  delay needed reintubation; unplanned extubation was under-studied.
- Patient-ventilator assessment includes oxygenation, ventilation, hemodynamics, neurologic state,
  airway position, security, and patency. After the immediate response, those domains inform the
  reported placement check and prevention review.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine, acquire monitoring or blood gases, deliver oxygen, ventilate by mask,
choose drugs or equipment, preoxygenate, intubate, confirm a tube, diagnose respiratory failure,
investigate the event, assign fault, predict disposition, or assess airway or procedural competence.
All findings and responses are authored panels.
