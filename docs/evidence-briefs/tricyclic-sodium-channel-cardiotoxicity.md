## Evidence application

The AHA describes sodium-channel-blocker poisoning as a whole pattern that can include QRS
prolongation, terminal rightward axis deviation, hypotension, seizures, ventricular dysrhythmias, and
collapse. It recommends sodium bicarbonate for life-threatening TCA cardiotoxicity while noting that
biochemical versus physiological endpoints remain debated and that refractory rescue evidence is
limited. The lab therefore records qualified intent and preparedness, not a learner dose or endpoint.

DailyMed identifies dysrhythmias, severe hypotension, convulsions, and CNS depression as critical
overdose manifestations and treats QRS axis or width changes as clinically significant, while warning
that absence is not exclusionary. The lab never makes one interval or axis finding diagnostic.
