# Neuromuscular respiratory failure reassessment

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate Respiratory Medicine lab rehearses recognizing progressive ventilatory and
cough failure in established ALS from a longitudinal whole-patient pattern, escalating before a
complete cause review, and coordinating urgent respiratory work around the person's communication,
preferences, symptom goals, and caregivers.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2023 CHEST neuromuscular-weakness guideline supports serial pulmonary-function and
  respiratory-muscle assessment, individualized to the disease and rate of progression, alongside
  cough-effectiveness and secretion-clearance review. This lab provides every result as a fixed
  qualified report and does not turn one value into a universal threshold.
- NICE NG42 recommends asking about breathlessness, orthopnea, disturbed sleep, morning headache,
  daytime sleepiness, weak cough and related respiratory symptoms, repeating FVC/VC and SNIP/MIP,
  and referring to a specialist ventilation service when respiratory function declines. It also
  keeps communication with the person and family central.
- Resting awake SpO₂ is not a complete ventilation assessment. The authored symptoms, examination
  claims, serial seated and supine FVC, SNIP, peak cough flow, and blood gas form one convergent
  pattern while aspiration, infection, mucus retention, lung, cardiac, metabolic, medication,
  central, testing, and other neuromuscular contributors remain open.
- Support interfaces, modes, settings, secretion-management techniques, airway procedures, and
  treatment decisions depend on qualified patient-specific assessment and local resources. The
  browser rehearses recognition, escalation, ownership, and handoff only.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine; acquire, perform, score, or interpret oximetry, capnography, blood gas,
spirometry, respiratory-muscle mechanics, peak cough flow, sleep, imaging, swallowing, cough,
secretion, or neurologic testing; diagnose; select or deliver oxygen, noninvasive or invasive
ventilation, an interface, settings, cough assistance, airway clearance, suction, medication,
nutrition, or treatment; intubate, place a tracheostomy, perform a procedure, determine disposition
or prognosis, or predict response or outcome. All findings are fixed authored reports.
