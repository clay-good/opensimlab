# Post-arrest temperature-control evidence brief

## Scope

A fixed adult ICU case 32 minutes after ROSC from witnessed VF arrest. The patient remains
unresponsive to verbal commands and is febrile. The lesson covers deliberate protocolized
temperature control, systemic guardrails, controlled rewarming, and reassessment. It does not teach
cardiac-arrest care, a cooling device, drug dosing, cause treatment, or neuroprognostication.

## Evidence translated into the lesson

- The 2025 AHA post-arrest guideline recommends deliberate protocolized temperature control for all
  adults unresponsive to verbal commands after ROSC, regardless of arrest location or presenting
  rhythm. The case begins at that eligibility boundary without treating it as a prognosis.
- AHA recommends maintaining 32–37.5°C and considers at least 36 hours reasonable. Evidence does not
  establish one universally superior temperature for all severity subgroups, so the learner records
  an individualized local protocol within the recommended range rather than “cool to 33°C.”
- Routine rapid infusion of cold IV fluid for prehospital cooling is not recommended. AHA also says
  avoiding rewarming faster than 0.5°C/h may be reasonable. Both appear as guardrails, not simulated
  fluid, cooling, or warming interventions.
- The fixed response lowers temperature into range while preserving uncertainty about cause,
  seizures, organ recovery, neurologic recovery, and prognosis.

## Boundaries and disputed practice

Target selection within the recommended range, device, sedation, shivering management, duration
beyond 36 hours, rewarming, and systemic support depend on patient context, local protocols, and
expert teams. The browser does not perform or assess temperature measurement, cooling, warming,
airway or ventilator care, medication or fluid delivery, EEG, cause treatment, prognostication,
teamwork, or outcome. No copyrighted algorithm or dosing table is reproduced.
