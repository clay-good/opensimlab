# Portable oxygen source failure

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate Respiratory Medicine lab rehearses recognizing a credible deterioration during
portable oxygen use, activating experienced help and a separate verified oxygen bridge before
troubleshooting, reviewing a fixed source-to-patient path, recording checked restoration with
independent backup, and proving delivery through an elapsed whole-person response. It is a patient
and system-safety lesson, not cylinder or flowmeter training.

## Evidence boundary

- NHS England's 2023 National Patient Safety Alert describes serious harm when cylinder oxygen is
  empty, not activated, or otherwise fails to reach the patient. It requires initial and ongoing
  checks of flow and remaining contents, especially during transfer and diagnostic testing.
- The BTS emergency-oxygen guideline says that when saturation falls below the intended range, the
  oxygen delivery system and oximeter should be checked for faults or errors while the patient is
  assessed and monitored. The lab therefore corroborates the signal but does not delay support for
  a final device label.
- WHO describes medical oxygen as a complete system spanning source, storage, distribution,
  regulation, delivery, patient monitoring, maintenance, and backup. The lab uses that
  equipment-agnostic chain and avoids a proprietary valve, connector, alarm, or repair sequence.
- The fixed response toward the authored pre-transfer state supports restored delivery only. It
  does not prove resolution of fibrotic lung disease, readiness to resume transport, durable
  equipment reliability, disposition, or outcome.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine; measure saturation or flow; acquire or interpret a blood gas, imaging,
monitor, or other test; diagnose; inspect, open, close, attach, detach, reconnect, replace, repair,
or operate a cylinder, valve, regulator, flowmeter, tubing, cannula, or other device; calculate
cylinder duration; select a device, source, interface, flow, FiO\u2082, oxygen target, prescription,
drug, ventilation, or treatment; deliver oxygen; perform a procedure; decide transport readiness,
disposition, or prognosis; or predict durable restoration or outcome. The source failure, qualified
bridge, path review, replacement, backup, delivery confirmation, and response are fixed reports.
