# Transcutaneous pacing mechanical-capture reassessment evidence brief

## What this slice teaches

One fixed monitored-Cardiology vignette separates transcutaneous pacing artifacts, electrical
capture, and mechanical capture. An experienced team has already started pacing for complete heart
block. Each stimulus is followed by a broad QRS and T wave, but the patient becomes pulseless and
the arterial and pleth waveforms are nonpulsatile. Learners recognize pulseless electrical activity,
activate the nonshockable-arrest pathway, keep causes and future pacing needs open, and hand off
active resuscitation without a fictional recovery.

## Current source floor

- The 2025 AHA adult advanced-life-support guideline and Adult Bradycardia With a Pulse Algorithm
  frame symptomatic bradycardia by cardiopulmonary compromise, include transcutaneous pacing when
  compromise persists, and move a patient without a pulse to cardiac-arrest care. Pacing during
  established asystole or pulseless electrical activity has not improved outcomes and must not delay
  evidence-based resuscitation.
- The 2018 ACC/AHA/HRS bradycardia guideline states that effective capture must be assessed by pulse
  or arterial waveform, not ECG appearance alone. It also describes transcutaneous pacing as an
  often uncomfortable, potentially unreliable short bridge rather than definitive pacing care.

## Authored vignette and sequence

- Immediately before transcutaneous pacing, the patient has complete heart block, ventricular rate
  and central pulse 24/min, BP 70/40 mmHg, confusion, and cool perfusion.
- A fixed experienced-team report then shows pacing stimuli at 70/min, each followed by a broad QRS
  and distinct T wave. This establishes electrical capture in this authored case, not a reusable ECG
  rule or learner interpretation skill.
- The patient is now unresponsive with agonal breaths. A fixed 10-second assessment reports no
  carotid or femoral pulse, nonpulsatile arterial and pleth waveforms, and no measurable BP. These
  findings establish absent mechanical capture and pulseless electrical activity in the vignette.
- Recognition precedes immediate pulseless-response activation. Open causes and future pacing-bridge
  questions follow without interrupting arrest care. A strictly later handoff preserves active
  resuscitation, capture evidence, causes, bridge questions, and owners. Return of spontaneous
  circulation, later perfusion, neurologic status, disposition, prognosis, and outcome are not
  reported.

## Deliberate exclusions

This is not an examination, pulse-check, ECG, waveform, resuscitation, pacing, or procedure trainer.
It does not expose pad placement, current, rate selection, pulse width, energy, drug, dose, sedation,
compression or ventilation mechanics, device operation, temporary access, pacing delivery, or a
definitive pacing choice. Completion does not establish return of circulation or outcome.
