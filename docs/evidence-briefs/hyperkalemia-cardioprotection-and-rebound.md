# Hyperkalemia: protection is not removal

Content version 0.1.0; renal/electrolyte preview. Sources checked August 27, 2026.
Independent clinical approval remains pending. This is educational simulation, not patient care.

## Clinical boundary

This perfusing adult has CKD with superimposed AKI and supplied confirmed hyperkalemia with ECG
changes. Cardiac protection, intracellular shifting, and individualized elimination are distinct.
Calcium does not lower potassium. Its benefit is temporary, and an improved ECG cannot establish
potassium safety. Shifting can precede rebound without elimination; glucose surveillance remains
necessary after insulin-glucose treatment. These principles follow the
[KDIGO conference report](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/KDIGO-Acute-Hyperkalemia-conf-report-FINAL.pdf),
pp. 332–335. This is conference consensus, not a new formal KDIGO guideline.

The [UKKA guideline](https://www.ukkidney.org/health-professionals/guidelines/treatment-acute-hyperkalaemia-adults-0)
was published December 19, 2023, with review listed for October 19, 2026. Sections 16–17 and 19
support differentiated treatment, serial potassium and glucose review, and renal or critical-care
ownership. Its selected schedules are not encoded as universal deadlines. The
[RCUK 2025 hyperkalemia section](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/special-circumstances-guidelines)
supports qualified emergency treatment and individualized elimination. Calcium preparation and
dose are deliberately not selected. Evidence about cardiac arrest must not be generalized to
withholding treatment in this patient with preserved circulation.

## Authored contrast, not calibrated physiology

The fictional patient is 68, with initial potassium 6.9 mmol/L, glucose 108 mg/dL, pulse 48/min,
BP 110/64 mmHg, RR 18/min, SpO2 98%, and temperature 36.7°C. Qualitative ECG conduction changes
are not a calibrated QRS duration or a potassium measurement.

| Decision or branch | Fictional consequence | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qualified calcium | Temporary ECG improvement, 45-minute review checkpoint | Potassium does not change; repeated review is available, not automatic dosing |
| Qualified insulin-glucose care | At 30 minutes, potassium 5.6 and glucose 104 | Includes qualified glucose verification and prevention; no guaranteed hypoglycemia |
| Renal support or removal plan | Ownership and planning only | No potassium effect |
| Qualified delivered elimination | At 60 minutes, potassium 5.1 and glucose 100 | Individualized care, not a dialysis, binder, or clearance solver |
| Shifting without completed elimination | At 150 minutes, potassium 6.6 | Rebound is an authored contrast, not an inevitable patient outcome |
| No shifting or elimination | Later bedside worsening and 120-minute instructor takeover | Calcium alone does not solve hyperkalemia; no death or arrhythmia prediction |

All timings are teaching contrasts, not recommended waits, safe treatment delays, or grading
cutoffs. The 360-minute session boundary ends unfinished practice, not clinical surveillance.
Later delivered care can improve the rebound branch without deleting earlier observed rebound.
Elimination response takes precedence over later shifting. No acidosis solver, automatic dialysis,
routine diuresis, binder-only rescue, universal medication cessation, or kidney recovery is supplied.

## Observation, tutor, and handoff

ECG-only and glucose-only checks are valid partial observations. Neither refreshes older potassium.
Full reassessment updates ECG, potassium, glucose, and bedside findings. Response clocks and
accepted actions do not leak hidden laboratory values. Guided and coached modes consume the same
public observations; unassisted mode is silent. The worked example uses ordinary accepted actions
and pauses for reading.

Handoff requires current full findings and continuing-care ownership, not a flawless history or
normalized potassium. Qualified removal may still be pending. The receiving team owns ECG and
calcium review, potassium and glucose surveillance, renal assessment, elimination, and escalation.
No discharge, durable safety, or competence certification is implied.
