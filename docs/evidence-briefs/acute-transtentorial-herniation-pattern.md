# Acute transtentorial herniation pattern

## Scope and distinctness

This lab rehearses recognition and coordinated first-hour rescue of an acute transtentorial
herniation pattern. It is distinct from the stable raised-pressure visual-threat lab, the
posterior-fossa hemorrhage and hydrocephalus lab, Emergency Medicine hemorrhage deterioration, and
Critical Care's monitored post-traumatic ICP/CPP treatment lab.

The learner connects a rapid consciousness decline, new ipsilateral nonreactive pupil,
contralateral motor change, severe physiology, and a supplied temporal mass-effect report. No single
pupil, scan finding, pressure value, or complete Cushing triad is treated as necessary or sufficient
alone. Qualified airway, neurocritical, neurosurgical, imaging, pharmacy, respiratory, nursing, and
operating-room teams own rescue and definitive control.

## Fixed fixture

- A previously independent 58-year-old man has a recently identified right temporal mass and 2
  hours of worsening headache and vomiting.
- Over 12 minutes, a qualified examination changes from GCS 14 to GCS 9, with a new right 6 mm
  nonreactive pupil, a left 3 mm reactive pupil, left-arm extension, and right-arm localization.
- Supplied physiology is T 36.9°C, HR 54/min, RR 14/min, BP 168/111 mmHg (MAP 130), and
  pulse-coherent room-air SpO2 97%. Spontaneous breathing continues, but airway protection is not
  reliable.
- Qualified CT reports a 5.2 cm right temporal mass, extensive vasogenic edema, 13 mm leftward
  midline shift, effaced right basal cisterns, and medial right-uncus displacement without authored
  acute hemorrhage or obstructive hydrocephalus.
- At a strict 15-minute report, qualified teams have secured the airway, report bilateral
  ventilation, SpO2 99%, and end-tidal CO2 36 mmHg, and have delivered individualized brain-rescue
  care while an emergency operating-room pathway remains active. The right pupil remains
  nonreactive; decompression, recovery, durable pressure control, and treatment effect are not
  reported.

## Evidence application

The Neurocritical Care Society's ENLS Version 6.0 protocol frames intracranial hypertension and
herniation as a time-critical first-hour emergency requiring rapid recognition and staged medical
and surgical rescue. The peer-reviewed ENLS manuscript likewise describes acute herniation as a
“brain code” requiring organized immediate evaluation and treatment. The lab applies that urgency
while keeping all examination, monitoring, airway, osmotic, imaging, and operative decisions with
qualified teams.

## Boundaries and hazards

- The healthy engine profile is scaffolding. Neurology, pupils, CT, airway reliability, supplied
  care, and later findings are authored snapshots rather than validated disease physiology.
- Controls only reconcile, recognize, activate owners, review boundaries, review a later report,
  and hand off. They do not perform clinical care.
- No universal osmotic agent, concentration, dose, route, ventilation target, airway device,
  operation, or timing recipe is taught.
- A better later monitor snapshot is not attributed to treatment and does not establish recovery,
  source control, survival, prognosis, or outcome.
- Real care requires immediate direct reassessment, local emergency protocols, experienced teams,
  verified serial findings, individualized treatment, and definitive cause control.
