# Nonconvulsive status epilepticus recognition

## Decision boundary

This lab teaches one narrow transition: unexplained fluctuating language and awareness with subtle
recurrent signs should trigger urgent qualified EEG assessment, but those clinical features alone do
not diagnose nonconvulsive status epilepticus (NCSE). A later fixed neurophysiologist report supplies
electrographic-status evidence. The learner does not view or interpret a raw tracing and does not
select treatment.

This is distinct from the visible focal-motor-status lab and from the critical-care refractory-status
lab. No prior convulsion is authored here, visible sustained clonus is absent, and the lesson ends at
qualified report integration and active-risk handoff, before drug or anesthetic selection.

## Evidence translated into the fixture

- The 2015 American Clinical Neurophysiology Society (ACNS) consensus identifies fluctuating or
  unexplained altered mental status, including aphasia and neglect, as an indication for continuous
  EEG and recommends starting monitoring promptly when nonconvulsive seizures are suspected.
- The 2021 ACNS terminology defines electrographic status using seizure burden of at least 20% in a
  60-minute period. The authored report states 24 minutes in 60 minutes, or 40%, and explicitly says
  that the definition is met. This arithmetic is scenario construction, not a learner calculation.
- Salzburg-criteria validation supports structured EEG assessment in the appropriate clinical
  context. Diagnostic performance is population- and implementation-dependent, so the simulation
  supplies a qualified conclusion instead of teaching raw-pattern classification.

The opening CT, CTA, glucose, sodium, oxygenation, breathing, and perfusion findings are fixed
snapshots. They do not permanently exclude ischemic, postictal, delirium, medication, toxic,
metabolic, infectious, immune, structural, psychiatric, or other causes.

## Authored values and hazards

The 72-year-old patient, 95-minute duration, intermittent speech arrest and right gaze deviation,
vital signs, laboratory values, imaging reports, 60-minute EEG interval, 24-minute seizure burden,
and later persistent fluctuation are authored for teaching. They are not a prevalence estimate,
universal threshold, patient-specific recommendation, or validated physiologic trajectory.

The principal hazards are premature clinical-only diagnosis, false reassurance from absent
convulsions, delay while alternatives are considered, raw-EEG overconfidence, permanent exclusion
from one negative snapshot, and inference that report review proves treatment, seizure control,
recovery, prognosis, or outcome. Strict action order, elapsed reassessment gates, and explicit false
state fields constrain those inferences.
