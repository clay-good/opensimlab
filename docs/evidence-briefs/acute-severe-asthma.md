# Acute severe asthma deterioration evidence brief

## What this slice teaches

One fixed adult acute-care vignette begins after documented initial asthma therapy has already been
delivered. The learner reconciles prior care against a worsening trajectory, recognizes evolving
respiratory failure, activates critical-care and airway-capable support before
finishing alternative-cause review, and hands off active unresolved care. This starts where the
Emergency Medicine adult-asthma first-response lesson ends.

## Current source floor

The 2026 Global Initiative for Asthma strategy report requires response review after initial
bronchodilator treatment and at 1 hour or earlier. Drowsiness, confusion, or a quiet chest calls for
immediate transfer to intensive care and urgent respiratory support. Blood gas assessment is
appropriate for severe airflow limitation, failure to respond, or deterioration; fatigue and
somnolence with increasing PaCO₂ signal respiratory failure and may require airway intervention by
experienced teams. Chest imaging is not routine, but can be appropriate when another diagnosis or
complication is suspected or the patient is not responding.

## Authored vignette and sequence

- On arrival, the patient had words-only speech, HR 126/min, RR 36/min, room-air SpO₂ 89%, and fixed
  PEF 28% predicted. One prior ICU admission increases concern but does not determine this episode.
- A verified experienced-team record reports controlled oxygen, 3 inhaled short-acting
  bronchodilator plus antimuscarinic cycles, systemic corticosteroid, and IV magnesium after poor
  response. The learner neither selected nor delivered them.
- At 75 minutes, the patient is drowsy and confused, cannot speak, has a quiet chest with weakening
  effort, HR 132/min, RR 18/min, BP 102/64 mmHg, SpO₂ 93% in an authored nominal 35% oxygen condition, and cannot perform
  PEF. The respiratory-rate decrease is fatigue in this fixed trajectory, not improvement.
- The fixed blood gas changes from pH 7.45/PaCO₂ 31/PaO₂ 61 mmHg at minute 10 to
  pH 7.24/PaCO₂ 58/PaO₂ 68 mmHg at minute 75. These values support the authored respiratory-failure
  pattern only when joined to the whole trajectory; they are not universal isolated thresholds.
- Recognition and critical-care escalation precede alternative-cause and ventilation-risk review.
  A strictly later handoff preserves active respiratory failure and named ownership. No response,
  disposition, prognosis, or outcome is reported.
- The live capnogram uses a generic severe lower-airway-obstruction cue only; it is not a
  patient-specific quantitative capnography model or substitute for examination.

## Deliberate exclusions

This is not an examination, PEF, blood-gas, imaging, quantitative capnography, airway, ventilation,
or treatment trainer. It
does not repeat bronchodilator, antimuscarinic, oxygen, corticosteroid, magnesium, antibiotic,
epinephrine, or fluid; select a drug or dose; trial noninvasive ventilation or high-flow oxygen;
perform intubation; select sedation, neuromuscular blockade, ventilator settings, or a permissive-
hypercapnia target; perform a procedure; determine disposition or prognosis; or predict outcome.
