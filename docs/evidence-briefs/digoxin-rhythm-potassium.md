# Digoxin toxicity: read the rhythm and potassium together

## Scope and distinctness

This lab rehearses a declared acute digoxin exposure with GI and visual symptoms, complete AV block,
shock, ventricular ectopy, and hyperkalemia. It is distinct from intrinsic conduction disease,
calcium-channel-blocker toxicity, isolated hyperkalemia, chronic therapeutic monitoring, and a
generic bradycardia or pacing algorithm.

## Fixed fixture

- A 67-year-old woman arrives 7 hours after a witnessed immediate-release digoxin-only ingestion.
  She has repeated vomiting, yellow-tinted blurred vision, and drowsiness. HR is 36/min,
  BP 76/42 mmHg, RR 18/min, room-air SpO2 98%, and T 36.4°C.
- A qualified ECG report supplies complete AV block, atrial rate 84/min, ventricular escape 36/min,
  QRS 108 ms, and ventricular ectopy without sustained ventricular tachycardia. A qualified
  pre-antidote sample drawn 7 hours after the last dose supplies digoxin 8.6 ng/mL, potassium
  6.1 mmol/L, magnesium 1.9 mg/dL, pH 7.32, bicarbonate 19 mmol/L, lactate 3.4 mmol/L, glucose
  103 mg/dL, and creatinine 1.1 mg/dL. Reported initial supportive care has not resolved shock.
- A strict fixed 60-minute report supplies clearer mentation, sinus rhythm 62/min, BP 100/62 mmHg,
  potassium 4.7 mmol/L, and lactate 2.1 mmol/L without sustained ventricular arrhythmia, hypoxemia,
  or pulmonary edema. A post-Fab total digoxin level is deliberately absent.

## Evidence application

The AHA recommends digoxin-specific antibody fragments for life-threatening digoxin poisoning,
allows qualified rhythm rescue while Fab is obtained, and recommends against hemodialysis,
hemofiltration, hemoperfusion, or plasmapheresis for toxin removal. The lab therefore records only
qualified Fab, surveillance, and rescue intent.

Current digoxin labeling identifies GI, visual, CNS, bradyarrhythmic, heart-block, ventricular-
arrhythmia, and hyperkalemic manifestations. Current DigiFab labeling says at least 6 to 8 hours are
required for serum-tissue equilibration, warns that potassium can fall rapidly after Fab, and says
standard total digoxin measurements can become clinically misleading. The lab never makes one
level, rhythm, potassium value, or reported exposure independently diagnostic.

## Boundaries

- Exposure, ECG, sample timing, laboratory evidence, prior care, and response are authored.
- No Fab product, vial count, dose, rate, target, access, electrolyte, fluid, airway, pacing,
  dialysis, cardioversion, antiarrhythmic, or rescue control is exposed.
- The fixed response does not prove treatment effect, durable perfusion, potassium stability,
  coingestant exclusion, safe disposition, or outcome.
