# Pediatric respiratory distress

## Intended rehearsal

This introductory Pediatrics lab rehearses rapid whole-child recognition, immediate experienced
support, and repeated reassessment when one reassuring number conflicts with worsening appearance,
effort, air movement, or mentation. It is an undifferentiated severity-and-escalation lesson, not a
disease-specific treatment pathway.

## Evidence boundary

- The AHA/AAP pediatric systematic approach begins with appearance, work of breathing, and
  circulation, then supports airway, breathing, and circulation while evaluation continues. The lab
  therefore makes the child's observable trajectory more important than a single respiratory rate or
  saturation.
- WHO emergency triage guidance treats severe respiratory distress and reduced consciousness as
  emergency signs and supports pulse oximetry and oxygen for hypoxemia. Experienced-team support is
  represented as a fixed event; the learner does not choose or deliver a device, flow, fraction, or
  target.
- PALICC-2 supports close monitoring and repeated assessment of respiratory rate, heart rate, work
  of breathing, gas exchange, and mental status when respiratory support is failing. It is used here
  only for the reassessment principle, not to diagnose pediatric ARDS or select ventilatory support.
- The healthy-child respiratory model provides finite engine scaffolding for one 6-year-old weighing
  20 kg. Every sick-child value and transition in this lab is an authored snapshot, not a validated
  disease trajectory or a general pediatric prediction.

## Authored course

The fixed opening report describes an awake, anxious 6-year-old girl with short-phrase speech,
marked recession and grunting, HR 138/min, RR 46/min, BP 104/66 mmHg, and clean pulse-coherent
room-air SpO2 87%. After qualified help, oxygenation, and monitoring, an early report shows SpO2 94%
but persistent grunting and recession. A later report shows drowsiness, weak one-word response,
shallower irregular breathing, less visible recession because effort is failing, RR 28/min, and
SpO2 90% on unchanged authored support. The lower rate is fatigue, not recovery.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine; acquire or interpret oximetry, capnography, blood gas, imaging, or
another test; diagnose bronchiolitis, croup, asthma, infection, sepsis, anaphylaxis, aspiration,
foreign-body obstruction, or another cause; select or deliver oxygen, a device, flow, fraction,
target, drug, dose, fluid, ventilation, or treatment; perform suction, an airway maneuver,
bag-mask ventilation, intubation, or another procedure; calculate a severity score; determine
disposition or prognosis; or predict recovery or outcome.
