# Pediatric diabetic ketoacidosis evidence brief

## Decision boundary

This lab rehearses recognition, qualified escalation, parallel neurological and metabolic safety review, serial reassessment, and handoff for a fixed pediatric DKA presentation. It does not teach a fluid, insulin, electrolyte, glucose, access, pump, or complication-treatment recipe. Those choices remain patient-specific, locally protocolized qualified work.

The lab is deliberately distinct from the adult DKA pathway. It does not expose potassium-to-insulin sequencing, dextrose thresholds, biochemical-resolution criteria, or transition controls. Its pediatric value is whole-child interpretation and continued surveillance for cerebral injury, electrolyte or osmotic disturbance, circulatory change, and failure of biochemical response.

## Fixed teaching facts

- A 9-year-old girl, 30 kg, has thirst, frequent urination, tiredness, reported weight loss, vomiting, abdominal discomfort, deep breathing, reduced intake, and clinical dehydration.
- The supplied initial panel is glucose 468 mg/dL, beta-hydroxybutyrate 5.6 mmol/L, venous pH 7.14, bicarbonate 8 mmol/L, potassium 4.6 mmol/L, and sodium 132 mmol/L.
- An experienced team supplies the DKA classification from hyperglycemia, ketonemia, and metabolic acidosis. The learner does not acquire or interpret the samples, diagnose DKA, or calculate severity.
- Orientation, warm extremities, normal-volume pulses, refill 2 seconds, and preserved pressure support the authored no-current-shock boundary. No current headache, bradycardia, hypertension, hypoxemia, or focal sign is authored. These are snapshots, not permanent exclusions of cerebral injury or another complication.
- Experienced pediatric, diabetes, nursing, pharmacy, and laboratory teams are available and prepared before the learner acts. The ownership action activates their locally protocolized fluid, insulin, glucose, electrolyte, access, input and output, rhythm, biochemical, neurological, and escalation work. It does not reveal or operate any treatment setting.
- The fixed minute-60 report is modestly improved but unresolved: alert and oriented; HR 108/min, deep RR 24/min, BP 104/66 mmHg, room-air SpO2 99%; glucose 342 mg/dL, beta-hydroxybutyrate 4.5 mmol/L, venous pH 7.20, bicarbonate 11 mmol/L, potassium 4.1 mmol/L, and sodium 134 mmol/L.
- The later report does not prove causal treatment effect, biochemical resolution, cerebral-injury exclusion, durable recovery, discharge readiness, or disposition.

All patient values and their timing are fictional face-valid fixtures. They are not predicted kinetics and must not be copied into clinical care.

## Source synthesis

ISPAD defines pediatric DKA from the combined hyperglycemia, ketonemia or ketonuria, and metabolic-acidosis pattern and emphasizes emergency whole-child assessment, experienced-center management, and frequent vital, neurological, fluid-balance, glucose, electrolyte, gas, and ketone monitoring. Its cerebral-injury section supports watching for evolving headache, vomiting, heart-rate slowing, neurological change, focal signs, and oxygen desaturation while warning that hypertension alone is not sufficient. This lab uses those principles only to frame qualified surveillance; it offers no learner diagnostic or treatment control.

NICE NG18 corroborates urgent pediatric DKA assessment and specialist hospital management, a supplied glucose-ketone-pH-bicarbonate diagnostic record, and repeated neurological and biochemical monitoring. The guideline's patient-specific fluid, insulin, electrolyte, glucose, and escalation recommendations remain outside the simulator controls.

BSPED provides UK operational corroboration for repeated conscious-level, vital, biochemical, electrolyte, fluid-balance, and response observations. Its calculator and treatment settings are intentionally not reproduced.

The PECARN randomized trial found no significant difference in neurological outcomes among its tested fluid-rate and sodium-content strategies. The lab therefore makes no claim that one fluid strategy causes or prevents cerebral injury and does not expose a fluid-selection control.

## Explicit nonclaims

- No real patient is examined, weighed, monitored, tested, diagnosed, severity-classified, treated, or dispositioned.
- No dehydration percentage, corrected sodium, osmolality, anion gap, deficit, maintenance, dose, concentration, rate, threshold, or resolution formula is calculated.
- No fluid, insulin, dextrose, potassium, phosphate, bicarbonate, hyperosmolar agent, other drug, access, pump, device, airway intervention, imaging, procedure, or treatment is selected or delivered.
- Initial normal orientation and absent warning signs do not exclude later cerebral injury, hypoglycemia, electrolyte or osmotic disturbance, infection, thrombosis, hemorrhage, ingestion, or another cause.
- The fictional biochemical trend does not establish a universal expected rate of improvement or prove treatment effect.
- DKA resolution, diabetes phenotype, precipitating cause, cerebral-injury exclusion, recurrence, recovery, prognosis, and outcome remain open.
