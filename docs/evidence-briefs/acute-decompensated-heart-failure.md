# Acute decompensated heart failure

## Intended rehearsal

Read inpatient decongestion as a serial whole-patient trajectory; separate partial response from
resolved congestion; review kidney, electrolyte, hemodynamic, and precipitant context; and record
an individualized transition plan whose readiness, ownership, education, and follow-up remain
explicit.

## Evidence boundary

- The 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA guideline recommends assessing congestion and perfusion in hospitalized
  heart failure, promptly treating significant fluid overload with intravenous loop diuretics,
  titrating therapy to resolve congestion, and including a diuretic-adjustment plan at discharge.
- The 2021 ESC guideline identifies diuretics as the foundation for congestion in acute heart
  failure and advises careful evaluation for persistent congestion and optimization of oral
  treatment before discharge.
- Neither guideline makes one fictional weight, fluid balance, urine output, or creatinine change a
  universal target. This lab therefore presents a fixed partial response and keeps individual
  treatment selection, dosing, and readiness with the real clinical team.

## Authored vignette

A 74-year-old man with HFrEF is 24 hours into admission after missed home medications and a
high-sodium week. Reported IV loop-diuretic treatment has already occurred. Weight changed from
77.2 to 75.8 kg versus a documented clinic weight of 72.0 kg; recorded net balance is −1.6 L with
2.4 L urine output. Dyspnea improved, but fixed orthopnea, JVP elevation, bibasal crackles, and 2+
edema persist. He is warm, BP is 118/73 mmHg, SpO2 is 94% on room air, and fixed creatinine changed
from 1.1 to 1.3 mg/dL with sodium 137 mmol/L, potassium 3.7 mmol/L, and magnesium 1.9 mg/dL. The
authored snapshot remains congested and is not discharge-ready.

## Exclusions

The screen does not take a history or examine; verify administration; acquire or interpret weight,
fluid balance, urine output, laboratory tests, ECG, ultrasound, or imaging; calculate dry weight,
fluid targets, doses, or scores; diagnose a real patient; select, dose, prescribe, or deliver a
diuretic or guideline-directed therapy; determine a regimen, transfer, disposition, prognosis,
readmission, or outcome.
