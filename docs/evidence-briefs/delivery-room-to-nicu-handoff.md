# Delivery-room-to-NICU handoff

## Evidence boundary

The 2025 AHA/AAP neonatal-resuscitation guideline states that newborns requiring resuscitation
beyond initial steps need further observation and monitoring. Postresuscitation assessment includes
respiratory effort and oxygenation, heart rate, temperature, and glucose; prolonged ventilation or
advanced resuscitation warrants close monitored care. The newborn, family, and surrounding
perinatal team all remain part of the transition.

AHRQ's perinatal TeamSTEPPS materials define handoff as transfer of information together with
authority and responsibility, with an opportunity to ask, clarify, and confirm. Their I PASS the
BATON structure covers introduction, patient, assessment, situation, safety concerns, background,
actions, timing, ownership, and next steps. The structure is a cognitive aid rather than proof of
accuracy, listening, shared understanding, or competent team behavior.

## Authored case estimate

The fixed case is a 34-week-2-day, 2.1 kg newborn at 12 minutes after emergency cesarean birth for a
supplied prolonged fetal-heart-rate deceleration. Qualified records supply initial apnea and heart
rate 76/min, effective ventilation beginning at 50 seconds, heart-rate recovery, spontaneous
breathing by 4 minutes, and current CPAP-supported respiratory transition without compressions,
epinephrine, access, or fluid. A fixed receiver check-back and 10-minute NICU arrival report preserve
the respiratory, thermal, glucose, neurologic, infection, feeding, family, and pending-data risks.
They do not prove learner communication, transport, treatment effect, durable stability, diagnosis,
disposition, or outcome.
