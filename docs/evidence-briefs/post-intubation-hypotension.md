# Evidence brief: post-intubation hypotension

## Intended rehearsal

This intermediate adult ICU case rehearses validating severe post-intubation hypotension, checking
immediate dangerous alternatives, integrating a mixed vasodilated and preload-sensitive pattern,
recording bounded concurrent support, and proving the response without closing the shock work.

## Evidence boundary

- SCCM's 2023 RSI guideline found insufficient evidence that peri-intubation vasopressors or IV
  fluids are superior for preventing further hypotension or arrest. The lesson therefore avoids a
  universal either-or claim.
- The 2026 Surviving Sepsis Campaign supports immediate resuscitation, an initial MAP near 65 mmHg,
  norepinephrine first line, balanced crystalloids, frequent reassessment, and dynamic measures over
  static examination alone to guide additional fluid.
- The fixed panel makes immediate disconnection, major tube failure, severe bronchospasm, dynamic
  hyperinflation, overt hemorrhage, and anaphylaxis less likely but does not exclude obstruction,
  pump failure, occult bleeding, drug effect, vasodilation, or preload loss.
- A passive-leg-raise response is an authored proxy. It supports fluid responsiveness, not a
  diagnosis or permission for unbounded fluid.

## Explicit exclusions

The controls do not examine, acquire pressure, inspect equipment, perform ultrasound or passive leg
raise, administer fluid or vasopressor, choose access or dose, change sedation or ventilation,
diagnose shock, treat infection, predict outcome, or assess airway, procedural, or ICU competence.
