# Pediatric anaphylaxis after first-line care evidence brief

## Decision boundary

This lab begins 5 minutes after one supplied, qualified community first-line intramuscular
epinephrine dose. A child still has airway, breathing, and circulation compromise. The learner
reconciles the exposure and supplied care, recognizes the persistent pediatric pattern, activates
qualified repeat first-line and resuscitation ownership without delay, then reviews airway, asthma
overlap, open causes, circulation, and refractory contingencies before one fixed later
report, and hands off unresolved observation, allergy, recurrence, and caregiver risk.

The lab does not repeat the adult emergency lesson's fixed adult dose, oxygen, or 20 mL/kg fluid
actions. It does not enter the perioperative lesson's intravenous epinephrine, ventilation, or
crystalloid-delivery surface. Pediatric respiratory predominance, asthma overlap, absent skin signs,
caregiver context, a supplied first dose, repeat-care escalation, and post-event ownership define
this phase.

## Fixed teaching facts

- A 6-year-old boy is 20 kg and 115 cm. He has mild asthma but no reported prior anaphylaxis.
- A witnessed insect sting is reported at school at minute 0, but species, allergen, and causal
  trigger remain unconfirmed.
- Within minutes he develops persistent cough and diffuse wheeze, hoarse one-to-two-word speech,
  repeated vomiting, pallor, drowsiness, and poor perfusion. No hives or facial or tongue swelling is
  authored; skin findings are not required for the fixed airway, breathing, and circulation pattern.
- A qualified responder activated emergency help, kept him lying flat and not standing or walking,
  supplied oxygen, and documented one appropriate first-line intramuscular epinephrine dose at
  minute 5. No product, concentration, dose, device, preparation, injection technique, or delivery
  control is exposed.
- At minute 10, he opens his eyes to his caregiver's voice and has spontaneous breathing with HR
  148/min, RR 34/min, BP 78/42 mmHg (MAP 54), clean pulse-coherent SpO₂ 91% on unspecified supplied
  oxygen, temperature 36.7°C, pale cool skin, weak pulses, and capillary refill 4 seconds. There is
  no apnea or pulse loss.
- No fever, infectious prodrome, abrupt choking, focal unilateral air-entry loss, trauma, seizure,
  or known medicine or food exposure is authored. These are limited snapshots, not exclusions.
- In the fixed minute-18 qualified report, no further vomiting has occurred since minute 11. He is
  alert, speaks in full short sentences, and has a clearer voice with reduced but persistent cough
  and wheeze. HR is 122/min, RR 24/min, BP 96/60 mmHg (MAP 72), clean pulse-coherent SpO₂ 97% on
  continued unspecified supplied oxygen, temperature 36.7°C, warm normal-volume pulses, and refill
  2 seconds. No hives or swelling is reported.
- The later report does not prove which qualified treatment caused change, complete resolution,
  diagnostic or trigger confirmation, durable response, absence of biphasic recurrence, discharge
  readiness, referral completion, prognosis, or outcome.

All values, care times, negative findings, and the later trajectory are fictional face-valid
fixtures. They are not predicted pediatric physiology or treatment response and must not be copied
into clinical care.

## Source synthesis

The current Royal Children's Hospital Melbourne guideline defines severe anaphylaxis through
potentially life-threatening airway, breathing, or circulation compromise and notes that typical
skin findings or shock can be absent. It says respiratory symptoms predominate in children, directs
intramuscular adrenaline and appropriate positioning, says to treat possible anaphylaxis before
asthma medicines, and defines refractory anaphylaxis by persisting respiratory or cardiovascular
symptoms after 2 appropriate intramuscular doses. The lab uses these boundaries without exposing a
dose table, device, repeat-delivery control, or refractory recipe.

The current 2026 ASCIA acute-management web guideline supports prompt first-line intramuscular
adrenaline, child positioning that avoids standing or walking, repeated treatment at 5-minute
intervals when severe symptoms persist, monitoring and support, and specialist escalation for
refractory disease. This lab records qualified ownership only and does not reproduce its dosing,
fluid, device, or infusion instructions.

NICE NG258, published May 27, 2026, uses suspected-anaphylaxis terminology and supports
risk-stratified observation after symptom resolution plus age-appropriate specialist-allergy
referral. Because this child's fixed later report remains symptomatic, the lab does not start a
universal observation clock or make a discharge decision.

## Explicit nonclaims

- No child is examined, monitored, scored, diagnosed, treated, counseled, or dispositioned by the
  learner.
- No medicine, concentration, dose, route, device, preparation, injection, access, oxygen interface
  or flow, fluid type, volume or rate, infusion, vasopressor, bronchodilator, antihistamine, or
  corticosteroid is selected or delivered.
- No airway device or procedure, ventilation, CPR, laboratory test, tryptase, imaging, or allergy
  investigation is acquired or interpreted.
- Fixed negative snapshots do not exclude anaphylaxis, asthma overlap, infection, obstruction,
  aspiration, another trigger, or another dangerous cause.
- Partial improvement does not establish treatment effect, complete or durable resolution,
  diagnostic or trigger proof, or absence of recurrent or biphasic symptoms.
- The lab does not choose an observation duration, admission or discharge, prescribe or train an
  autoinjector, assess an action plan or caregiver communication, complete a referral, predict
  prognosis, or report an outcome.
